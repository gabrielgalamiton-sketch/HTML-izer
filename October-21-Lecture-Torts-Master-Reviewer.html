Who Pays for the Tort?

Persons liable for torts, joint tortfeasors, motor vehicle owners, parents, guardians, schools, administrators and teachers.

October 21 lecture · Parts 1–3 · Civil Code, Family Code, R.A. 6809, R.A. 9344 (as amended) and controlling jurisprudence.

00 · Orientation

The Big Picture

A tort may be committed by one person, but the law may require another person—or several persons—to pay. The exam question is not merely “Who did it?” but “Who bears legal responsibility, why, and under what conditions?”

1 · Direct liability

The actor's own fault or negligence caused an injury. Art. 2176.

2 · Shared liability

Two or more legally liable causes combine. Art. 2194: solidary external liability.

3 · Imputed liability

A legally responsible person is answerable for an actor under custody, employment or supervision. Art. 2180 / FC 218–219.

Lecture roadmap: the four questions

  1. WHO? Direct actor, corporation, government, co-tortfeasor or vehicle owner.
  2. WHY TOGETHER? Concerted act, concurrent causes and solidary payment/contribution.
  3. WHO SUPERVISED? Parent/guardian/employer at the time of the act, with age/custody limitations.
  4. WHICH SCHOOL DUTY? Student-caused harm, authorized activity, outsider harm, or contractual safety duty.
Reading convention

Lecture rule reproduces the professor's teaching framework. Verified clarification flags statements that are overbroad, misstated or contradicted by the actual statutory text or Supreme Court disposition. In a conflict, memorize both but cite the verified controlling rule in the exam.

Part I · Topic I

I. Tortfeasors: Natural, Artificial, State & LGU

General rule · Art. 2176

A tortfeasor commits an actionable act or omission involving fault or negligence, or is otherwise legally responsible for the resulting injury. Quasi-delict requires fault/negligence causing another's damage, without a pre-existing contractual obligation governing the negligent breach. An act may also be an intentional civil wrong under other applicable provisions.

Who can be answerable?

DefendantLegal basisWhen responsibility arises
Natural personArts. 2176 (and applicable intentional-wrong provisions)Own wrongful act or omission, actual injury and proximate causation.
Corporation / partnershipArts. 46, 2176, 2180Direct wrongful acts through authorized/ratified officers; or employee negligence within assigned tasks, subject to applicable defenses. Artificial persons act through people.
State1987 Constitution, Art. XVI §3; Civil Code Art. 2180Consent-to-sue rules are distinct from substantive liability. For Art. 2180, tortious damage by a special agent, not merely an official doing regular assigned functions.
Province/city/municipalityArt. 2189; LGC §24Defective public works under control or supervision; other lawful bases for proprietary acts. Sec. 24 rejects blanket exemption but does not dispense with proving a cause of action.

Direct corporate fault

Board authorizes an unsafe policy or ratifies conduct: the organization may be accountable for its own corporate act.

Vicarious corporate fault

Employee's task-related negligence may trigger employer liability from presumed culpa in eligendo/vigilando.

Government-specific distinctions

  • Sovereign immunity: “cannot be sued without consent” is a rule about the capacity to hale the State into court—not a declaration that it can never cause a legal wrong.
  • Special agent (Art. 2180): one tasked with a definite, fixed commission foreign to regular official functions. For an official acting within ordinary duties, personal Art. 2176 liability may arise; State liability must have an independent legal basis.
  • Proprietary vs. governmental: distinguish private/corporate operations from sovereign functions when evaluating State or LGU responsibility.
  • Art. 2189: covers provinces, cities and municipalities and defective roads, streets, bridges, public buildings and other public works under their control or supervision, even without ownership.
Example

A city-controlled bridge collapses due to an unrepaired dangerous defect. The bridge is owned by another government agency. Art. 2189 can still be evaluated against the city if it actually controlled or supervised the work and the defect proximately caused the injury.

Recall strip: Who did it? Natural / corporate officer / employee / special agent / LGU works. State immunity concerns suit; Art. 2189 concerns control.
Quick test: Must an LGU own the defective road to incur Art. 2189 liability?
No. The statutory words are “under their control or supervision.” Ownership is not indispensable.

Part I · Topic II

II. Joint Tortfeasors & Solidary Liability

General rule · Art. 2194

The responsibility of two or more persons liable for a quasi-delict is solidary. The injured person may recover the whole legally recoverable amount from any one liable defendant, subject to no double recovery. Art. 1216 permits proceeding against one, some or all; Art. 1217 governs payment and reimbursement.

Three routes to a single injury

1 · CO-OP

Concerted cooperation: two actors deliberately join in the harmful act.

2 · LINK

Single causal set: multiple linked contributions form one causal sequence (e.g., driving + negligent supervision).

3 · CONVERGE

Independent concurrent sets: independent negligent acts combine proximately to produce one indivisible injury.

Study aid “C–L–C”: Cooperate, Link, Converge. This summarizes the lecture's causal grouping, not a statutory verbatim enumeration. No prior conspiracy is required.

RelationTo whom?Governing ruleResult
ExternalVictim vs. co-tortfeasorsArts. 2194, 1216Any one solidary debtor may owe 100% to victim.
InternalPaying tortfeasor vs. othersArt. 1217Claim co-debtor's proper share plus applicable interest; insolvency share is spread as the article provides.
Professor's ₱1,000,000 illustration · 2 actors

A and B independently cause C one indivisible ₱1M loss. C may recover ₱1M from A alone. After A fully pays, C's claim is extinguished. If the internal allocation is 50:50, A may claim B's ₱500,000 share.

Victim collects: ₱1,000,000 (A) + ₱0 (B) = ₱1,000,000, not ₱2,000,000.
A's internal burden after valid reimbursement: ₱1,000,000 − ₱500,000 = ₱500,000.

A 50:50 division is an illustration, not an automatic finding of equal fault in every case. An established different internal allocation controls. Article 1217 also regulates the interest and co-debtor insolvency consequences.

Exam trap

“Joint tortfeasor” does not mean merely joint (pro rata) liability to the victim. Under Art. 2194, their liability is solidary, though reimbursement is internal and proportionate.

Recall strip: 2194 → victim: full · 1216 → choose defendants · 1217 → payment extinguishes + reimbursement.
Quick test: Does the victim have to sue both A and B to collect full damages?
No. Subject to procedural and evidentiary requirements, Art. 1216 permits proceeding against any one or more solidary debtors until the debt is fully collected.

Part I · Topic III

III. Motor Vehicle Mishaps · Article 2184

Statutory fork

Owner INSIDE: solidarily liable with a negligent driver if the owner could have prevented the mishap by exercising due diligence. Owner OUTSIDE: Art. 2184 expressly directs analysis to Art. 2180; also examine the registered-owner doctrine when applicable.

Inside · “Can I stop this?”

ALL MUST CONCUR: mishap and injury; negligent driver proximately causes injury; owner is aboard; reasonable preventive opportunity was available but neglected.

Outside · “What is my role?”

No Art. 2184 presence liability. If driver is employee acting under covered circumstances, test Art. 2180. Registered-owner rule is a distinct possible basis, including in certain lease/borrower settings.

Two drivers, one important period

Disputable presumption Under Art. 2184, driver negligence is disputably presumed when the driver was found guilty of reckless driving or traffic violations at least twice within the immediately preceding two months. The presumption is rebuttable; it is not automatic fault from a single citation.

ScenarioOwner's awarenessAnalysis
Owner reads; driver suddenly swervesNo prior warning or real time to interveneNo automatic owner fault under Art. 2184 merely because the owner was on board.
Owner sees prolonged overspeedingTime to tell driver to slow/stop but remains passiveSolidary liability under Art. 2184 may attach if preventive diligence was possible.
Driver alone, on employer errandOwner absentAnalyze Art. 2180; evidence of selection, supervision and scope of assigned task is pertinent.
Driver alone in rented/borrowed vehicleOwner absent; employment may be absentDo not automatically assume an employment relationship or that diligence defeats the registered-owner doctrine.
Recall anchor · 2–2–P

2 convictions / 2 months / Presence determines which branch of Art. 2184 to test. “Present → preventable?”

Recall strip: Owner inside + ability to prevent = Art. 2184 solidarity; outside = Art. 2180 + independent registered-owner analysis. 2 in 2 = rebuttable driver presumption.
Quick test: May an owner be liable despite not witnessing the accident?
Yes, possibly under Article 2180 or the registered-owner rule. But not through the owner-present clause of Art. 2184 if absent.

Part I · Topic IV

IV. Why Vicarious Liability Exists

Vicarious liability means a person may be answerable even without personally performing the tortious physical act. Under Art. 2180, Philippine employer/parent liability is traditionally explained through one's own presumed failure of care.

Culpa in eligendo

Negligence in selecting a worker: Did the employer hire or entrust responsibility to a competent person?

Culpa in vigilando

Negligence in supervising a child, ward, student or employee: Did the responsible person exercise care to prevent the injury?

BasisMaster answers for servant's job-related tortPresumed negligence in selection/supervision (paterfamilias)
DefenseCareful hiring is not itself a defense to strict derivative responsibilityArt. 2180 last paragraph: show all diligence of a good father of a family to prevent damage
NatureDerivative employer liabilityDirect/primary civil responsibility of responsible person, as described in Philippine cases
Different sourceNot a rule that replaces special statutesRPC Art. 103 subsidiary ex delicto liability operates under its own elements, not Art. 2180's diligence defense
Mnemonic · E + V

E = Eligendo, choose carefully. V = Vigilando, watch carefully. For parents and teachers, the focus is especially V.

Recall strip: Philippine rule is traditionally presumed personal negligence, rebuttable through proper diligence, unless an independent rule controls.

Part II · Topic V

V. Parents: Primary, Direct & Solidary

Picture a child breaking a neighbor's window. The parent does not become liable only when the child's savings are exhausted. Because the law presumes a failure of parental supervision, a covered parent is sued directly and may be liable with the child.

General rule · Civil Code 2180 + Family Code 211, 221

Parents and other persons exercising parental authority may be held civilly liable for injuries and damages caused by the acts or omissions of children living in their company and under their parental authority, subject to defenses provided by law. Under Libi, the parental obligation under the quasi-delict framework is primary and direct—not subsidiary; Art. 2194 supplies solidarity where appropriate.

Statutory evolution: the “father first” rule is superseded

Original Civil Code, Art. 2180Family Code, Arts. 211 & 221
Text listed father first, mother if father dead/incapacitated.Joint parental authority of father and mother over common children; liability attaches to those exercising authority, subject to custody and defenses.
Primary liability from presumed parental negligence, despite old preferential wording.Modern analysis does not mechanically impose a father-first preference; both parents may be principally held responsible.

Four checkpoints for a parental tort claim

ALL MATERIAL CONDITIONS MUST BE ESTABLISHEDAge 18–20 special rule below
  1. Covered age: under 18 in the ordinary minor-child rule; Art. 2180 responsibility for children below 21 is expressly preserved by R.A. 6809 notwithstanding emancipation at 18.
  2. Parental authority / custody: relevant parent or guardian exercised legally cognizable responsibility, and the child lived in their company under applicable rules.
  3. Harmful act + causation: the child's intentional or negligent act or omission caused legally compensable damage.
  4. Diligence not established: after factual predicates are proven, the parent has the statutory opportunity to rebut presumed negligence with actual care to prevent damage.
Two different uses of “subsidiary”

Parents under general Art. 2180: primarily liable (Libi). Parents under Family Code Art. 219 during school special parental authority: expressly subsidiarily liable, provided that Art. 219 is applicable. Never transfer one label blindly to the other setting.

Intentional harm is covered. A child's attack can give rise to parental liability; limiting the rule to accidental negligence would produce the illogical result condemned in Libi—less parental accountability for a deliberate harm than for an accidental one.

Recall strip: Both parents (joint authority) · primary, direct in general Art. 2180 claims · custody · actual preventive diligence.
Quick test: Is proof that a minor has no money required before suing the parents under Art. 2180?
No. The parent's liability is primary and direct, not merely subsidiary. Compare the different statutory ordering in Family Code Art. 219 for school-related torts by unemancipated minors.

Part II · Topic VI

VI. Age of Majority, Below-21 Exception & Juvenile Delicts

General rule + statutory exception

R.A. 6809 §1 / amended FC Art. 234: majority begins at 18. R.A. 6809 §3 / amended FC Art. 236: expressly retains duties of parents and guardians for children/wards below 21 mentioned in Civil Code Art. 2180, paragraphs 2 and 3. Thus 18, 19 and 20 may still fall under the preserved tort-liability rule if the applicable family/custody facts are met.

Memory anchor · “18 owns, 21 protects”

At 18, civil majority generally begins. Until the 21st birthday, Art. 2180's specially preserved parental/guardian tort responsibility can remain relevant. Do not call an 18–20-year-old legally unemancipated: the liability is a statutory carve-out, not continuation of general minority.

Professor's 20-year-old at home

Child C, 20, is studying and living with parents A and B. C negligently injures a neighbor. A/B cannot succeed merely by saying “C is already legally an adult.” The below-21 exception requires evaluation of their Art. 2180 responsibility and diligence.

Criminal accountability ≠ civil compensation

R.A. 9344 §6, as amended by R.A. 10630, separates criminal age from civil liability: an exemption from criminal liability does not erase civil liability, to be enforced under existing laws. Distinguish civil liability ex delicto from an independent quasi-delict claim; do not describe every civil claim arising from a child's act as the same cause of action.

Age when act committedCriminal status under R.A. 9344 §6Civil-law inquiry

Above 15 but below 18; no discernmentExempt; intervention.Civil responsibility remains; assess adult authority/custody, Art. 101 as applicable, and any available independent civil ground.
Above 15 but below 18; with discernmentNot exempt; juvenile proceedings, diversion/suspended-sentence rules where applicable.Criminal civil consequences are distinct from parental Art. 2180 responsibility; Libi discusses parents' direct responsibility for minors' intentional delicts.
18–below 21Ordinary adult criminal liability.General parental ex delicto responsibility for minors does not simply extend because the child is under 21; preserved Art. 2180 quasi-delict responsibility is a separate question.
Statutory precision / recent judicial discussion

The **below-21 clause** is in amended Family Code Art. 236, introduced by §3 of R.A. 6809. It is not an independent one-sentence §3 standing by itself. In CICL XXX v. People (G.R. No. 238798, Mar. 14, 2023), a separate concurring opinion questioned the tension between this exception and full emancipation at 18. That separate opinion is not a majority repeal of the express statute. Keep the current statutory text distinct from the suggested reconsideration.

Recall strip: Age ladder: ≤15 exempt · >15 to <18 discernment decides · 18 majority · <21 exception for Art. 2180 · criminal exemption ≠ civil exemption.
Quick test: The accused is 16 and acted without discernment. Is compensation automatically extinguished?
No. Criminal exemption under R.A. 9344 is not civil exemption; appropriate civil liability is governed by existing laws.

Part II · Topic VII

VII. Guardianship, Adoption & The Custody Clock

Hierarchy of substitute parental authority · Family Code Art. 216

PRIORITY ORDER
  1. Surviving grandparent, with the applicable court determination under Art. 214 if multiple grandparents remain.
  2. Oldest brother or sister over 21, unless unfit/disqualified.
  3. Actual custodian over 21, unless unfit/disqualified.

This hierarchy operates in default of parents or a judicially appointed guardian. Under Family Code Art. 217, proper institutions can receive parental authority through the specified proceedings for abandoned and similarly situated children. Art. 222 separately deals with guardians of a child's property or ad litem.

Parents / natural guardians

Ordinarily exercise parental authority by law; do not require a fresh court order to act as personal guardians of their own unemancipated child. FC Art. 225 addresses court supervision/bond in property guardianship above the statutory threshold, not a general new appointment for the person.

Adult incapacitated ward

Art. 2180 covers incapacitated persons under a guardian's authority and living in the guardian's company. Prove legal authority, incapacity, residence/custody and causal damage.

Tamargo: the crucial date is the tort, not the decree

Custody clock

When an adoption petition is pending, evaluate who actually had the child under their care and supervision WHEN the injury was inflicted. A later adoption decree's retroactivity must not fictionally impose past supervisory negligence on adopters who had no custody, nor erase natural parents' responsibility during actual custody.

Facts visual · Tamargo

Dec. 10, 1981: petition to adopt Adelberto filed → Oct. 20, 1982: Adelberto, 10, shoots Jennifer with an air rifle while living with natural parents → Nov. 18, 1982: adoption decree granted. Result: natural parents remain the proper parents to sue on the supervision theory; adoption's later decree cannot transfer the earlier fault.

Memory hook

“Custody at the trigger pull.” The child held the air rifle in the natural parents' home period; the adoption decree came afterward. Later legal paperwork cannot rewrite past actual supervision.

Hypothetical caution

If prospective adoptive parents actually had authorized care/supervision during trial custody, evaluate the precise legal authority in force then and the actual facts. Do not state that any prospective adopter is automatically liable just from temporary possession, or that all natural parents are “solely liable” regardless of independent negligence of others.

Recall strip: Grandparent → adult sibling → adult custodian. Tamargo = tort-time custody controls.
Quick test: Adoption filed before injury, granted afterward. Natural parents had actual custody during injury. Who is the Art. 2180 focus?
The natural parents who exercised the relevant authority and physical custody when the tort occurred, subject to due diligence defenses (Tamargo).

Part II · Topic VIII

VIII. The Parent's Defense: Prevention, Not Provision

Exception / defense · Art. 2180, final paragraph

Responsibility under Art. 2180 ceases when the relevant person proves that they observed all the diligence of a good father of a family to prevent damage. The defendant's evidence must demonstrate prevention of foreseeable harm, not merely financial support or good character.

Insufficient by itself

“I paid tuition, bought clothes, gave food and allowance.” This proves support, not effective supervision of risky behavior.

What helps show care

Reasonable discipline, conduct monitoring, appropriate instruction, response to warning signs, securing dangerous items and practical preventive measures suited to circumstances.

Firearm injuries: concurrent negligence

  1. Child's parent: may be responsible through failed supervision and accessible firearms.
  2. Third-party owner: an aunt or police officer is not automatically a “parent,” but can be a direct Art. 2176 tortfeasor for negligent storage if proven.
  3. Combined injury: if negligent storage and supervision both proximately cause a single indivisible injury, Art. 2194 may yield solidary responsibility among legally liable defendants.
Classroom firearm hypothetical · ALAC

Answer: Parents and the firearm-owning aunt may be solidarily liable. Legal basis: Arts. 2176, 2180, 2194; causal connection and negligence required. Application: the minor's parents failed to supervise despite warning signs; the aunt left a loaded service firearm openly accessible; both defaults enabled the shooting. Conclusion: solidarity is justified if each person's negligence is found to have proximately caused the one injury; neither family status nor gun ownership alone establishes every element.

Libi: the revolver key

Wendell Libi, between 18 and 19 at the time under the former age regime, fatally shot Julie Ann Gotiong and himself with his father's revolver. His mother kept one key to the gun's safety-deposit box in her handbag; the parents had been unaware of missing gun access and the son's troubled activities. The Supreme Court found parental diligence lacking and clarified primary, not subsidiary civil liability. Two deaths were evidenced by separate single gunshot wounds, not “both killed by one bullet.”

Memory hook

“Locked box, loose key = negligent oversight.” A nominal lock does not prove careful supervision when its key is accessible.

Recall strip: Proof of support ≠ proof of prevention. Libi = revolver key + missing parental supervision + primary liability.
Quick test: Parents show receipts for food and tuition. Have they proved due diligence under Art. 2180?
Not merely from those receipts. They must establish legally sufficient diligence to prevent the harm under the facts.

Part III · Topic IX

IX. Schools, Administrators & Teachers: Which Statute?

The most important distinction: Was the injury caused by a student's act, by a teacher's own negligence, by equipment, or by an outsider? Merely being on school property is not a sufficient answer to who pays.

Civil Code · Art. 2180, teacher/head clause

Teachers or heads of establishments of arts and trades are responsible for damages caused by pupils, students or apprentices while in their custody. Amadora explains the division: academic school → teacher-in-charge; arts/trades establishment → head. This is a specific teacher/head vicarious-liability provision, distinct from a school's own negligence.

Family Code · Arts. 218–219

For a minor child under their supervision, instruction or custody, the school, administrators and teachers or persons/institutions engaged in child care exercise special parental authority in all authorized activities, inside or outside. They are principally and solidarily liable for damage caused by the acts/omissions of the unemancipated minor; parents, judicial guardians or substitute parents are subsidiarily liable, subject to proof of required diligence.

Child-care institutions: look at function, not brand

Daycare centers, employer-sponsored childcare and supervised commercial play areas may qualify as “individual, entity or institution engaged in child care” under FC Art. 218 when they actually undertake custody/supervision of the minor. A commercial mall playground is not automatically liable for every injury: identify whether the child caused another's injury (special parental authority) or whether the facility's own unsafe premises/equipment/staff caused the harm (direct or contractual grounds).

Very common trap · “caused by” vs. “suffered by”

FC Art. 219 expressly addresses damages caused by the minor's acts/omissions. If a child instead suffers a chemical burn from a teacher's unsafe experiment, analyze the teacher's and school's own negligent conduct under Arts. 2176/2180 (employer) and/or contractual duty as factually applicable. Do not make FC Art. 219 automatic liability for every injury to a child.

Recall strip: 2180 = teacher/head, offender in custody. FC 218–219 = minor actor + authorized custody → school/teacher principal, parents subsidiary. Other direct wrongs require their own theory.
Quick test: A 20-year-old college student assaults a classmate during class. Does Amadora automatically exclude teacher liability because he is over 18?
No. Amadora states that the teacher-in-charge rule can operate regardless of the student's age. Evaluate actual custody, supervision, diligence, causation and other facts; FC Art. 218's minor-child special authority is a separate statutory scheme.

Part III · Topic X

X. Custody, Authorization, Waivers & Four Classroom Problems

The A–C–C test for school activities

A · Authorized?

Did the school or properly empowered authority actually sanction the function?

C · Custody?

Was the child within the relevant supervision/instruction/care when the event occurred?

C · Caused by whom?

Minor student, teacher, outsider, equipment or another independent cause?

Study aid A–C–C is an analysis checklist, not a codal three-element statutory test. A negative A does not immunize a personally negligent teacher; a positive A does not erase the need for actual negligence and causation where required.

Activity hypotheticals · model ALAC applications

1 · Chemical science experiment

A: Teacher and school may be liable. L: Arts. 2176 and 2180; duty to supervise and school safety duties. A: Unsafe sulfur experiment in an ordinary classroom, no goggles, child peers into test tube and is blinded; teacher's deficient precautions and school policy/equipment require proof. C: When negligence and proximate cause are established, liability can attach; avoid saying FC 219 automatically applies merely because a student was hurt.

2 · Parish catechist visiting class

A: The school cannot merely say “the catechist is not our employee” and assume it has no remaining duty. L: FC 218–219 for injury caused by a supervised minor; Arts. 2176/2180 and contractual principles for the catechist's or school's own negligence. A: If student attacks a student, analyze ongoing school supervision; if catechist himself hits a student, analyze his own wrongdoing and any proven negligent entrustment or school failure. C: Liability is fact-dependent, not automatically joint for every scenario.

3 · Beach varsity training in semestral break

A: Authorized varsity training may bring minor players within FC 218 even outside campus. L: FC 218's express off-premises authorized-activity rule; direct negligence principles if injury results from poor planning/supervision. A: Coach conducts school-approved training at Agoo beach; player drowns. Identify whether negligence in training safety proximately caused death. C: Approval defeats a bare “off-campus / vacation” defense but does not automatically establish damages liability.

4 · Private, unauthorized beach picnic

A: School special-authority liability is weaker without authorization. L: St. Francis; Arts. 2176 and 2180 (independent personal fault and employment scope). A: Teachers organize a purely private trip and a child drowns; identify their personal negligent act if any, separate from mere participation. C: Do not assume teachers are liable just for attending; St. Francis majority found even the teachers had exercised due care.

Consent form ≠ universal release

A parental permission slip may evidence consent to attend, but it does not conclusively waive legally actionable negligence. Art. 1171 explicitly nullifies a waiver of an action for future fraud; an attempted waiver of future negligence requires its own analysis under Arts. 6, 1172 and public policy. Do not claim Art. 1171 literally declares all future-negligence waivers void.

Recall strip: A–C–C: Authorized? Custody? Caused by whom? School approval ≠ automatic liability; outside school ≠ automatic immunity; consent ≠ categorical waiver of negligence.

Quick test: The principal merely heard about a private outing. Does knowledge alone equal formal school authorization?
No, not automatically. St. Francis majority held that the principal's mere knowledge of the private picnic did not amount to school sanction.

Part III · Topic XI

XI. Seven Assigned Cases: Facts → Issue → Holding → Hook

Read each digest, then close it and recall the hook. Each holding is tied to what the Supreme Court actually disposed of, not what a hypothetical would suggest. For court-ready citations, memorize the complete G.R. number and date.

1. Tamargo v. Court of Appeals · Adoption / custody

G.R. No. 85044 · June 3, 1992 · Feliciano, J.

Material facts: Ten-year-old Adelberto Bundoc fatally shot Jennifer Tamargo with an air rifle on Oct. 20, 1982. He lived with natural parents Victor and Clara Bundoc. The Rapisuras had applied to adopt Adelberto in Dec. 1981; adoption was granted Nov. 18, 1982, after the shooting. Natural parents relied on adoption's possible retroactive effect to deny that they were proper defendants.

Issue: Can adoption's retroactivity shift Art. 2180 parental responsibility away from natural parents who actually had custody when the injury occurred?

Ruling: No. Liability rests on actual legal and physical supervision at the time of the wrong. The later adoption decree cannot retrospectively put actual supervision onto adopters who lacked it or absolve the natural parents. The Court also resolved procedural appeal difficulties to permit resolution on the merits.

Doctrine: Culpa in vigilando attaches to the parental custody/control existing when the quasi-delict happened.

🧠 Air rifle BEFORE adoption decree = biological parents' custody then.

2. Libi v. Intermediate Appellate Court · Parents / firearm

G.R. No. 70890 · September 18, 1992 · Regalado, J. (En Banc)

Material facts: Wendell Libi, then roughly 18–19 under the age framework applicable to his 1979 acts, was rejected by Julie Ann Gotiong. Evidence supported that he used father Cresencio Libi's revolver to kill Julie Ann and himself. One key to the gun's locked box was carried in mother Amelia's handbag, and the parents were inadequately aware of his activities and access to the weapon.

Issue: Are parents liable for an intentional wrong of their child, and is their liability primary or subsidiary?

Ruling: Primary and direct, not subsidiary. The Supreme Court sustained liability and held that the parents failed to establish diligent supervision and safeguards. It addressed civil responsibility arising both from quasi-delict and minors' criminal acts, while distinguishing RPC Art. 101.

Doctrine: Intentional wrongful acts are within the relevant parental-liability framework; diligence must be shown through meaningful prevention.

🧠 Smith & Wesson in locked box; the loose key gives access → supervision failure.

3. Amadora v. Court of Appeals · Teacher-in-charge / legitimate presence

G.R. No. L-47745 · April 15, 1988 · Cruz, J. (En Banc)

Material facts: On Apr. 13, 1972, 17-year-old Alfredo Amadora went to Colegio de San Jose-Recoletos for a legitimate graduation-related physics purpose and was shot by classmate Pablito Daffon in the auditorium. Suit was brought against school personnel including the physics teacher. An earlier confiscated gun had been returned to a different student, but its identity as murder weapon was not established.

Issue: Does Art. 2180 cover academic institutions and students still on campus after formal classes; who is liable?

Ruling: Yes, the teacher/head clause covers academic and technical schools, and Alfredo was in school custody for a legitimate purpose even though the term ended. But the named defendants were not held liable: the responsible teacher-in-charge at the shooting was not shown to have neglected actual custody; the rector/principal/dean were not teacher-in-charge; the school was not directly answerable on these facts.

Doctrine: Academic → teacher-in-charge; vocational arts/trades → head. The Court stated that the teacher's custody duty may exist even for students over the age of majority.

🧠 Physics report + auditorium shooting = custody YES, particular defendants' liability NO.

Full Supreme Court text

4. St. Francis High School v. Court of Appeals · Private picnic

G.R. No. 82465 · February 25, 1991 · Paras, J.

Material facts: Ferdinand Castillo, 13, drowned at a Talaan Beach outing in Sariaya, Quezon arranged privately by teachers and students, not officially sanctioned by St. Francis High School. The principal knew of planning but had not approved the picnic. Teachers with first-aid/swimming knowledge assisted in rescue; evidence included life-saving equipment.

Issue: Does an unofficial picnic make the school and personally participating teachers liable for the drowning?

Controlling majority ruling: No liability was upheld against the school, principal or petitioning teachers. The picnic was private, not within the teachers' assigned school tasks; the majority also found teachers exercised due care, and set aside the damages judgment. Padilla, J., dissenting would have found the participating teachers negligent and the school responsible.

Doctrine: Mere principal awareness ≠ official sanction. Personal teacher negligence must still be proved; attendance and a child's drowning alone do not establish liability.

🧠 Unapproved picnic + lifesavers = majority absolves BOTH school and teachers; dissent disagrees.

Full Supreme Court text (majority and dissent)

5. St. Mary's Academy v. Carpitanos · Proximate cause / broken steering

G.R. No. 143363 · February 6, 2002 · Pardo, J.

Material facts: Students were on an authorized school enrollment drive. Sherwin Carpitanos died in a jeep accident. Minor James Daniel II drove a jeep owned by Villanueva after being allowed to do so by the latter's grandson. Evidence established that the steering wheel guide detached, causing it to overturn; the school had not been shown to have authorized the minor to drive.

Issue: Does authorization of the school activity alone make the school principally and solidarily liable under FC Arts. 218–219?

Ruling: No. The school's negligence or the minor's actionable negligence had not been established as the proximate cause. The sudden mechanical defect intervened. The Supreme Court reversed the school judgment and remanded for assessment of other defendants (excluding the school), identifying registered-owner responsibility.

Doctrine: Even for authorized school activities, causal link remains indispensable. Registered-vehicle ownership may independently ground responsibility to road users.

🧠 Authorized enrollment drive—but detached steering guide breaks school causation.

Full Supreme Court text

6. Philippine School of Business Administration v. CA · Outsiders / enrollment contract

G.R. No. 84698 · February 4, 1992 · Padilla, J.

Material facts: Carlitos Bautista, a PSBA commerce student, was stabbed to death on the second floor by outsiders not belonging to the school community. His parents sued PSBA and officers for allegedly inadequate security; the school sought early dismissal of the complaint.

Issue: Is school liability impossible because assailants were not students covered by Art. 2180?

Ruling: No early dismissal. The Court held Art. 2180's pupil/student-tort basis was inapplicable to outsider assailants, but the enrollment contract included an implied obligation to take adequate steps to maintain peace and a conducive learning environment. No final factual finding of school negligence or damages liability was made; trial was ordered to proceed.

Doctrine: The ordinary theory is culpa contractual; an independently wrongful tort (notably conduct implicating Art. 21) could also be actionable. A school is not an insurer against every determined intruder.

🧠 Outsider's knife: Art. 2180 NO; enrollment safety contract MAY; trial still needed.

Full Supreme Court text

7. Valenzuela v. Court of Appeals · Company car / presumed employer fault

G.R. Nos. 115024 & 117944 · February 7, 1996 · Kapunan, J.

Material facts: After a flat tire, Ma. Lourdes Valenzuela stopped by Aurora Boulevard; speeding driver Richard Li struck her while driving a company-issued car registered to Alexander Commercial, Inc. She suffered a traumatic amputation of the left leg (the lecture notes said “legs”). Li was using a car granted for broad business/personal purposes as a managerial benefit.

Issue: Is the employer liable despite the fact the immediate trip was private and outside ordinary office duties?

Ruling: Yes, on these facts. The Court reinstated the trial judgment. By granting nearly unlimited use of a company vehicle for purposes beneficial to business image and client relations, the employer assumed a responsibility to select/supervise responsible users; it failed to demonstrate sufficient paterfamilias diligence. Li was negligent; Valenzuela's emergency stop was not contributory negligence.

Doctrine: Art. 2180 employer liability is grounded in culpa in eligendo/vigilando, not purely strict respondeat superior. Company-car benefits require fact-specific supervision analysis.

🧠 Flat tire victim + unrestricted company car = employer's own supervision failure.

Full Supreme Court text

Seven-case recital: Tamargo—custody; Libi—gun key; Amadora—teacher-in-charge; Francis—private picnic (majority no liability); Mary—mechanical cause; PSBA—outsider contract; Valenzuela—company car. “T L A F M P V” is a case sequence index, not statutory mnemonic.
Rapid case test: Which court opinion—in St. Francis—found the teachers and school negligent?
The dissent (Padilla, J.), aligning with the lower-court approach. The Supreme Court majority absolved the petitioning teachers and school.

03 · Legal accuracy audit

Verified Clarifications & Examination Traps

The lecture's contested statements are retained as learning cues, but these are the points that need precise language in a written answer. Citations link to primary statutory text and the seven decisions. The reviewer was cross-checked against sources available as of 27 October 2026.

1 · St. Francis majority vs notes — MATERIAL CORRECTION

Notes: “Only the negligent teachers who organized the picnic were solidarily liable.” Verified: the Supreme Court majority set aside liability for the school and petitioning teachers, finding no official sanction and adequate care by the participating teachers. A dissent would have imposed liability. G.R. No. 82465, Feb. 25, 1991.

2 · PSBA was not a final damages judgment — MATERIAL CORRECTION

Notes: school is directly liable for negligence in the outsider stabbing. Verified: the Supreme Court denied the motion to dismiss and ordered trial to continue; negligent breach was not yet proven. The ordinary duty rests on enrollment contract; a separately tortious Art. 21-type wrong could support tort analysis on proof. G.R. No. 84698, Feb. 4, 1992.

3 · Amadora student age under Art. 2180

Notes: Art. 2180 teacher/head clause only below 21. Verified: Amadora expressly explained that teacher responsibility may apply regardless of the student's age, since custody/control rather than minority alone is the basis. Distinguish FC Art. 218 as applying expressly to minor child. G.R. No. L-47745, Apr. 15, 1988.

4 · FC 219 regulates wrongdoing BY the minor

Notes: school/administrator/teacher automatically solidarily liable when any minor student is injured on a supervised activity. Verified: FC Art. 219 addresses damages caused by the acts or omissions of the unemancipated minor. Injuries from a teacher's unsafe science experiment or catechist's blows require additional direct or employer/contractual grounds and proof of causation. FC Arts. 218–219.

5 · Age 18 and the retained below-21 responsibility

R.A. 6809 §3 amended FC Art. 236, and it is Art. 236's present text that expressly saves the below-21 Art. 2180 duties. Children 18–20 are generally already emancipated adults. A 2023 separate concurring opinion in CICL XXX v. People raised an interpretive objection; do not mistake that opinion for controlling majority amendment. R.A. 6809 · G.R. No. 238798 (2023).

6 · Waiver language requires nuance

Art. 1171 refers explicitly to future fraud, while Art. 1172 addresses negligence and Art. 6 limits waivers contrary to law, morals or public policy. Parent consent for school travel does not conclusively release actionable negligence, but avoid saying all future-negligence clauses are automatically void solely under Art. 1171. Civil Code Arts. 6, 1171–1172.

7 · Vehicle owner absent ≠ no liability

Art. 2184 sends the absent-owner case to Art. 2180, yet St. Mary's explains the additional registered-owner doctrine for road users; mere absence or a vehicle lease does not necessarily end responsibility. Also do not confuse Art. 2184's “2 violations within 2 months” with the separately stated Art. 2185 traffic-violation-at-the-time presumption. Carpitanos · Civil Code.

8 · Specific case fact corrections

Libi: Julie Ann and Wendell each sustained a single gunshot wound from the revolver; not “both with one bullet.” Valenzuela: the injury involved the left leg, not both legs. St. Mary's: the lower courts' negligence ruling was reversed; the SC remanded to determine responsibility of defendants other than the school.

9 · State and LGU responsibility

LGC §24 says LGUs and officials are not exempt from damages, but still requires an actionable basis; governmental/proprietary distinction, consent requirements and specific Art. 2189 control/supervision must be kept distinct. In Art. 2180, the special agent is an exception linked to a specific commission, not simply any public employee.

10 · Parent/guardian ordering under special parental authority

General Art. 2180 / Libi: parents can be primary. FC Art. 219: the listed parents/guardians are subsidiarily liable behind qualified school/childcare special-authority defendants, subject to appropriate diligence. The same parent can occupy different positions under different liability theories.

“FIVE switches” for every exam hypothetical

  1. Identity switch: direct actor, employer, parent, guardian, owner, school or government?
  2. Source switch: Art. 2176 / 2180 / 2184 / FC 219 / enrollment contract / delict?
  3. Control switch: employment assignment, vehicle presence, child age, custody, school authorization?
  4. Causation switch: whose act actually and proximately produced the injury? Concurrent causes or a superseding/independent cause?
  5. Remedy switch: solidary, principal, subsidiary or individual? Due diligence defense? Full payment and contribution?

Solidary ≠ subsidiary

Solidary: each may pay all externally. Subsidiary: secondary, reached upon legal predicate such as principal debtor's insolvency. Neither word means the same as merely “joint.”

Cause ≠ mere occasion

Carpitanos: authorized activity occasioned the trip, but school negligence was not proved the proximate cause of the overturned jeep.

Flashcards — answer before revealing

1. Two independent reckless drivers cause one inseparable injury. Is prior conspiracy required?
No. If both were legally liable proximate contributors to one indivisible injury, Art. 2194 solidarity may apply.
2. Which code article lets A recover B's share after A alone pays the whole award?
Art. 1217, with applicable interest and insolvency qualifications. Art. 2181 may additionally be relevant to a party paying for a dependent/employee.
3. Owner present, sudden driver mistake with no reaction time. Automatically solidarily liable?
No. Art. 2184 requires that the owner could have prevented the mishap by due diligence.
4. A 20-year-old lives with parents and commits a quasi-delict. Is the parent's only answer “adult already”?
No. R.A. 6809 §3's amendment to FC Art. 236 retained Art. 2180 responsibility for children and wards below 21 where the remaining conditions apply.
5. In Amadora, was the student within custody but all named defendants still absolved?
Yes. Custody existed from legitimate campus presence, but the Court found no sufficient legal/evidentiary basis to impose liability on the particular defendants.
6. Are parents primary or subsidiary under FC Art. 219's school special-parental-authority scheme?
Subsidiary, subject to the statutory defenses and appropriate predicates. Compare general Art. 2180 / Libi parental liability, which is primary.
7. St. Francis vs St. Mary's: why was each school absolved?
St. Francis: private, not school-sanctioned picnic; majority also found participating teachers diligent. St. Mary's: authorized event, but the necessary proximate cause attributable to school was not shown—mechanical steering defect.
8. Does PSBA finally hold the school liable for damages?
No. It recognizes the actionable enrollment safety obligation and orders the trial to continue on whether there was negligent breach.
