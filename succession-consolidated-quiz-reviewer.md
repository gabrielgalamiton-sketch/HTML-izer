# PART I: THE LAW ON DISINHERITANCE (ARTS. 915–923)
> **Macro Anchor (Title IV, Ch. 2, Sec. 6):** Right after the Code locks in compulsory *legitimes*, Section 6 asks when and how a testator may take that protected share away.

## I. Concept, Scope & 7 Essential Requisites (Arts. 915–917)

> **Context & Statutory Compass:** `[Legitimes Protected (886–914)] → [YOU ARE HERE: 7 Formal Gatekeepers (915–917)] → [Grounds (919–921)]`
> * **Statutory Address:** Testamentary Succession — Disinheritance General Rules (Arts. 915–917). Exists only in a valid will against compulsory heirs.
> * **Chain-of-Logic Bridge:** Before examining *why* an heir may be disinherited (Arts. 919–921), you must satisfy all 7 procedural gatekeepers (`W-E-L-S-T-U-T`) and recognize who bears the burden of proof (Art. 917).
> * **Why the Law Cares:** Stops testators from casually disowning family members out of fleeting anger or vague boilerplate.

**[MEM] Mnemonic for the 7 Requisites — `W-E-L-S-T-U-T` ("WELSTUT"):**
1. **W — Valid Will:** Strictly testamentary; valid extrinsically (Arts. 804–814) and duly **probated**. Cannot be done via *inter vivos* instrument, affidavit, or oral declaration.
2. **E — Express & Specifically Named Heir:** Must unequivocally identify the compulsory heir. Tacit omission in the direct line = *preterition* (Art. 854), NOT disinheritance.
3. **L — Lawful Cause:** Must be one of the exclusive (*numerus clausus*) grounds in **Arts. 919, 920, or 921**.
4. **S — Stated in the Will:** The legal ground must be expressly articulated in the text of the will.
5. **T — True & Existing at Execution:** Cause must already exist when the will is made—never future or speculative.
6. **U — Unconditional:** Deprivation cannot depend on a suspensive or resolutory condition.
7. **T — Total (No Partial):** All of the *legitime* or none; cannot forfeit just 1/3 or 1/2.

* **Compulsory Heirs Only (Art. 915):** Only compulsory heirs have a *legitime* and can be disinherited. Voluntary/testamentary heirs (friends, strangers, collaterals) inherit from the free portion by liberality—to exclude them, simply omit or revoke their institution.

### General Rule vs. Exception: Conditional Disinheritance vs. Conditional Revocation
* **GENERAL RULE:** A **conditional disinheritance** is **VOID** (e.g., *"I disinherit my daughter if she marries someone below our socioeconomic bracket"* — void for non-statutory cause AND future/contingent event).
* **EXCEPTION:** A **conditional revocation (lifting) of a disinheritance** is **VALID**. Where the disinheritance is anchored on an existing statutory cause (e.g., *"she is currently leading a dishonorable life, but if she mends her ways, returns home, and serves me well, this disinheritance is lifted"*), the condition attaches to the **pardon/revocation**, not to the disinheritance itself (Professor's Illustration).

### Burden of Proof (*Onus Probandi* — Art. 917)
* If the disinherited heir **denies or contradicts** the truth of the stated cause, the **burden of proof rests on the OTHER HEIRS** who seek to sustain the disinheritance (by preponderance of evidence). The disinherited heir never has to prove a negative.

## II. Grounds to Disinherit Children & Descendants (Art. 919 — 8 Exclusive Grounds)

> **Context & Statutory Compass:** `[7 Formal Gatekeepers] → [YOU ARE HERE: Descendant Grounds (Art. 919)] → [Parent Grounds (Art. 920)]`
> * **Statutory Address:** Testamentary Succession — Exclusive Grounds vs. Children & Descendants (Art. 919[1–8]).
> * **Chain-of-Logic Bridge:** Supplies the exact 8 offenses satisfying the "Lawful Cause" requisite of `WELSTUT`.
> * **Why the Law Cares:** Balances family *legitime* security against grave ingratitude while distinguishing conviction-based grounds from non-conviction fallbacks (Art. 919[6]).

**[MEM] Mnemonic — `A-G-A-F-R-M-D-C` ("A Great Act Forbids Respect, Means Disinheritance Coming"):**
1. **A — Attempt on Life (Art. 919[1]):** Found guilty by **final judgment** of an attempt (attempted, frustrated, or consummated under Art. 6, RPC) against the life of the testator, spouse, descendants, or ascendants.
   * *Executive Pardon:* An absolute presidential pardon **does NOT defeat disinheritance**; it erases penal liability to the State, not personal ingratitude to the testator.
   * *No Criminal Prosecution / No Conviction:* **Cannot** use Art. 919(1); **EXCEPTION / FALLBACK:** Use **Art. 919(6) (Maltreatment by deed)**, which requires NO criminal conviction.
2. **G — Groundless Accusation (Art. 919[2]):** Accused testator (complaint, private complainant, or voluntary witness) of a crime punishable by **6 years or more**, and the accusation is found groundless.
   * *Key Trap:* Acquittal for **reasonable doubt** or technicality does **NOT** suffice. Judgment must positively show the accusation was **false, malicious, or fabricated** (no crime occurred, or child knew testator was innocent).
3. **A — Adultery or Concubinage with Testator's Spouse (Art. 919[3]):** Requires **final conviction** (Arts. 333–334, RPC). Commonly applies to illicit relations with a **stepmother or stepfather**.
4. **F — Fraud, Violence, Intimidation, or Undue Influence (Art. 919[4]):** Coerced or deceived testator into making a will or changing one already made (even if that coerced will was later revoked and replaced).
5. **R — Refusal to Support Without Justifiable Cause (Art. 919[5]):** Requires: (1) testator's actual need, (2) demand, (3) child's financial capacity (if poor, refusal is justified), and (4) unjustified refusal.
6. **M — Maltreatment by Word or Deed (Art. 919[6]):** **No conviction required.** Covers physical violence/unprosecuted attempt on life ("by deed") and grave oral defamation, cursing, or public social-media humiliation ("by word").
7. **D — Dishonorable or Disgraceful Life (Art. 919[7]):** *"Leads a life"* = **continuity/habituality**, not a single isolated slip. Not limited to sexual immorality (includes habitual criminality, drug trafficking, notorious scandal).
8. **C — Civil Interdiction (Art. 919[8]):** Conviction of a crime carrying civil interdiction (Arts. 34, 41, RPC: **death [if not executed], *reclusion perpetua*, *reclusion temporal***). **Need NOT be committed against the testator**—may be committed against a total stranger.

## III. Grounds to Disinherit Parents & Ascendants (Art. 920 — 8 Exclusive Grounds)

> **Context & Statutory Compass:** `[Descendant Grounds (919)] → [YOU ARE HERE: Parent/Ascendant Grounds (Art. 920)] → [Spouse Grounds (Art. 921)]`
> * **Statutory Address:** Testamentary Succession — Exclusive Grounds vs. Parents & Ascendants (Art. 920[1–8]).
> * **Chain-of-Logic Bridge:** When a testator leaves no descendants, parents step in as compulsory heirs—this section governs when a child may strip those parents.
> * **Why the Law Cares:** Protects vulnerable children from abandonment or abuse while setting traps on female-specific virtue, omission of maltreatment, and Art. 920(8) vs. 920(2).

1. **Abandonment vs. Virtue/Immorality (Art. 920[1]) — Gender Trap:**
   * **Abandonment of children** (total deprivation of parental care, support, and moral guidance during minority) → **Gender-Neutral** (applies to sons and daughters).
   * **Inducing daughters to live a corrupt/immoral life** (e.g., prostitution) OR **attempting against their virtue** (e.g., attempted rape, acts of lasciviousness, no conviction required) → **Female-Specific** (daughters and granddaughters).
2. **Five Overlapping Grounds (Art. 920[2–5, 7]):** Mirror Art. 919(1–5) reciprocally (Attempt on life w/ conviction; Groundless accusation ≥6 yrs; Adultery/concubinage w/ testator's spouse; Fraud/violence on will; Refusal to support).
3. **Loss of Parental Authority (Art. 920[6]):** Must be due to **parental fault/culpability** under the Family Code (e.g., excessive harshness, corrupting orders), NOT benign termination like reaching age 18.
4. **Attempt by One Parent Against the Other's Life — `Art. 920(8)` vs. `Art. 920(2)` (TOP EXAM TRAP!):**
   * **Art. 920(8) (No Conviction):** Father attempts to kill Mother; **no criminal case/conviction**. Child can disinherit Father, **UNLESS** Mother and Father reconcile. Parental reconciliation extinguishes Art. 920(8).
   * **Art. 920(2) (With Final Conviction):** Father attempts to kill Mother (an ascendant of the child-testator) and is **convicted by final judgment**. Mother's subsequent forgiveness/reconciliation **does NOT bar the child** from disinheriting Father! Under Art. 922, only reconciliation between Father and the **child-testator himself** defeats Art. 920(2).

## IV. Spousal Disinheritance (Art. 921), Reconciliation (Art. 922), Representation (Art. 923) & Imperfect Disinheritance (Art. 918)

> **Context & Statutory Compass:** `[Grounds (919–920)] → [YOU ARE HERE: Spouse, Reconciliation, Rep. & Defects (921–923, 918)] → [Part II: Legacies & Devises]`
> * **Statutory Address:** Testamentary Succession — Final Disinheritance Rules (Arts. 921–923, 918; FC Arts. 55, 63[4]).
> * **Chain-of-Logic Bridge:** Covers the surviving spouse and answers the three aftermath questions: *Can reconciliation undo it? Do innocent kids inherit? What if the testator failed `WELSTUT`?*
> * **Why the Law Cares:** Shields innocent grandchildren from a parent's fault (Art. 923) and enforces the crucial difference between partial annulment (Art. 918) and total collapse under preterition (Art. 854).

### A. Grounds to Disinherit a Spouse (Art. 921 — 6 Grounds)
**[MEM] Mnemonic — `A-G-F-L-P-R`:**
1. **A**ttempt on life of testator, descendants, or ascendants (conviction) — Art. 921(1).
2. **G**roundless accusation (≥6 yrs) — Art. 921(2).
3. **F**raud, violence, intimidation, undue influence on will — Art. 921(3).
4. **L**egal separation cause given — **Art. 921(4)** (no final decree needed; if decree already exists, spouse is disqualified by law under FC Art. 63[4]). Incorporates all **10 grounds under Family Code Art. 55**: *(1) repeated physical violence/grossly abusive conduct; (2) pressure to change religion/politics; (3) attempt to corrupt/induce prostitution; (4) final sentence >6 yrs even if pardoned; (5) drug addiction/habitual alcoholism; (6) lesbianism/homosexuality; (7) subsequent bigamous marriage; (8) sexual infidelity/perversion (broader than adultery/concubinage, no conviction needed); (9) attempt against testator's life (bypasses Art. 921[1]'s conviction requirement!); (10) unjustified abandonment >1 year.*
5. **P**arental authority loss grounds — Art. 921(5).
6. **R**efusal to support children or spouse without justifiable cause — Art. 921(6).

![Art. 919 Descendant vs Art. 921 Spouse Disinheritance Grounds Matrix](images/12-art919-vs-art921-grounds-traps.jpg)

### B. Doctrine of Reconciliation (Art. 922)
* **Before Will:** Deprives testator of the right to disinherit for that offense.
* **After Will:** Renders the disinheritance **ineffectual by operation of law** (no new will/codicil required).
* **Bilateral Rule:** Requires (1) offer of forgiveness, (2) acceptance by offender, and (3) **actual restoration of former relations**. A **unilateral deathbed pardon** (*"I forgive everyone who wronged me"*) without restoration of relations **does NOT lift disinheritance** (Professor's Illustration).

### C. Representation of the Disinherited Heir (Art. 923)
* *Peccata patrum non nocent filiis*: Descendants step into the disinherited heir's place and preserve compulsory rights **as to the *legitime* ONLY** (plus intestate share of any undisposed free portion).
* The disinherited parent is strictly barred from **usufruct or administration** over the property inherited by his/her children.

### D. Imperfect Disinheritance (Art. 918) vs. Preterition (Art. 854) — ₱120M Comparison
* **Imperfect Disinheritance (Art. 918):** No cause stated, non-statutory cause, future/unproved cause, partial, or conditional. Annuls institution **ONLY insofar as it prejudices the heir's *legitime***; free portion institution remains valid!
* **Preterition (Art. 854):** Total omission of a compulsory heir in the direct line. Annuls the institution of heirs **TOTALLY** (unless legacies/devises exist).

| ₱120M Estate (4 Legitimate Children: A, B, C, D) | Legal Analysis | Distribution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Scenario 1: Invalid Disinheritance + Preterition** (*"Disinherit D; give all to A & B"* — C omitted) | D's disinheritance void (Art. 918); C's omission = preterition (Art. 854) → **total annulment** → Intestacy! | **A=₱30M, B=₱30M, C=₱30M, D=₱30M** |

# PART II: THE LAW ON LEGACIES AND DEVISES (ARTS. 924–959)
> **Macro Anchor (Title IV, Chapter 2, Section 7 — Arts. 924–959):** Completing Testamentary Succession right after Disinheritance (Sec. 6): rather than instituting heirs to fractional aliquots of the whole estate, Section 7 governs particular gifts of determinate objects, money, or individualized rights payable strictly out of the **free portion**.

## V. General Concepts, Burdens, Solidary Liability & Eviction (Arts. 924–928)

> **Context & Statutory Compass:** `[Part I: Disinheritance (915–923)] → [YOU ARE HERE: Particular Gifts, Persons Burdened & Warranties (924–928)] → [Property Ownership Rules (929–933)]`
> * **Statutory Address:** Testamentary Succession — Legacies & Devises General Framework (Arts. 924–928, 782, 854, 1087; NIRC Sec. 94; Rule 88).
> * **Chain-of-Logic Bridge:** Once we know which compulsory heirs keep their legitimes, Section 7 governs particular gifts carved out of the free portion: *what things may be given, who must pay them, and what liability attaches upon loss or eviction.*
> * **Why the Law Cares:** Shields compulsory legitimes from sub-legacy overcharging while holding co-heirs in possession solidarily accountable for negligent destruction (Art. 927).

### A. Core Definitions, Purposes & Heir vs. Legatee/Devisee Distinctions (Arts. 782 & 924)
* **Legacy vs. Devise:** **Legacy** = gift of *personal* property (corporeal/incorporeal); **Devise** = gift of *real* property (immovable). Both confer an **economic benefit other than a fractional aliquot share** (*Manresa; J.B.L. Reyes*), chargeable exclusively to the **free portion** (never impairing the legitime).
* **Subject Matter Limit ("Within the Commerce of Man"):** Must exist (present or determinate future thing like expected harvest), be lawful, and be susceptible of private appropriation (void: human organs, public plazas, prohibited narcotics). *Testator may also bequeath a mere right (credit, usufruct, easement, remission of debt).*
* **Four Statutory Purposes (*Paras citing 6 Manresa 652*):** Rewards for past services (remuneratory), gratitude, charity/piety, and individual personal preferences.

| Parameter | Instituted Heir (Art. 782) | Legatee / Devisee (Art. 924) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Object Received** | Whole estate or aliquot fractional share (1/2, 1/3). | Specific, determinate object, cash sum, or individualized right. |
| **Source of Right** | Testate (*by will*) **OR** Intestate (*by law*). | Exists **ONLY in testamentary succession** (never by intestacy). |
| **Effect of Preterition (Art. 854)** | Institution of heirs annulled ***in toto***. | **Respected & preserved**, insofar as not inofficious. |
| **Liability for Estate Debts** | Liable up to inheritance value received. | Not personally liable unless sub-charged or estate is insolvent (Art. 925 / Rule 88). |

* **Tax & Distribution Check — *Vera v. Navarro*, 79 SCRA 408, G.R. No. L-27745 (Oct. 18, 1977)** *(Citation Note: cited in Paras as 79 SCRA 608; official report is `79 SCRA 408`)*: Heirs/beneficiaries are **not solidarily liable** for succession taxes; each answers individually up to benefits received. A probate court cannot order premature distribution prior to tax clearance/bond *(modern NIRC TRAIN Law retains a single 6% estate tax + BIR eCAR bar on premature transfer under Sec. 94)*.

### B. Persons Burdened, Ceilings & Proportional Contribution (Arts. 925–926)
* **Classifications (*Paras*):** **Legacy Proper** (charge on estate; no specific heir named) vs. **Sub-Legacy / Sub-Devise** (burden imposed personally on an heir/legatee, *e.g., "car to X, provided X pays Y ₱500"*) vs. **Pre-Legacy** (testamentary gift granted to a co-heir in addition to his hereditary share).
* **Jurado's Procedural Nuance:** Because wills under probate are administered by an executor/administrator, **Art. 926(2)** (making heirs personally liable in proportion to shares) operates in practice **only when there is no judicial administration**.
* **Ceilings (*Art. 925[2]*):** (1) A **compulsory heir** burdened with a sub-legacy pays **ONLY up to his free-portion excess** (legitime is untouchable—*Jurado Example:* ₱10k legitime + ₱5k FP charged w/ ₱10k gift → pays **₱5k max**); (2) a **legatee/devisee** pays **ONLY up to the value of the gift received** (₱100k piano charged w/ ₱150k gift → **₱100k max**).
* **Proportionate Contribution Ratio (*Paras' Computation*):** Net estate = **₱1,000,000**. Legitimate children **A** & **B** instituted **3/5** (₱600k = ₱250k legitime + **₱350k FP**) and **2/5** (₱400k = ₱250k legitime + **₱150k FP**), charged with a **₱50,000** legacy to **F**. Contribution ratio tracks **free-portion shares (350:150 = 7:3)**, NOT 3:2! → **A pays ₱35,000 (7/10); B pays ₱15,000 (3/10)**.

### C. Solidary Possession (Art. 927) & Eviction / Hidden Defects Warranty (Art. 928)
* **Art. 927:** Two or more co-heirs who **take possession** of estate property are **solidarily liable** to the legatee/devisee for loss or destruction due to **fault/negligence** of any one of them (external solidary liability; internally under **Art. 1087**, the innocent paying co-heir recovers full reimbursement from the negligent co-heir — ***Debuque v. Climaco*, 99 SCRA 353 [1980]**).
* **Art. 928 (Eviction & Hidden Defects):**
  * **Generic / Indeterminate Bequest:** Obligor (estate or burdened heir) IS **liable for eviction** AND impliedly for **hidden defects/encumbrances** (*Paras, Manresa, Valverde*, since Art. 941 mandates medium quality).
  * **Specific / Determinate Bequest:** **NO warranty** against eviction (testator intended to give only whatever right he possessed).
  * **Paras' Court-Approval Qualification:** If delivered pursuant to **court order in judicial administration proceedings**, there is **NO warranty against eviction** by the estate/administrator even if generic.

## VI. Property Ownership Rules (Arts. 929–933) & Encumbrances, Credits & Creditor-Beneficiaries (Arts. 934–939)

> **Context & Statutory Compass:** `[Burdens & Warranties (924–928)] → [YOU ARE HERE: Ownership Matrix & Credits/Mortgages (929–939)] → [Generic, Special, Vesting & Reduction (940–950)]`
> * **Statutory Address:** Particular Bequests — Owned Partly/Wholly by Strangers or Beneficiaries (Arts. 929–933), Mortgages/Credits/Mistaken Debts (Arts. 934–939; R.A. 11057; Rule 86 Sec. 7; Arts. 1423–1430, 2154).
> * **Chain-of-Logic Bridge:** Resolves what happens when the testator tries to give away something *not wholly his* (co-owned, stranger-owned, already beneficiary-owned) or *burdened by a mortgage or debt*.
> * **Why the Law Cares:** Prevents unintended gifts of strangers' assets through mistaken belief of ownership (Art. 930) while protecting creditor-legatees' bonus presumption (Art. 938).

| **Art. 929 (Partly Owned by Testator)** | **General Rule:** Testator co-owns asset with stranger/heir.<br/>**Exception:** Express declaration giving the **entirety** *(express order to acquire unnecessary — Paras)*. | Confined strictly to **testator's share/interest**. If entirety expressly declared, estate keeps testator's part + buys co-owner's share (or pays just value under Art. 931). If partitioned to co-owner w/ cash reimbursement before death: **revoked (Art. 957[2])** unless entirety expressly declared (survives as to co-owner's original half; *Jurado & Paras*). |
| **Art. 930–931 (*Legado de Cosa Ajena*)** | **1. * **Paras' 4 Classic Car Variations (*T writes "I give X's car to Y", knowing X owns it*):**
  1. **X sells to Z, then T dies:** **Y inherits** (estate buys from Z or pays *justo precio*).
  2. **X sells to T himself:** **Y inherits** (T owns it at death).
  3. **X sells to Y himself (onerous):** **Y gets reimbursement** from estate (Art. 933[2]).
  4. **X donates to Y (gratuitous):** **Y gets nothing** (double enrichment barred, Art. 933[2]).
* **Naked Ownership Bequeathed (*Paras*):** If **T** owns naked ownership and **U** holds usufruct, and **T** leaves naked ownership to **D**, full ownership consolidates in **D** upon **U**'s subsequent death.

### B. Pledged/Mortgaged Gifts (Art. 934), Credits (Arts. 935–937) & Creditors (Arts. 938–939)
* **Art. 934 (Encumbered Gifts):**
  * **Pledges, Mortgages, Antichresis & R.A. 11057 (Personal Property Security Act) Security Interests** (whether before or after will): **Estate MUST pay the secured debt** so beneficiary receives unencumbered title (*unless contrary intent in will OR security was for a non-recoverable obligation like fidelity bond — 6 Manresa 683*). Under **Rule 86, Sec. 7**, mortgagee has 3 remedies: waive security & file claim; foreclose & claim deficiency; or rely solely on mortgage (10-yr action, waiving deficiency).
  * **Perpetual/Temporary Charges:** **Easements, usufructs, registered/over-1-yr leases, annuities** **PASS WITH THE PROPERTY**—estate does NOT clear them!
* **Arts. 935–937 (Legacy of Credit & Remission of Debt):**
  * **Credit vs. Third Party:** Estate assigns all **rights of action & accessory securities**; **NO warranty** of debtor's solvency or credit's existence.
  * **Remission of Beneficiary's Debt:** Estate issues an **acquittance/release** (subject to reduction if inofficious). **Generic remission (Art. 937)** covers **ONLY debts existing at will execution**, NOT subsequent loans!
  * **Revocation Trap (Art. 936):** Both credit and remission are revoked ONLY if testator files a **JUDICIAL SUIT against the debtor** (*Jurado & Paras:* a **lawyer's extrajudicial demand letter does NOT revoke!**). **Par. 2:** Returning/bequeathing a pledged item to the debtor extinguishes **only the pledge/security interest (incl. mortgage/antichresis per Paras)**—the principal debt remains collectible!
* **Arts. 938–939 (Legacy to Creditor & Mistaken Debts):**
  * **Art. 938 (Presumption of Liberality):** A legacy to a creditor is a **BONUS (gets BOTH debt and gift)** unless expressly declared in payment (*dacion en pago* if accepted unconditionally).
  * **Santos v. Manarang, 27 Phil. 209 (1914):** A clause directing payment of listed debts is **NOT a legacy**; creditors must still file claims inside probate's **statute of non-claims (Rule 86)** or be forever barred.
  * **Art. 939 (Mistaken Debts):** Ordering payment of a **non-existent debt** is void (*considered not written*; recovery under **Art. 2154** *solutio indebiti*); excess sum not due unless intended as liberality. **Exception:** Ordering payment of a **prescribed debt** is **VALID** as compliance with a **natural obligation** (**Arts. 1423–1430**).

## VII. Generic vs. Specific Gifts, Vesting, Risk & the Art. 911 vs. Art. 950 Reduction Battle (Arts. 940–950)

> **Context & Statutory Compass:** `[Ownership & Credits (929–939)] → [YOU ARE HERE: Generic vs. Specific Risk, Vesting & Reduction Battle (940–950)] → [Delivery, Election, Merging & Revocation (951–959)]`
> * **Statutory Address:** Alternative/Generic/Support/Pension/Usufruct Bequests, Vesting/Fruits/Risks, and Payment Order (Arts. 940–950, 911, 1199–1205, 866; Rule 88).
> * **Chain-of-Logic Bridge:** Once we know a gift is valid, we must determine *when title passes, who collects rent before/after death, who suffers accidental loss, and who gets cut when the estate runs out of cash*.
> * **Why the Law Cares:** Separates fungible movables from individualized real estate (Art. 941) and enforces the bright-line trigger between compulsory-heir protection (Art. 911 *pro rata*) and legatee priority (Art. 950 `R-P-S-E-S-A`).

![Specific Determinate vs Generic Personal vs Indeterminate Real Property Matrix](images/21-specific-vs-generic-realty-risk.jpg)

### A. Alternative & Generic Bequests, Quality & Right of Choice (Arts. 940–943, 1199–1205)
* **Alternative Bequests (Art. 940):** Choice belongs to **burdened heir/executor** unless expressly given to legatee/devisee (**Art. 1200**). Once **communicated (Art. 1201)**, choice is **irrevocable** (even if exercised in a will — *Paras*), and converts to a simple obligation; **Arts. 1199–1205** govern impossibility.
* **Specific vs. Generic Personal vs. Indeterminate Real Property:**

| Feature | Specific Determinate Thing (Arts. 948, 928) | Generic Personal Property (Arts. 941[1], 949) | Indeterminate Real Property (Art. 941[2]) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Validity if None in Estate** | Void / Inoperative (unless acquired before death) | **VALID** (Estate must buy **medium quality** w/ estate cash) | **VOID / INOPERATIVE** unless realty of that kind exists in estate! |
| **Eviction & Hidden Defects (Art. 928)** | **NO warranty** | **LIABLE** *(unless court-ordered delivery — Paras)* | **LIABLE** *(unless court-ordered delivery — Paras)* |
| **Transfer of Ownership** | **Immediately at Death** (Art. 948; Art. 777) | Upon **selection / choice** | Upon **selection / choice** |
| **Fruits & Interests** | **From Death** (uncollected pre-death rent → estate!) | From **selection/demand**, unless ordered from death | From **selection**, unless ordered from death |
| **Risk of Fortuitous Loss** | On **Beneficiary from death** (*res perit domino*) | On **Estate** (*genus nunquam perit*) | On **Estate** until selection |

* **Reason (*Jurado & Paras*):** Movables are fungible in commerce, whereas realty lacks natural uniformity and depends on unique location/boundaries. When choice is given to the beneficiary (Art. 942), he picks **any** item in the estate; by default, the executor delivers **medium quality** (*neither inferior nor superior*).

### B. Specialized Gifts, Vesting, Ownership & Fruits (Arts. 944–949)
* **Support & Education (Art. 944):** Support lasts **for life** (even beyond majority); Education ends at **age of majority OR completion of profession/trade** (whichever occurs LATER!). Measured by testator's custom/estate means; **intransmissible** (*intuitu personae*) upon legatee's death unless expressly stated.
* **Periodical Pension (Art. 945):** Enforceable upon **death**; legatee demands installment at the **start of each period** without refund obligation if legatee dies mid-period *(actual cash payout awaits Rule 90 debt settlement/bond, with arrears accruing — Paras)*.
* **Usufruct (Art. 946) / Vesting Rules (Arts. 947–949):** Pure legacies/devises **vest at death** and transmit to beneficiary's heirs; **suspensive conditions** confer only a non-transmissible expectancy until fulfillment (Art. 866). **Pre-death overdue rents** stay with estate; **growing/post-death natural or industrial fruits** go to specific devisee **without charging production costs** (*6 Manresa 731*). Money legacies bear interest **ONLY from formal judicial/extrajudicial demand** absent express testamentary grant (***Fuentes v. Canon*, 6 Phil. 117 [1906]**; ***Chiong Joc-Soy v. Vaño*, 8 Phil. 119 [1907]**). Good-faith withdrawn probate opposition does not forfeit a devise, and devisee keeps post-death fruits (***Santos v. Buenaventura*, 18 SCRA 47 [1966]**). *(Persuasive citation: **Lake v. Harrington**, 120 Miss. 74 [1953] on ademption).*

### C. The Reduction Battle: Art. 911 vs. Art. 950 (`R-P-S-E-S-A`)
| Scenario (When Free Portion Is Insufficient) | Governing Rule | Order & Method of Reduction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Compulsory legitimes impaired OR *donations inter vivos* exist** | **ARTICLE 911** | All legacies/devises reduced ***PRO RATA*** without class distinction (except **preferential** gifts cut last before reducing donations *inter vivos*). |
| **Legitimes INTACT (or no compulsory heirs) AND NO donations *inter vivos*** | **ARTICLE 950 (`R-P-S-E-S-A`)** | Paid strictly top-to-bottom until cash runs out:<br/>1. **R**emuneratory → 2. **P**referential → 3. **S**upport → 4. **E**ducation → 5. **S**pecific determinate thing in estate → 6. **A**ll others ***pro rata***. |

* **Statutory Update (Debt Liquidation First — *Paras*):** Before applying Art. 950, estate debts must first reduce legacies proportionately under **Rule 88, Secs. 1–3 & 6, Rules of Court** *(superseding Act No. 190, Sec. 729 cited in old textbooks)*.
* **All 3 Source Computation Walkthroughs:**
  1. ***Jurado* #1 (No Compulsory Heirs):** Gross ₱100k − Debts ₱40k = **Net ₱60k**. Bequests: R=₱10k, P=₱10k, S=₱15k, E=₱15k, Ordinary X=₱15k, Y=₱5k (Total ₱70k). **Result (`R-P-S-E-S-A`):** R(10k) + P(10k) + S(15k) + E(15k) paid **in full (₱50k)**; remaining **₱10k** split *pro rata* (3:1) → **X=₱7,500; Y=₱2,500**.
  2. ***Jurado* #2 (Compulsory Heirs Present, Legitime Intact, No Donations):** Net ₱40k; 2 LCs take ₱20k legitime; **Free Portion = ₱20k**. Bequests: R=₱5k, S=₱10k, E=₱15k, X=₱10k. **Result:** Art. 950 governs (no legitime impairment & no donations) → **R=₱5k; S=₱10k; E=₱5k**; **X=₱0**.
  3. ***Paras* #3 (Debts First + Art. 950 Combined):** Gross ₱410k − Debts ₱50k = **Net ₱360k** (no compulsory heirs). Bequests: Support=₱200k, Education=₱200k, Specific Piano=₱100k (Total ₱500k; 2:2:1 ratio). **Step 1 (Deduct ₱50k debts *pro rata* per Rule 88):** S=₱180k, E=₱180k, Piano=₱90k. **Step 2 (Apply Art. 950 on ₱360k net):** **Support=₱180k; Education=₱180k; Specific Piano=₱0**.

## VIII. Delivery, Election, Merging, Revocation by Law & Relatives (Arts. 951–959) + 5 Inline ALAC Problems

> **Context & Statutory Compass:** `[Generic, Risk & Art. 950 (940–950)] → [YOU ARE HERE: Delivery, Merging, Revocation & Relatives (951–959)] → [Part III: Intestacy (Arts. 960–1014)]`
> * **Statutory Address:** Final Rules of Legacies & Devises (Arts. 951–959, 830[1], 892, 960[2], 962, 1010, 1015, 1055).
> * **Chain-of-Logic Bridge:** Bridges directly into Intestacy and Common Rules: failed gifts merge back via **Art. 956 (`ISRAI`)**, dual onerous acceptance mirrors **Art. 1055**, and general *"relatives"* clauses test **Art. 959** vs. intestate rules.
> * **Why the Law Cares:** Stops heirs from looting probate assets (Art. 953), strips away un-revoked transfers where consent was vitiated (*Dimagiba*), and catches the classic widow/nephew exclusions under Art. 959.

### A. Delivery, Payment, Election & Merging Into Estate Mass (Arts. 951–956)
* **Delivery Condition & No Self-Help (Arts. 951–953):** Deliver thing **with all accessories/accessions (Art. 1166)** in the **condition at testator's death** (estate need not strip subsequent easements nor add improvements); cash legacies **must be paid in cash** even if estate holds zero cash. **Zero Self-Possession (Art. 953):** Legatee/devisee **cannot forcibly seize the property**; must demand delivery from executor/administrator only after debt/tax liquidation (***Pastor, Jr. v. CA*, 122 SCRA 885 [1983]**; ***Benedicto v. Javellana*, 10 Phil. 197 [1908]**). Assets donated *inter vivos* leave the estate and cannot answer for legacies (***Cembrano v. Pardo de Tavera*, 68 Phil. 175**). Legacies from specified sale proceeds don't fail disproportionately absent contrary intent (***Testate Estate of Isidro Aragon*, L-2920 [1951]**).
* **Partial/Onerous Election (Arts. 954–955) & Cross-Bridge to Art. 1055:**
  * **Single Gift Partly Onerous (Art. 954[1]):** **All or nothing** (cannot keep free part and reject burden). **Single Gift Wholly Gratuitous:** May accept/repudiate a portion; **Art. 954(2)** transmits election right to heir's own heirs (each chooses independently).
  * **Two Separate Gifts (Art. 955[1]):** If **ONE is onerous**, beneficiary **CANNOT accept the gratuitous and reject the onerous**. *(Contrast: if BOTH onerous or BOTH gratuitous, may accept one and reject the other—unless testator expressly tied them together!)*
  * **Compulsory Heir Who Is Also a Legatee (Art. 955[2] vs. Art. 1055):** A compulsory heir **MAY accept the *legitime* and renounce the free-portion legacy/devise, OR vice versa** (*Art. 955[2]*—contrast **Art. 1055**, where repudiating a *testamentary heirship* forfeits the intestate share too!).
* **Ineffectiveness & Merging into Estate Mass (Art. 956) + Cross-Bridge to Art. 1015 (`ISRAI`):**
  * When a beneficiary cannot/will not take (predecease, incapacity, failed condition, repudiation), and there is **NO substitute** and **NO accretion (Art. 1015)**: the gift **merges into the estate mass** (passing to residuary/legal heirs via intestacy **Art. 960[2]**). Follow **Paras' `ISRAI`**: **I**nstitution → **S**ubstitution → **R**epresentation *(zero for voluntary legacies!)* → **A**ccretion → **I**ntestacy!
  * **Parish Priest of Victoria v. Rigor, 89 SCRA 493 (1979):** Testator conditioned devise of ricelands on a male relative already *studying for the priesthood* at death; none qualified at death (could not wait indefinitely for unborn/future relatives) → devise inoperative, merged into estate mass via **Art. 956** to intestate heirs.

![Art. 957 Implied Revocation by Law & Art. 936 Suit Flowchart](images/23-art957-revocation-and-936-suit.jpg)

### B. Revocation by Law (Art. 957), Mistaken Description (Art. 958) & "Relatives" (Art. 959)
* **Art. 957 (`T-A-L` Revocation by Operation of Law under Art. 830[1]; *Jurado adds Art. 936 suit as 4th mode*):**
  1. **Transformation (No. 1):** Testator/agent changes **BOTH form AND denomination** (*cloth → suit; riceland → fishpond; swimming pool → tennis court*). Mere repair, accession, or enlargement does NOT revoke (*Jurado*).
  2. **Alienation (No. 2):** Voluntary alienation of whole or part permanently revokes (to extent alienated)—**EVEN IF contract is null** (e.g., for lack of consideration/formal defect, since *animus revocandi* still existed!) and testator reacquires it!
     * **THREE CRITICAL EXCEPTIONS (Revives / Remains Valid):**
       1. Reacquisition via **Right of Repurchase (*Pacto de Retro*)** (**Art. 957[2]**);
       2. Alienation annulled due to **Vitiated Consent** (fraud, intimidation, undue influence — ***Fernandez v. Dimagiba*, 21 SCRA 428 [1967]** lacked genuine *animus revocandi*);
       3. **Involuntary Alienation** (judicial execution sale / expropriation): **`[AUTHOR SPLIT]`** *Paras citing 15 Scaevola 260* holds reacquisition preserves the devise; *Source 3 Commentary* treats permanent involuntary loss under **No. 3 (legal loss)**. *(Conditional alienation: failed suspensive promise does not revoke; resolutory sale revokes immediately — Paras).*
  3. **Total Loss (No. 3):** Fortuitous destruction before or after death (without heir's fault) extinguishes specific gift; generic gifts survive (*Art. 928* eviction/delivery remains).
* **Art. 958 (Mistake in Name or Description):** ***Falsa demonstratio non nocet***—wrong street number (*"No. 957"* vs. *"No. 759 Dian St."*) or wrong car model year is harmless if object is clearly identifiable.
* **Art. 959 (Dispositions Generally to *"My Relatives"*):**
  * Restricted strictly to **consanguinity (blood kin) up to the 5th degree (Art. 1010)** of the **TESTATOR himself** (*excludes surviving spouse & in-laws*).
  * **Strict Proximity (Art. 962) Applies WITHOUT Intestate Rules:** **NO right of representation** (siblings exclude nephews/nieces); **NO preference between lines** (2nd-deg grandchild and 2nd-deg sister share equally! — *6 Manresa 36*); **NO 2:1 full/half blood distinction** (*Jurado*).
  * **Jurisprudence Trap:** ***Belen v. BPI*, 109 Phil. 1008 (1960)** (Art. 959 does NOT govern *"descendants"* of a third-party legatee—children/grandchildren concur *per capita*); ***Vda. de Singson v. De Lim*, 74 Phil. 109 (1943)** *(not 47 Phil.)* (bequest *"to all who are entitled thereto"* triggers full **intestate succession**, allowing representation!).
  * ***Jurado*'s ₱60,000 Problem:** **A** leaves all *"to all my relatives"*; survivors: Widow **B**, brothers **C & D**, nephews **E & F** (kids of dead sister). **Result:** **B = ₱30k** (pure 1/2 legitime; excluded from *"relatives"*); **C = ₱15k, D = ₱15k** (take FP equally); **E & F = ₱0** (no representation!).

> **Cross-Topic Quiz Bridges (Arts. 924–959 Integration):**
> 1. **Mixed Succession (`Art. 960[2]`):** When a will gives *only* a ₱10M legacy, NEVER deduct it off the top of the gross estate—compute legitimes vs. intestate shares on the full estate first, then charge the ₱10M solely to the heir whose intestate share exceeds legitime (e.g., Surviving Spouse's 1/4 vs. 1/8).
> 2. **Vacancies (`Art. 956` → `Art. 1015`):** Inoperative bequests follow `ISRAI` (**I**nstitution → **S**ubstitution → zero Rep → **A**ccretion → **I**ntestacy).
> 3. **Election (`Art. 955[2]` vs. `Art. 1055`):** Compulsory heir can waive a free-portion legacy yet take legitime (`955[2]`), whereas a dual heir who repudiates *testamentary heirship* forfeits intestacy (`1055`).

### C. Five Worked Bar-Style ALAC Problems (Arts. 924–959 Inline Practice)
1. **ALAC 1 — Generic Devise vs. Generic Legacy (`Art. 941`):** *Facts:* **T** bequeathed *"five dairy cows"* to **A** and devised *"two hectares of citrus land"* to **B**. At death, estate held only cash & stocks (zero cows, zero land). **Rule:** **A gets five cows; B gets ₱0.** Generic personalty is valid even if none is in the estate (executor purchases medium quality w/ estate cash); indeterminate realty is **VOID** unless immovable property of that kind exists in the estate at death (**Art. 941**).
2. **ALAC 2 — Involuntary Alienation & Repurchase (`Art. 957[2]`):** *Facts:* **T** devised a building to **B**. Creditor levied & sold it at sheriff's execution sale to **X**. Before death, **T** bought it back from **X**. **Rule:** **B gets the building** (*Paras/Scaevola* prevailing view). Art. 957(2) revokes upon *voluntary* alienation manifesting *animus revocandi*; an involuntary judicial auction does not revoke if **T** reacquires title before death.
3. **ALAC 3 — Onerous vs. Gratuitous Election (`Art. 955[1]`):** *Facts:* **T** left **B** (1) a beach resort charged w/ paying ₱100k/yr to an orphanage, and (2) an unburdened sports car. **B** accepts the car and renounces the resort. **Rule:** **INVALID.** Under **Art. 955(1)**, when one of two separate gifts is onerous, the beneficiary **cannot** reject the onerous one while accepting the gratuitous one (must accept both or renounce both).
4. **ALAC 4 — Conditional Devise & Merging (`Art. 956` / *Rigor*):** *Facts:* **T** devised a commercial lot to grandniece **G** *"provided G graduates as a doctor."* **G** died in 2nd-yr college before **T**, leaving son **S**. No substitute/co-devisee named. **Rule:** **T's legal heirs take via intestacy; S gets ₱0.** Conditional suspensive gift lapsed when **G** predeceased before fulfillment (**Art. 866**); voluntary gifts allow zero representation, so the lot merges into the estate mass (**Art. 956**; *Rigor*).
5. **ALAC 5 — Reduction Priority (`Art. 950` `R-P-S-E-S-A`):** *Facts:* **T** left no compulsory heirs (no donations *inter vivos*), Net Estate = **₱300k**. Gifts: (1) ₱100k to Nurse **N** (unpaid past care — *remuneratory*); (2) ₱100k support for disabled Cousin **C**; (3) ₱100k education for Nephew **E**; (4) ₱100k cash to Friend **F**. **Rule:** **Art. 950** controls: Remuneratory (**N**=₱100k) → Support (**C**=₱100k) → Education (**E**=₱100k) exhausts the ₱300k estate; **F** (6th tier) gets **₱0**.

## IX. Causes, Lines, Proximity & Orders of Intestacy (Arts. 960–962, 978–1014)

> **Context & Statutory Compass:** `[Part II: Legacies & Devises (924–959)] → [YOU ARE HERE: Intestate Triggers & Priority Order (960–1014)] → [Exact Shares (983–1009)]`
> * **Statutory Address:** Legal/Intestate Succession — Causes (Art. 960), Lines/Proximity (Art. 962), Regular vs. Irregular Orders (Arts. 978–1014).
> * **Chain-of-Logic Bridge:** Before computing pesos or fractions, you must identify who stands in line first and whether the decedent was legitimate or illegitimate.
> * **Why the Law Cares:** Prevents property from becoming *bona vacantia* up to the 5th degree and ensures order hierarchy overrides mere degree proximity.

### A. When Intestacy Happens (Art. 960 + Doctrinal Grounds)
1. **No will / Void will / Revoked will** (Art. 830);
2. **Partial disposition / No universal heir** → **Mixed Succession** (Art. 960[2]);
3. **Suspensive condition fails**;
4. **Predecease, incapacity, or repudiation** without substitute or accretion;
5. **Resolutory condition fulfilled** (Art. 871) or **Resolutory term expires** (Art. 885).

### B. Preference of Lines & Subordination of Proximity (Art. 962)
* **Order of Lines:** **Direct Descending > Direct Ascending > Collateral**.
* **Golden Trap:** The Rule of Proximity (*propinquior excludit remotiorem*, Art. 962) **ALWAYS YIELDS to the statutory Order of Intestate Succession**.
  * *Professor's Illustration:* Decedent X is survived only by **Uncle E** (3rd degree: X→Father→Grandfather→Uncle E) and **Nephews C & D** (3rd degree: X→Father→Brother→Nephew). Even though both are 3rd degree, **Nephews C & D (Rank 5) totally exclude Uncle E (Rank 6)**!

### C. Regular vs. Irregular Order of Intestacy & Concurrence Rules
| Priority Rank | Regular Order (Legitimate Decedent) | Irregular Order (Illegitimate Decedent) | Key Mechanics & Cutoffs |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1st Class** | **Legitimate Children** & Descendants (Art. 979) | **Legitimate Children** & Descendants (Art. 979) | Concurs with Rank 3 (Regular) / Rank 2 (Irregular) & Spouse. Excludes Parents! |
| **2nd Class** | **Legitimate Parents** & Ascendants (Art. 985) | **Illegitimate Children** & Descendants (Art. 988) | **Ranks 2 & 3 SWAP.** In Irregular Order, any IC excludes illegitimate parents (Art. 993). |
| **3rd Class** | **Illegitimate Children** & Descendants (Art. 988) | **Illegitimate Parents ONLY** (Art. 993) | Illegitimate decedent's ascending line **stops at parents** (no grandparents via Art. 992). |
| **4th Class** | **Surviving Spouse** (Art. 995) | **Surviving Spouse** (Art. 994) | Concurs with LCs, Ascendants, ICs, or Siblings/Nephews/Nieces! |
| **5th Class** | **Brothers, Sisters, Nephews & Nieces** (Art. 1003) | **Illegitimate Siblings, Nephews & Nieces** (Art. 994) | Excludes all Rank 6 collaterals; Art. 992 bars legit siblings from/by illegit decedent. |
| **6th Class** | **Other Collaterals up to 5th Degree** (Art. 1009–1010) | **THE STATE** (Art. 1011) | 5th-degree limit = *anak ng pinsang buo* (child of 1st cousin). |
| **7th Class** | **THE STATE** (Arts. 1011–1014) | *(Ends at Rank 6)* | *"Kabalikat sa kaunlaran"*—ultimate default heir. |

## X. Intestate Shares, Double-Share Rule, Concurrence & Mixed Succession

> **Context & Statutory Compass:** `[Intestate Orders (960–962)] → [YOU ARE HERE: Shares & Mixed Legacies (983, 895, 1000, 1006)] → [Representation & Art. 992]`
> * **Statutory Address:** Intestate Sharing Ratios & Mixed Succession (Arts. 983, 895, 996–1009, 960[2], FC Art. 176).
> * **Chain-of-Logic Bridge:** Once Section IX identifies the surviving classes, Section X computes their exact fractions—and stops the fatal mixed-succession error of deducting legacies off the top of the gross estate.
> * **Why the Law Cares:** Protects the Legitimate Direct Line's 1/2 minimum floor and maintains the 2:1 full-vs.-half blood rule for siblings and nephews/nieces.

### A. The "One-Half Rule" & Legitime vs. Intestate Combinations
**[MEM] Rule of Thumb:** Whenever the **legitimate direct line** concurs with irregular heirs, the legitimate line gets **at least 1/2** of the estate, and **100% of the estate is exhausted** (no free portion exists in pure intestacy).

| Surviving Heirs | Testate Legitime | Pure Intestate Share | Key Basis |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1 LC + SS** | LC: 1/2 \| SS: 1/4 (FP: 1/4) | **LC: 1/2 \| SS: 1/2** | Art. 996 (*Santillon v. Miranda*, G.R. No. L-19281: plural "children" includes singular!) |
| **2+ LCs + SS** | LCs: 1/2 \| SS: = 1 LC (from FP) | **LCs & SS divide equally** (SS counts as 1 LC) | Art. 996 |
| **LP + ICs + SS** | LP: 1/2 \| ICs: 1/4 \| SS: 1/8 (FP: 1/8) | **LP: 1/2 \| ICs: 1/4 \| SS: 1/4** | Arts. 889, 896, 899 vs. **Art. 1000** |
| **ICs + SS** | ICs: 1/3 \| SS: 1/3 (FP: 1/3) | **ICs: 1/2 \| SS: 1/2** | Art. 894 vs. **Art. 998** |
| **SS + Siblings / Nephews / Nieces** | SS: 1/2 (1/3 if *in articulo mortis*) \| Collaterals: 0 | **SS: 1/2 \| Collaterals: 1/2** | Art. 900 vs. **Art. 1001** |

### B. Double-Share Rule for Full vs. Half Blood (Arts. 1006, 1008, 1009)
* **GENERAL RULE:** Full-blood brothers, sisters, nephews, and nieces take **DOUBLE (2:1)** the share of half-blood brothers, sisters, nephews, and nieces (also applies to 3rd-degree *reservatarios* in *reserva troncal*: *Padura v. Baldovino*, G.R. No. L-11960).
* **EXCEPTION / LIMIT (Art. 1009):** Does **NOT** apply to other collaterals (uncles, aunts, **first cousins**, grandnephews, grandnieces)—they inherit *per capita* **without distinction** between full and half blood.

### C. Concurrence of Legitimate & Illegitimate Children in Intestacy (Arts. 983, 895; FC Art. 176)
* **Step 1 (Always Mandatory):** Satisfy statutory legitimes first (`LCs = 1/2`; `each IC = 1/2 of 1 LC`). If the remaining 1/2 cannot cover all ICs in full, ICs divide the remaining 1/2 equally (never impair LCs' 1/2!).
* **Step 2 (If a Surplus Remains — e.g., ₱120M Estate, 2 LCs [A,B] + 2 ICs [C,D] → ₱30M surplus after ₱60M + ₱30M):**
  * **`[FAVORED VIEW]` Concurrence Theory (Professor's Preference):** Apply the **2:1 ratio to the entire estate** (or the ₱30M surplus: 2:2:1:1). **Final: A=₱40M, B=₱40M, C=₱20M, D=₱20M.**
  * **`[ALTERNATIVE VIEW]` Exclusion Theory:** LCs (1st class) exclude ICs from the ₱30M surplus. **Final: A=₱45M, B=₱45M, C=₱15M, D=₱15M.**

### D. Mixed Succession & Charging Legacies (Arts. 960[2], 1000, 899)
* **FATAL TRAP:** Never deduct a testamentary legacy off the top of the gross/net estate! That shrinks the base and illegally impairs compulsory legitimes.
* **3-Step Legal Formula:**
  1. Compute **legitimes** and **theoretical intestate shares** on the **FULL estate**.
  2. Find the heir whose **intestate share > legitime**.
  3. Deduct the legacy **solely from that heir's excess intestate share** (never dipping below their legitime).
* *Professor's Illustration (₱120M; LP [A,B] + 2 ICs [C,D] + Spouse W; Will: ₱10M to Friend Sarah):*
  * LP: Legitime ₱60M = Intestate ₱60M (0 excess) → **₱60M**
  * ICs: Legitime ₱30M = Intestate ₱30M (0 excess) → **₱30M**
  * Spouse W: Legitime **₱15M (1/8)** vs. Intestate **₱30M (1/4)** → **₱15M excess!** Deduct Sarah's ₱10M here → **W gets ₱20M** (still > ₱15M legitime); **Sarah gets ₱10M**.

## XI. Right of Representation & The Iron Curtain Rule (Arts. 969–977, 990, 992, 777)

> **Context & Statutory Compass:** `[Intestate Shares (983–1009)] → [YOU ARE HERE: Representation & Art. 992 (969–977, 990, 992, 777)] → [Part IV: Common Rules]`
> * **Statutory Address:** Representation, Universal Repudiation & Art. 992 (with R.A. 11642 & *Aquino v. Aquino*).
> * **Chain-of-Logic Bridge:** Explains when descendants or nephews/nieces climb up a degree to step into a failed relative's shoes—and where repudiation (Art. 977) or Art. 992 blocks that climb.
> * **Why the Law Cares:** Preserves branch equality (*per stirpes*) across generations while separating true representation from direct transmission (Art. 777, *Dela Merced*).

### A. Core Mechanics & Limits of Representation
| Parameter | Testate Succession | Intestate Succession |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Triggers** | Predecease, Incapacity/Unworthiness, Valid Disinheritance | Predecease, Incapacity/Unworthiness *(no disinheritance in intestacy)* |
| **Scope Taken** | **LEGITIME ONLY** (Never voluntary free portion!) | **ENTIRE INTESTATE SHARE** |
| **Lines (Art. 972)** | **Descending:** YES (always) \| **Ascending:** NEVER \| **Collateral:** Only in intestacy for **nephews/nieces** surviving with ≥1 uncle/aunt (*per stirpes*; if no uncle/aunt survives, nephews/nieces take *per capita* per Art. 975). Grandnieces excluded (*De Los Santos v. De La Cruz*, G.R. No. L-29192). |

![Collateral Intestacy Double Share & Nephew Representation](images/14-collateral-siblings-nephews-delossantos.jpg)
*(Figure 14 — Collateral Intestacy & Double-Share Mechanics: Rank 5 nephews/nieces beat Rank 6 uncles/cousins, take per stirpes only when concurring with a sibling [Art. 975], apply 2:1 full-vs.-half blood up to nephews/nieces [Art. 1008], and stop before grandnieces [De Los Santos].)*

### B. The Two-Way Repudiation Rule & Universal Repudiation
**[MEM] Anchor:** **"Repudiation Kills Representation (Art. 977), NOT Downward Transmission (Art. 976)."**
* **Art. 977:** An heir who **repudiates CANNOT be represented** by his children.
* **Art. 976:** A person **MAY represent** a predeceased parent whose own estate that person previously renounced (representative inherits directly from grandparent, not through parent's indebted estate).
* **Universal Repudiation Exception (Art. 969 vs. Art. 982) — ₱300M Estate (3 Children A, B, G; 5 Grandchildren D, E, F, H, I):**
  * *If A disinherited, B incapacitated, G predeceased:* Representation applies (*per stirpes*, Art. 982) → 3 branches of ₱100M → **D=₱100M; E,F=₱50M each; H,I=₱50M each**.
  * *If ALL 3 children (A, B, G) repudiate:* No representation (Art. 977). Grandchildren inherit **in their own right (*per capita*, Art. 969)** → 5 equal heads → **D, E, F, H, I = ₱60M each!**

### C. Adopted Children & Representation
* **`[PROFESSOR'S RULE]`:** Adoption creates a purely personal civil bond (*fictio iuris*) solely between adopter and adoptee; an adopted child **cannot represent** the adopter and **cannot be represented** by his/her descendants in the estate of the adopter's relatives.
* **`[CURRENT LAW / BAR UPDATE — R.A. 11642, Secs. 41 & 43]`:** Under the Domestic Administrative Adoption and Alternative Child Care Act (R.A. 11642), legitimate filiation between adopter and adoptee **now extends to the adopter's parents, adopter's legitimate siblings, and legitimate descendants** (altering the old exclusivity/"stranger-to-the-adoption" bar against representation). Watch which statute/era the exam premise invokes!

### D. Article 992 ("Iron Curtain Rule") vs. *Dela Merced* (Art. 777) & *Aquino*
* **`[PROFESSOR'S RULE]`:**
  * **Predeceased Legitimate Child:** Represented **ONLY by legitimate descendants** (illegitimate grandchild barred by Art. 992).
  * **Predeceased Illegitimate Child:** Represented by **BOTH legitimate and illegitimate descendants** (Arts. 902, 990) in a **2:1 ratio** (e.g., ₱30M share → Legit E gets ₱20M, Illegit F gets ₱10M).
  * *Bar Exam Terminology Caution:* Use the statutory Code term **"illegitimate children"** on Philippine Bar exams for codal precision rather than Justice Leonen's sociological term **"nonmarital children"**.
  * *Scope:* Applies *ab intestato* only—does NOT prevent instituting an illegitimate relative in the **free portion** of a valid will.
* **`[CURRENT LAW / BAR UPDATE — Aquino v. Aquino, G.R. Nos. 208912 & 209018, Dec. 7, 2021]`:** The Supreme Court En Banc relaxed the traditional Art. 992 bar in the **direct ascending/descending line**, holding that a nonmarital (illegitimate) grandchild **may represent** a predeceased parent (whether marital or nonmarital) to inherit from a legitimate grandparent. However, **Article 992's barrier still applies in the collateral line** (nonmarital children cannot inherit *ab intestato* from legitimate siblings/uncles/aunts of their parents).
* **The Order-of-Deaths Escape — *Dela Merced v. Dela Merced* (G.R. No. 126707, Feb. 25, 1999):**
  * *Facts (Accuracy Check vs. Oct. 19 Lecture Slip):* Legitimate sister **Evarista** (not "Evaristo") died intestate first (1987). Her legitimate brother **Francisco** **SURVIVED** her (did not predecease her) and died later (1988), leaving illegitimate son **Joselito**.
  * *Ratio:* Under **Art. 777**, Evarista's 1/3 share vested immediately in living Francisco at Evarista's death, becoming Francisco's own property. When Francisco later died, Joselito inherited directly from **his father Francisco** (Art. 988), **NOT by representation** in Evarista's estate. Therefore, **Art. 992 does not apply!**

## XII. Right of Accretion (Arts. 1015–1021)

> **Context & Statutory Compass:** `[Part III: Intestacy] → [YOU ARE HERE: Accretion Mechanics (Arts. 1015–1021)] → [Master Vacancy Matrix]`
> * **Statutory Address:** Common Provisions, Chapter 4, Section 1 — Accretion (Arts. 1015–1021).
> * **Chain-of-Logic Bridge:** Explains when surviving co-heirs called jointly (*pro indiviso*) absorb a vacated share before it drops into intestacy.
> * **Why the Law Cares:** Honors collective lump-sum gifts without disturbing physically earmarked shares (Art. 1017) or the compulsory *legitime* (Art. 1021[2]).

**[MEM] Mnemonic — `U-P-R-A` ("UPRA") + Not Property Alluvion (Art. 457):**
1. **U — Unity of Object:** Single mass, legacy, or devise.
2. **P — Plurality of Subjects *Pro Indiviso*:** 2+ beneficiaries without physical segregation (Art. 1016).
   * *Aliquot vs. Earmarking Trap (Art. 1017):* Saying *"1/2 each"* or *"in equal shares"* allows accretion. **Earmarking** (physical segregation—e.g., *"BPI account to A, Metrobank to B, home vault cash to C"*, or *"Northern half to A, Southern half to B"*) **DESTROYS unity of object and BARS accretion**.
3. **R — RIP Vacancy:** **R**epudiation, **I**ncapacity (or unworthiness/disinheritance), or **P**redecease (+ failed suspensive condition or Art. 845 unidentifiable co-heir).
4. **A — Acceptance & Absence of Substitute:** Remaining co-heirs accept, and testator did not prohibit accretion or name a substitute (substitution defeats accretion!).

* **Accretion in Intestacy (Art. 1018) — Why Only Repudiation Is Named:**
  * **`[FAVORED VIEW]` Justice Jurado's Non-Vacancy Theory (Professor's Preference):** Predecease and incapacity in intestacy never create an Art. 1018 vacancy because either representation operates or the dead/incapacitated person was never called at death (Art. 777), so survivors take **in their own right (*iure proprio*)**. Repudiation creates a true post-opening vacancy (since Art. 977 bars representation), so it **always accrues** to co-heirs.
  * **`[ALTERNATIVE VIEW]` Pro-Accretion Analogy View:** Applies Art. 1016 by analogy when predeceased/incapacitated intestate heirs leave no representatives (identical final math).
* **Compulsory Heirs (Art. 1021):** Accretion applies **ONLY to the free portion** (Art. 1021[1]). If the **legitime** is repudiated, co-heirs succeed **in their own right (*iure proprio*)**, NOT by accretion (Art. 1021[2]).

> **Structured Routing Note (Visual Slot 16 — Pure HTML Architecture in Document):** `Pro indiviso` test (*aliquot fractions* → ACC OK; *physical earmarking* → ACC BARRED) + *Legitime repudiated* → Co-heirs own right (Art. 1021[2]) vs. *Free Portion vacated* → Sub → Accretion (Art. 1021[1]) → Intestacy.

## XIII. Master Matrix of Vacancies & The ₱120M Master Problem

> **Context & Statutory Compass:** `[Substitution] + [Representation] + [Accretion] → [YOU ARE HERE: Unified Vacancy Priority Hierarchy]`
> * **Statutory Address:** Cross-Title Synthesis (Arts. 857–872, 904, 960, 970–977, 1015–1021).
> * **Chain-of-Logic Bridge:** Integrates all four vacancy remedies into one authoritative priority grid for testate legitime, testate free portion, and intestate shares.
> * **Why the Law Cares:** Enforces both immunity of the *legitime* (no substitution) and primacy of the testator's expressed Plan B (substitution beats accretion in the free portion).

### A. Two Philosophical Pillars
1. **Primacy of Testator's Will (Extension of Ownership):** Testacy > Intestacy; substitution ("Plan B") always outranks accretion in the free portion.
2. **Absolute Immunity of the Legitime (Arts. 872, 904):** No condition, burden, or **substitution** can ever be imposed on the legitime (only valid disinheritance can strip it).

### B. Master Vacancy Resolution Matrix
| **Cause of Vacancy** | **TESTATE: Legitime** | **TESTATE: Free Portion** | **INTESTATE: Whole Share** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Predecease / Incapacity** | **NO SUB** → **1. REP** (descending line) → **2. IS** (co-heirs own right / legal heirs) | **NO REP** → **1. SUB** (if named) → **2. ACC** (Art. 1016) → **3. IS** | **NO SUB** → **1. REP** → **2. IS** *(or ACC under minority view)* |
| **Repudiation** | **NO SUB / NO REP (Art. 977) / NO ACC (Art. 1021[2])** → **IS / Own Right** | **NO REP (Art. 977)** → **1. SUB** (if named) → **2. ACC** (Art. 1016) → **3. IS** | **NO SUB / NO REP (Art. 977)** → **1. ACC (Art. 1018)** → **2. IS** (next degree, Art. 969) |

> **3-Column Vacancy Routing Funnel (Visual Slot 17 — Pure HTML Flowchart):** Always split every vacant estate slice into **(Col. 1) Testate Legitime**, **(Col. 2) Testate Free Portion**, or **(Col. 3) Intestate Share** *before* applying SUB / REP / ACC / IS.

## XIV. Capacity to Succeed, Undue Influence & Unworthiness (Arts. 1024–1039, 40–43)

> **Context & Statutory Compass:** `[Vacancies] → [YOU ARE HERE: Threshold Qualification (Arts. 1024–1039, 40–43)] → [Heir's Choice (Arts. 1041–1057)]`
> * **Statutory Address:** Common Provisions, Chapter 4, Section 2 — Capacity & Unworthiness (with Arts. 40–43, 739, 823).
> * **Chain-of-Logic Bridge:** Checks who is disqualified up front—whether from failing personhood (24-hr intrauterine rule), sickbed undue influence (Art. 1027 FP-only void), or total unworthiness (Art. 1032).
> * **Why the Law Cares:** Protects vulnerable dying testators from manipulation and stops wrongdoers/paramours from benefiting from wrongdoing.

### A. Physical Existence, Conceived Children & Simultaneous Death
* **Living at Opening of Succession (Art. 1025):** Exception: Representation when proper.
* **Conceived Unborn Child — "INTRAUTERINE LIFE" (Arts. 40–41, 1025):**
  * *Terminology Alert:* NEVER write "below 7 months old" (that's a baby outside the womb!). Always write **"intrauterine life"**.
  * **< 7 Months Intrauterine Life:** Must survive **24 full hours** after **complete delivery (cutting of the umbilical cord)** to inherit.
  * **≥ 7 Months Intrauterine Life:** Deemed born and acquires capacity the instant it is **alive at complete delivery (cord cut)**—even if it dies **5 minutes later**, its inherited share transmits to its own heirs!
* **Presumption of Simultaneous Death (Art. 43):** Doubt as to who died first among persons called to succeed each other → presumed simultaneous → **no transmission between them**, **EXCEPT** Art. 43 **does NOT bar Right of Representation** (grandchildren represent deceased parent directly from grandparent).
* **Non-Natural Beneficiaries (Arts. 1026, 1029, 1030):**
  * Associations for religious, scientific, cultural, educational, or charitable purposes (Art. 1026); The poor (Art. 1030).
  * **Prayers/Pious Works for the Soul (Art. 1029):** **50% Church / 50% State** (Art. 1013 public schools/charity) **ONLY IF** made in **general terms without specifying application**. If testator specifies exact masses/parishes/proportions → **Art. 1029 does NOT apply** (100% follows specified instructions).

### B. Incapacity by Reason of Possible Undue Influence (Art. 1027)
**[MEM] Mnemonic — `P-R-G-W-M` ("Priests, Relatives, Guardians, Witnesses, Medical") + 3 Ironclad Shields:**
1. Applies **ONLY to Testamentary Succession (Free Portion)**.
2. **NEVER affects Intestacy** or a compulsory heir's **Legitime**.
3. **Prior Healthy Will = VALID** (only wills made *during* the last illness / confidential window are tainted).

* **Par. 1 — Priests & Ministers:**
  * *Catholic Priest:* Must have **actually heard sacramental confession** during last illness (praying the Rosary ≠ confession, *though* Oct. 19 notes praying the Rosary with a dying testator may constitute **extending spiritual aid**!). Even a longtime friend-priest is barred; **all 5 sequential priests** who heard confession during the last illness are barred.
  * *Protestant / Non-Catholic Minister:* Extending **any spiritual aid** (prayers/Scripture) during last illness disqualifies.
* **Par. 2 — Priest's 4th-Degree Relatives & Church/Order/Institution:** Closes loopholes via family/church intermediaries.
* **Par. 3 — Guardians:** Barred **before approval of final accounts** (even if testator dies after approval). **Built-In Exception:** Valid if guardian is **ascendant, descendant, brother, sister, or spouse**.
* **Par. 4 — Attesting Witnesses & Spouse/Parents/Children (with Art. 823):** Barred **UNLESS** there are **3 other competent, disinterested witnesses**.
* **Par. 5 — Physician, Surgeon, Nurse, Health Officer, or Druggist (= Pharmacist, NOT drug user!):** Must have **actually taken care of** (continuously attended) testator during last illness. **Consulting Specialist (Dr. A) NOT barred.**
  * **`[FAVORED VIEW]` Physician/Priest-Child Debate (Professor's Preference):** Teleological/cultural exemption—when the doctor, nurse, or priest is the testator's **own child, spouse, sibling, or ascendant**, Art. 1027(5)/(1) should **NOT** bar them even from the free portion (Filipino filial duty would be absurdly penalized).
  * **`[ALTERNATIVE VIEW]` Strict *Expressio Unius* View:** Par. 3 expressly exempted close relatives while Pars. 1 & 5 omitted it, so Father A (priest-son), Dr. B (doctor-son), and C (2nd-degree sibling under Par. 2) are barred from the **free portion**—yet they STILL inherit their ₱60M legitime AND take the vacated ₱60M free portion **by intestacy** (since Art. 1027 doesn't apply to intestacy!), beating First Cousin FC (5th degree from Father A)!

### C. Morality (Art. 1028 / 739) & Unworthiness (Arts. 1032–1039)
* **Art. 1028 in rel. to Art. 739:** Testamentary gifts to person with whom testator was guilty of **adultery or concubinage** at execution are **VOID**.
* **Unworthiness (Arts. 1032, 1035):** Operates **automatically by law** in **both testate and intestate** succession and **strips even the legitime** (8 causes cited in notes: abandoning/corrupting children, attempt on life, false accusation ≥6 yrs, failure to report violent death within 1 month, **conviction of adultery/concubinage with spouse [Par. 5]**, fraud/violence on will, falsifying/suppressing will).
* **The Art. 1032(5) "Paramour Paradox" (Professor's Classic Scenario):** Husband X catches Wife W and Brother B in adultery, prosecutes and convicts both, then dies **INTESTATE**. Result: **Brother B is disqualified under Art. 1032(5), while unfaithful Wife W is NOT covered by Art. 1032(5) and inherits 100%!**
  * **[MEM] Professor's Hook:** *"If you catch your spouse cheating, immediately write a holographic will disinheriting them under Art. 921(4)!"*
* **Erasing the Offense — Art. 1033 (Pardon) vs. Art. 922 (Reconciliation):**
  * *If NO disinheritance in a will:* **Art. 1033 governs** → requires **Written Condonation** OR **Implied Testamentary Pardon** (instituting heir in will *with prior knowledge* of unworthiness). Informal/tearful reconciliation FAILS.
  * *If EXPRESSLY disinherited in a will:* **Art. 922 governs** → informal **bilateral reconciliation suffices** (no separate written pardon needed).
* **Moment of Determining Capacity (Art. 1034 & Art. 777):**
  * **General Rule:** Moment of **death** of decedent.
  * **Suspensive Condition Exception (Dual Moments):** Capacity required at **BOTH** (1) testator's death AND (2) fulfillment of condition.
  * **Conviction-Based Unworthiness (Art. 1032[2, 3, 5]):** Wait for **final judgment**.
* **Nationality Principle (Art. 1039 & Art. 16[2]) — Mnemonic `O-A-I-C`:** Four succession issues always governed by the **national law of the decedent** (regardless of property nature/location): **O**rder of succession, **A**mount of successional rights, **I**ntrinsic validity of testamentary provisions, and **C**apacity to succeed.

## XV. Acceptance and Repudiation of the Inheritance (Arts. 1041–1057)

> **Context & Statutory Compass:** `[Capacity & Unworthiness] → [YOU ARE HERE: Exercise of Heir's Choice (Arts. 1041–1057)] → [Part V: Drills]`
> * **Statutory Address:** Common Provisions, Chapter 4, Section 3 — Acceptance & Repudiation (with Presumptive Death Arts. 390–392).
> * **Chain-of-Logic Bridge:** Governs how a living, capacitated heir exercises the purely voluntary option to take or reject property—and how Art. 1052 blocks sham waivers that defraud creditors.
> * **Why the Law Cares:** Prevents ownership uncertainty (*interregnum*) via retroactivity to death (Art. 1042), protects wards (Art. 1044), and prevents opportunistic double-role cherry-picking (Art. 1055).

**[MEM] Master Anchor — `V-R-I`:** **V**oluntary & Free (Art. 1041) • **R**etroactive to Death (Art. 1042) • Generally **I**rrevocable (Art. 1056).

### A. Core Rules & Preconditions (Arts. 1041–1043, 390–392)
1. **Voluntariness & Partial Choice (Art. 1041):** Even a *legitime* may be repudiated *after* death (cannot repudiate future expectancy before death). *Paras Commentary:* Partial acceptance/repudiation is allowed since old prohibition was omitted, **SUBJECT TO Arts. 954–955** (cannot keep a gratuitous legacy and reject a connected onerous legacy where prohibited).
2. **Retroactivity & Pure/Absolute Choice (Art. 1042):** Effects relate back to death (Jan 1 death → March 1 acceptance = Jan 1 effects) to prevent an *interregnum*. Heir's own act of acceptance/repudiation cannot carry a condition or term.
3. **Certainty of `DEATH + RIGHT` (Art. 1043) & Presumptive Death (Arts. 390–392):**
   * **Ordinary absence (Art. 390):** **10 years** (or **5 years** if absentee disappeared **after age 75**). **TRAP:** **7 years** (ordinary presumption for other purposes) does **NOT** open succession!
   * **Qualified/Danger of Death (Art. 391):** **4 years** (lost vessel/airplane, war/armed forces, other danger of death).
   * **Returning Absentee (Art. 392):** Recovers property in current condition, alienation price, or property bought with price—**NO fruits or rents**.

### B. Who May Accept or Repudiate (Arts. 1044–1048)
| Beneficiary | Acceptance | Repudiation | Key Trap / Update |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Person w/ Free Disposal** (Age 18 per R.A. 6809) | Personally | Personally | Capacity to inherit ≠ capacity to choose personally. |
| **Minors / Wards (Art. 1044)** | Parents or guardians (no court approval generally needed unless burdened) | **Judicial authorization REQUIRED** | *Guy v. CA* (G.R. No. 163707): Notarized waiver by mother WITHOUT court approval = **VOID**. |
| **The Poor (Art. 1044 / 1030)** | Person named by testator → Executor → Justice of peace, mayor & municipal treasurer (majority + court approval) | Beneficiaries themselves once determined | Designation power ≠ power to surrender poor's rights. |
| **Qualified Juridical Entities (Art. 1045)** | Lawful representative | **Court approval REQUIRED** | Entity must be qualified to acquire the property. |
| **Public Official Establishments (Art. 1046)** | **Government approval REQUIRED** | **Government approval REQUIRED** | Applies even if unconditional! *Qualification:* Red Cross is *sui generis* / independent, not a plain example. |
| **Married Woman of Age (Art. 1047)** | Personally | **Without husband's consent** | **Correction:** Old Civil Code Arts. 114 & 200 (restricting wife) were repealed by the Family Code. |
| **"Deaf-Mutes" (Art. 1048)** | Read/write: personal or agent \| Cannot: guardian | Read/write: personal/agent \| Cannot: guardian + **judicial approval** | Historical statutory wording; read with general capacity rules. |

### C. Forms, Deemed Acceptance Traps & Formalities (Arts. 1049–1051)
* **Express Acceptance (Art. 1049):** Public or private **document** (oral alone isn't express documentary acceptance, though conduct may be tacit).
* **Tacit Acceptance vs. Preservation (Art. 1049):** Repairing a leaking roof, harvesting fruits, or collecting rent provisionally for the estate = **NOT acceptance** if title of heir is not assumed. Filing partition, selling/appropriating as owner = **TACIT acceptance**.
* **Art. 1050 Deemed Acceptance Traps ("Label Doesn't Matter!"):**
  1. **Selling, donating, or assigning** hereditary rights (to stranger or co-heirs) → **Acceptance** (*Ignacio v. Martinez*, 33 Phil. 576: "renouncing" to pay a **₱2,500** debt [Paras text says ₱25,000] = assignment/acceptance).
  2. **Selective gratuitous renunciation** for 1 or some co-heirs (e.g., *"I waive in favor of B only"*) → **Acceptance** (exercise of ownership/donation).
  3. **Renunciation for a price** even to all co-heirs indiscriminately → **Acceptance** (sale).
  * **ONLY EXCEPTION (True Repudiation):** **Gratuitous** AND **indiscriminate** renunciation benefiting the exact co-heirs who would take by **accretion**.
* **Formalities of Repudiation (Art. 1051 — Must Be Express):** (1) **Public instrument** (notarized), (2) **Authentic instrument** (indubitable writing), or (3) **Petition to settlement court**. Silence or saying *"I don't want any part of this"* = NOT valid repudiation.

### D. Creditors, Transmission, Dual Capacities, Irrevocability & 30-Day Rule (Arts. 1052–1057)
* **Protection of Heir's Prejudiced Creditors (Art. 1052):**
  * *4 Requisites:* (1) Existing debt at repudiation; (2) Valid repudiation; (3) Prejudice (insufficient remaining assets); (4) **Judicial authorization**.
  * *Rule:* Any single creditor may petition. Creditor accepts **in heir's name** **only up to the credit amount**. **Excess NEVER goes to the renouncer**—it passes by succession rules (e.g., ₱1.2M share, ₱300k debt → ₱300k creditor, ₱900k co-heirs/legal heirs, **₱0 to renouncer**).
* **Transmission of Unexercised Option (Art. 1053) vs. Representation:**
  * *Chronology:* **A dies → B SURVIVES A → B dies undecided → B's heirs (C) inherit the option.** C must accept B's estate to exercise the transmitted option, and can then reject A's estate (two separate decedents!).
  * *Individual Choice (Art. 1054):* Co-heirs (including multiple heirs under Art. 1053) may choose independently as to their respective shares.
* **Dual Capacities in the SAME Estate (Art. 1055):**
  * **Repudiates as Testamentary Heir** → **Deemed repudiated in BOTH** (cannot claim undisposed 1/4 intestate share!).
  * **Repudiates as Intestate Heir WITHOUT Knowledge of Will** → **May still accept as Testamentary Heir.** *Qualification:* Paras opines even *knowing* intestate repudiation allows testamentary acceptance—treat as author commentary; statute expressly requires *"without knowledge."*
* **Irrevocability & Impugning (Art. 1056):** Irrevocable once made (mere regret never undoes it). Impugnable ONLY for:
  1. **Vitiated Consent** (mistake, violence, intimidation [note Art. 1335: threatening a lawful suit is NOT intimidation], undue influence, fraud); OR
  2. **Unknown Will Appears** *(Manresa/Paras commentary: substantial changes needed)*.
* **30-Day Deemed Acceptance Rule (Art. 1057):** Runs within **30 days from the COURT'S ORDER OF DISTRIBUTION** (NOT from decedent's death!). Silence in extra-judicial/non-court settings does NOT trigger Art. 1057's presumption.

## XVII. 24 Sample Bar-Style ALAC & Recall Drills (With Computation Walkthroughs)

### Problem 1 — Conditional Disinheritance vs. Conditional Lifting (Arts. 915–917)
X executes a will stating: *(Clause 1)* "I disinherit my son A if he ever joins a rival political party." *(Clause 2)* "I disinherit my daughter B because she is presently living a scandalously dishonest life as a notorious swindler; however, if she reforms, leaves the syndicate, and reconciles with me, this disinheritance is lifted." **Are Clauses 1 and 2 valid?**
* **ALAC Answer:** **Clause 1 is VOID; Clause 2 is VALID.** Under Arts. 915–916, disinheritance must be based on an existing statutory cause and must be unconditional. Clause 1 cites a non-statutory, future contingent event and attaches a condition to the disinheritance itself. Conversely, Clause 2 grounds B's disinheritance on an existing statutory cause under Art. 919(7) (leading a dishonorable/disgraceful life), and the condition attaches validly to the *revocation/lifting* (conditional pardon), not to the disinheritance itself.

### Problem 2 — Attempt on Life Without Conviction vs. Art. 919(6) Fallback
Son D stabs his father X with intent to kill. X survives and declines to file criminal charges to avoid scandal. Later, X executes a will disinheriting D for *"physically assaulting and maltreating me by deed when he stabbed me."* D challenges the disinheritance for lack of final criminal conviction. **Rule on D's challenge.**
* **ALAC Answer:** **D's challenge fails; the disinheritance is VALID.** While Art. 919(1) requires final conviction of an attempt against the testator's life, Art. 919(6) allows disinheritance for maltreatment of the testator by word or deed, which requires **no prior criminal conviction**. Because X expressly grounded the disinheritance on maltreatment by deed and assuming the other heirs prove the stabbing under Art. 917, the disinheritance stands.

### Problem 3 — Art. 920(8) vs. Art. 920(2): Parental Attempt & Reconciliation
Father F attempts to murder Mother M. F is prosecuted and convicted by final judgment of frustrated parricide. While F is in prison, M forgives F and reconciles with him. Thereafter, their child C executes a will disinheriting F. F invokes M's forgiveness to invalidate the disinheritance. **May C still validly disinherit F?**
* **ALAC Answer:** **Yes.** While parental reconciliation extinguishes the child's right to disinherit under Art. 920(8) (which applies where there is no conviction), F's **final conviction** triggers **Art. 920(2)** (conviction of an attempt against the life of the testator's ascendant). Under Art. 922, only a bilateral reconciliation between F and the **testator C himself**—not M's forgiveness—extinguishes disinheritance under Art. 920(2).

### Problem 4 — Defective Disinheritance Without Preterition vs. With Preterition (₱120M)
X has four legitimate children: A, B, C, and D, and a ₱120M net estate. In Version 1, X's will says: *"I disinherit D; I institute A, B, and C to my whole estate."* In Version 2, X's will says: *"I disinherit D; I institute A and B as sole heirs"* (omitting C). **Distribute the estate in both versions.**
* **ALAC Answer:**
  * **Version 1 (No Preterition):** D's disinheritance is void for stating no cause (Art. 918), which annuls the institution *only insofar as it prejudices D's legitime*. Collective legitime = ₱60M (₱15M each); ₱60M FP stays validly with A, B, C (₱20M each). **Final: A=₱35M, B=₱35M, C=₱35M, D=₱15M.**
  * **Version 2 (With Preterition of C):** Omitting C totally annuls the institution of A and B under Art. 854, triggering intestacy. **Final: A=₱30M, B=₱30M, C=₱30M, D=₱30M.**

### Problem 5 — Proximity Yielding to Order of Intestacy & Double-Share Collaterals
X (legitimate) dies intestate without descendants, ascendants, or spouse. Survivors: Uncle U (brother of X's father), Full-blood Nephew N1, Half-blood Nephew N2 (children of X's predeceased full/half brothers), and First Cousin FC (full-blood). Net estate: ₱12M. **Who inherits and how much?**
* **ALAC Answer:** **N1 gets ₱8M; N2 gets ₱4M; U and FC get ₱0.** Under Art. 962, proximity yields to the Order of Intestate Succession: though Uncle U and Nephews N1 & N2 are all 3rd-degree collaterals, nephews/nieces belong to Rank 5 (Art. 1003) and exclude Uncle U and First Cousin FC (Rank 6). Under Art. 1008, full-blood nephew N1 takes **double** half-blood nephew N2's share (2:1 → ₱8M and ₱4M).

### Problem 6 — *Per Stirpes* vs. Universal Repudiation (*Per Capita*, Art. 969)
X dies intestate leaving ₱300M, 3 living children (A, B, G), and 5 grandchildren (D from A; E & F from B; H & I from G). All 3 children validly repudiate so their kids can inherit. **Distribute the estate.**
* **ALAC Answer:** **D, E, F, H, and I receive ₱60M each.** Under Art. 977, heirs who repudiate cannot be represented. Because *all* nearest-degree children repudiated, the grandchildren inherit **in their own right (*per capita*)** under **Art. 969**, dividing ₱300M equally by 5 heads (₱60M each), rather than *per stirpes* (₱100M / ₱50M / ₱50M).

### Problem 7 — Iron Curtain (Art. 992), *Aquino v. Aquino*, and *Dela Merced* (Art. 777)
Legitimate siblings Evarista and Francisco are the only heirs of their parents. Evarista dies intestate (₱90M) leaving Francisco and two nephews from a predeceased sister. One year later, Francisco dies intestate, survived by his legitimate child L and illegitimate child I. L seeks to exclude I from the ₱30M share Francisco inherited from Evarista, citing Art. 992. **Is L correct? Contrast if Francisco had *predeceased* Evarista.**
* **ALAC Answer:** **L is WRONG.** Under *Dela Merced v. Dela Merced* (G.R. No. 126707) and Art. 777, because Francisco **survived** Evarista, her ₱30M share vested in Francisco upon Evarista's death. Upon Francisco's death, I inherits directly from his father Francisco (Art. 988), not from Aunt Evarista; thus Art. 992 does not apply (L gets ₱20M, I gets ₱10M at 2:1). **Contrast:** If Francisco had **predeceased** Evarista, I would be **barred** by Art. 992 from representing Francisco in Aunt Evarista's collateral estate (note: *Aquino v. Aquino* relaxed Art. 992 only in the *direct* grandparental line, leaving the collateral curtain intact).

### Problem 8 — Mixed Succession Trap (Arts. 960[2], 1000, 899)
X dies with a ₱24M net estate, survived by legitimate parents P1 & P2, spouse W, and two illegitimate children C1 & C2. X's valid will contains one clause: *"I give ₱2M to my friend F."* **Compute each survivor's share.**
* **ALAC Answer:** **P1 & P2 = ₱12M collectively (₱6M each); C1 & C2 = ₱6M collectively (₱3M each); F = ₱2M; W = ₱4M.** Deducting ₱2M off the top (from ₱24M down to ₱22M) is **illegal** because it shrinks the base and impairs P1/P2's 1/2 legitime (₱12M) and C1/C2's 1/4 legitime (₱6M). Comparing full-base legitimes vs. intestate shares (Art. 1000): only **W** has an excess (Intestate ₱6M [1/4] − Legitime ₱3M [1/8] = **₱3M excess**). F's ₱2M legacy comes strictly from W's share, leaving W with ₱4M (above W's ₱3M legitime).

### Problem 9 — Earmarking & Accretion Exclusion (Arts. 1016–1017)
X's will provides: *"I bequeath the balance of my BPI account to A, the balance of my Metrobank account to B, and my home vault cash to C."* A predeceases X leaving child D. No substitute was named. **May B and C claim A's BPI account by accretion, or may D represent A?**
* **ALAC Answer:** **Neither.** Under Art. 1017, physical segregation (**earmarking**) of distinct bank accounts destroys unity of object, barring accretion to B and C. D cannot represent A because representation never applies to voluntary legatees. The BPI account passes by **intestate succession** to X's legal heirs (Art. 960[2]).

### Problem 10 — The ₱120M Master Vacancy Problem (Testate vs. Intestate)
X leaves ₱120M by will equally to 4 legitimate children (A, B, C, D) with no substitutes. C predeceased (leaving kids E & F); D repudiated (leaving kid G). **Distribute under (a) the Part 2 Vacancy Matrix Formula and (b) Pure Intestacy.**
* **ALAC Answer:**
  * **(a) Testate (Settled Matrix Formula):** Baseline = ₱15M Legitime + ₱15M FP each. **C's ₱15M Legitime** → E & F by REP (₱7.5M each); **C's ₱15M FP** → A & B by ACC (₱7.5M each, since voluntary FP is not represented); **D's ₱15M FP** → A & B by ACC (₱7.5M each; G=₱0 per Art. 977); **D's ₱15M Legitime** → Art. 1021(2) 3-way division across active lines A, B, and C's branch (₱5M to A, ₱5M to B, ₱2.5M each to E & F). **Settled Final Answer: A=₱50M, B=₱50M, E=₱10M, F=₱10M, G=₱0.**
  * **(b) Pure Intestacy:** ₱30M baseline each. C's ₱30M → E & F by REP (₱15M each). D's ₱30M → ACC (Art. 1018) to A & B (₱15M each; G=₱0). **Final: A=₱45M, B=₱45M, E=₱15M, F=₱15M, G=₱0.**

### Problem 11 — Intrauterine Life (Art. 41) & Art. 1027 Undue Influence Timing
T, while healthy in 2023, executes a will giving the free portion to Father P (his friend). In 2026, during T's last illness, T confesses to Father P and is treated by Dr. D (an unrelated physician), while Specialist Dr. S is consulted once by phone. During the illness, T executes a codicil giving ₱5M to Dr. D and ₱2M to Dr. S. T dies survived by a 7.5-month conceived fetus (child of predeceased son), which is completely delivered alive by cutting the umbilical cord and dies 5 minutes later. **Resolve the validity of all gifts and the infant's capacity.**
* **ALAC Answer:**
  1. **Father P's 2023 Gift: VALID.** The will was executed while T was healthy, long before the last illness/confession (no undue influence window).
  2. **Dr. D's 2026 Codicil: VOID (Art. 1027[5]).** Dr. D actually took care of T during his last illness when the codicil was executed.
  3. **Dr. S's 2026 Codicil: VALID.** A mere consulted specialist who did not take care of T is not disqualified.
  4. **Infant's Capacity: CAPACITATED (Arts. 40–41, 1025).** Having an **intrauterine life of ≥ 7 months**, the child acquired civil personality the moment it was alive at complete delivery (umbilical cord cut), even though it died 5 minutes later; its represented share passes to its own heirs.

### Problem 12 — Art. 1032(5) Paramour Paradox & Art. 1033 vs. Art. 922
Husband X catches Wife W and his only sibling, Brother B, in adultery. Both are convicted by final judgment. X dies intestate 2 weeks later after tearfully saying on his deathbed, *"I forgive both of you."* No will was ever executed. **Who inherits X's estate?**
* **ALAC Answer:** **Wife W inherits 100%; Brother B gets ₱0.** Under Art. 1032(5), only the paramour (B) convicted of adultery with the spouse is rendered unworthy—the unfaithful spouse (W) is **not** covered by Art. 1032(5) and remains capacitated in intestacy absent a disinheriting will (Art. 921[4]) or legal separation decree (FC Art. 63[4]). Moreover, because X made **no disinheriting will**, **Art. 1033** governs B's unworthiness, requiring an express written pardon—X's informal deathbed words do not restore B.

### Problem 13 — Minor's Notarized Waiver (Art. 1044) & Art. 1050 Selective Waiver + Art. 1052 Creditors
X dies leaving ₱1.2M each to Adult Son A, Adult Daughter B, and 10-year-old Son M.
1. M's mother executes a notarized waiver of M's ₱1.2M without court approval.
2. Son A, owing Creditor C ₱300k with no other assets, executes a formal public instrument waiving his ₱1.2M *"gratuitously in favor of my brother M only."* **Rule on both acts and Creditor C's remedy.**
* **ALAC Answer:**
  1. **M's Waiver is VOID (Art. 1044; *Guy v. CA*):** Notarization only addresses form (Art. 1051), not capacity; a parent's repudiation of a minor's inheritance requires **judicial authorization**.
  2. **A's Selective Waiver = DEEMED ACCEPTANCE (Art. 1050[2]):** Because A waived selectively in favor of M (excluding B), it is legally an acceptance + donation. If A had instead validly repudiated indiscriminately, **Creditor C** could petition the court under **Art. 1052** to accept in A's name up to **₱300k**, with the **₱900k** excess going to co-heirs—**₱0 to A**. *(Under Art. 1050[2], because A's act is a gratuitous donation to M in fraud of C, C attacks it as a prejudicial disposition/acceptance rather than an Art. 1052 repudiation).*

### Problem 14 — Art. 1053 Transmission vs. Art. 1055 Dual Capacities & Art. 1057 30-Day Trap
X institutes friend H to 3/4 of his estate (1/4 undisposed). H survives X, does nothing for 45 days after X's death (no settlement court distribution order yet), and then executes a formal repudiation as testamentary heir. Later, H dies without having acted on a separate estate left to him by Y, and H's sole child K accepts H's estate and repudiates Y's estate. **Resolve (a) whether H was deemed to accept X's estate after 45 days, (b) whether H can claim the 1/4 intestate share of X's estate, and (c) whether K can accept H's estate while rejecting Y's.**
* **ALAC Answer:**
  * **(a) No deemed acceptance under Art. 1057:** The 30-day presumption runs ONLY from the **court's order of distribution**, not from X's death.
  * **(b) H CANNOT claim the 1/4 intestate share (Art. 1055[1]):** Repudiating as testamentary heir is deemed a repudiation in **both** testamentary and intestate capacities of the same estate.
  * **(c) YES, K can accept H and reject Y (Art. 1053):** Because H survived Y and died undecided, H's unexercised choice transmitted to K as part of H's succession. Accepting H and rejecting Y involve **two distinct estates**, not Art. 1055.

### Problem 15 — Free Portion Ratio Contribution (Art. 926)
Net estate = ₱1,200,000. Compulsory heirs **A** and **B** (legitimate children) are instituted to 2/3 and 1/3 of the estate respectively, and charged generally with a ₱60,000 legacy to **X**. **How much must A and B each contribute?**
* **ALAC Answer:** **A pays ₱50,000; B pays ₱10,000.** Total legitime = ₱600,000 (**A** = ₱300k; **B** = ₱300k). Total given to **A** (2/3) = ₱800,000 (₱300k legitime + **₱500k FP**). Total given to **B** (1/3) = ₱400,000 (₱300k legitime + **₱100k FP**). Under **Art. 926** (Paras' rule), they contribute in proportion to their **free-portion shares** (500:100 = **5:1** → 5/6 and 1/6 of ₱60,000).

### Problem 16 — Stranger's Property Without Express Order to Acquire (Art. 931 Doctrinal Split)
Testator **T** bequeaths *"my neighbor's vintage Mustang"* to **L**, knowing fully that his neighbor owns it, without writing any explicit instruction for the executor to buy it. **Is the legacy valid?**
* **ALAC Answer:** Under the **prevailing view (Tolentino, Jurado, Paras)**, it is **VALID** because knowledge of third-party ownership implies an order to acquire under Art. 931. Under the **strict view (J.B.L. Reyes, Puno, Caguioa)**, it is **VOID/INEFFECTIVE** because Art. 931 requires an express order to acquire.

### Problem 17 — Legatee's Subsequent Purchase After Testator Alienates (Art. 933 vs. Art. 957[2])
**T** executes a will giving **L** a specific painting belonging to **T**. Later, **T** sells the painting to **S**, and **L** buys it from **S** for ₱100,000 before **T** dies. **Can L demand ₱100,000 reimbursement from T's estate?**
* **ALAC Answer:** **NO.** When **T** sold the painting to **S**, the legacy was impliedly revoked under **Art. 957(2)**. **L**'s subsequent purchase from **S** does not trigger reimbursement under Art. 933(2) (*Paras*).

### Problem 18 — Generic Remission of Debts & Demand Letter Trap (Arts. 936–937)
**T** executes a will stating: *"I forgive all debts owed to me by my nephew N."* At execution, **N** owed ₱200,000. Later, **T** lent **N** another ₱100,000 and sent a formal lawyer's demand letter for the ₱200,000 without filing a case. **At T's death, how much (if any) is remitted?**
* **ALAC Answer:** **₱200,000 is remitted; the subsequent ₱100,000 debt is NOT remitted.** Under **Art. 937**, a generic remission covers only debts existing at will execution. Under **Art. 936**, only a *judicial action* (not a mere demand letter) revokes the legacy.

### Problem 19 — Generic Movable vs. Generic Immovable & Eviction Warranty (Arts. 941, 942, 928)
Explain why a generic legacy of *"a 2024 SUV"* is valid even if **T** owned no car at death, whereas a generic devise of *"a 500-sqm commercial lot"* is void if **T** owned no commercial lot—and state the standard of quality and eviction rule if **T** owned multiple cars.
* **ALAC Answer:** Generic personalty is valid even if none exists in the estate because movables are fungible in commerce, whereas indeterminate realty is valid **only if immovable property of that kind exists in the estate at death (Art. 941)**. The executor delivers **medium quality** (unless choice was expressly given to the heir/legatee under **Art. 942**) and the obligor is liable for **eviction** and hidden defects (**Art. 928**, subject to Paras' court-order qualification).

### Problem 20 — Pre-Death Arrears, Post-Death Rent & Loss Risk (Art. 948)
**T** devised an apartment building to **D**. **T** died on May 15. April rent (₱40,000) was overdue and unpaid; June rent (₱40,000) was later collected by the administrator. An earthquake destroys the building on May 20. **Who owns each rent, and who bears the loss?**
* **ALAC Answer:** **April rent** (due/unpaid before death) belongs to the **estate**; **June rent** (accruing after death) belongs to **D**. **D bears the earthquake loss** because ownership and risk (*res perit domino*) passed to **D** upon death (**Art. 948**).

### Problem 21 — Reduction Gatekeeper: Art. 911 vs. Art. 950
Testator dies leaving two legitimate children (**A** & **B**), a net estate of ₱100,000 (legitime ₱50,000 intact; free portion ₱50,000), **NO donations *inter vivos***, and four legacies: ₱20,000 remuneratory (**R**), ₱20,000 support (**S**), ₱20,000 education (**E**), and ₱20,000 cash (**X**). **Which article applies—Art. 911 or Art. 950—and how much does each legatee receive?**
* **ALAC Answer:** **Article 950** applies because legitimes are intact and there are no donations *inter vivos*. Out of the ₱50,000 free portion following `R-P-S-E-S-A`: **R = ₱20,000; S = ₱20,000; E = ₱10,000; X = ₱0.** *(Contrast: If a donation inter vivos had existed, Art. 911 would have reduced all four pro rata to ₱12,500 each.)*

### Problem 22 — Three Exceptions to Implied Revocation by Alienation (Art. 957[2])
Under what **three circumstances** does an alienation (or purported alienation) of bequeathed property *fail* to permanently revoke the legacy or devise?
* **ALAC Answer:** (1) Reacquisition via exercise of a **right of repurchase (*pacto de retro*)** (**Art. 957[2]**); (2) alienation annulled due to **vitiated consent** like fraud or undue influence (*Fernandez v. Dimagiba*); and (3) **involuntary alienation** (e.g., execution sale) later reacquired by the testator (*Paras/Scaevola* view).

### Problem 23 — Disposition "To My Relatives" (*6 Manresa 36* & *Vda. de Singson*, Art. 959)
**T** leaves his entire ₱120,000 estate *"to my relatives."* He is survived by his widow **W**, his sister **S**, his legitimate grandson **G** (from a predeceased legitimate daughter), and his nephew **N** (son of a deceased brother). **Distribute the estate.** *(Bonus: How would the result change if T left no spouse or descendants, leaving only S and N, and instead wrote "to all who are entitled thereto"?)*
* **ALAC Answer:** **G = ₱75,000** (₱60,000 legitime + ₱15,000 FP); **W = ₱30,000**; **S = ₱15,000**; **N = ₱0**. Under **Art. 892**, **G** (1 legitimate descendant) gets 1/2 = ₱60,000 legitime and **W** gets 1/4 = ₱30,000 legitime, leaving **₱30,000** free portion. Under **Art. 959**, **W** is excluded from *"relatives"* (consanguinity only). On the ₱30,000 FP, **G** (2nd degree direct) and **S** (2nd degree collateral) are both in the **2nd degree** (no line preference; *6 Manresa 36*), splitting ₱30,000 equally (₱15,000 each), while **N** (3rd degree) is excluded. *(Bonus: Under **Vda. de Singson v. De Lim**, phrasing "to all who are entitled thereto" triggers intestate representation, giving **S** and **N** ₱60,000 each.)*

### Problem 24 — Mixed Succession Integration With a ₱10M Legacy (Arts. 960[2], 1000, 899)
Suppose **T** executes a will containing *only* a ₱10M legacy to his friend **F**, leaving a ₱120M estate and survived by his legitimate parents, surviving spouse, and two illegitimate children. **From whose intestate share is F's ₱10M legacy deducted?**
* **ALAC Answer:** Strictly from the **surviving spouse's intestate share**, because the spouse's intestate share (**1/4 = ₱30M**) exceeds her legitime (**1/8 = ₱15M**) by ₱15M, leaving her **₱20M** after deducting **F**'s ₱10M legacy without impairing her ₱15M legitime (whereas legitimate parents' 1/2 = 1/2 and illegitimate children's 1/4 = 1/4 leave zero excess over their legitimes).

## XVIII. 25-Item 60-Second Rapid Trap Drill

1. **T/F:** A father disinherits his daughter in an acknowledged public instrument (*Affidavit of Disinheritance*). *(False — must be in a valid probated will, Art. 915).*
2. **T/F:** If the disinherited heir denies the cause, he must prove his innocence. *(False — burden is on the OTHER heirs, Art. 917).*
3. **Spot the Trap:** Daughter gets an absolute presidential pardon for attempting to kill her father. Can father still disinherit her under Art. 919(1)? *(Yes — pardon erases penal liability, not personal ingratitude).*
4. **Spot the Trap:** Son is convicted of murder of a **stranger** carrying *reclusion perpetua*. Can his mother disinherit him? *(Yes — Art. 919[8] civil interdiction; victim need not be testator).*
5. **T/F:** Art. 920(1) inducement to immorality / attempt against virtue applies equally to sons and daughters. *(False — abandonment is gender-neutral; inducement/virtue is female-specific).*
6. **Quick Choice:** Father attempts to kill Mother, is **convicted**, and Mother forgives him. Child's right to disinherit Father under Art. 920(2): *Extinguished* or *Survives*? *(Survives — only Art. 920[8] without conviction is extinguished by parental reconciliation).*
7. **Quick Compute:** 1 legitimate child + surviving spouse in **pure intestacy** on ₱10M. How much each? *(₱5M each — Art. 996, Santillon v. Miranda).*
8. **T/F:** Full-blood first cousins take double the share of half-blood first cousins. *(False — double-share rule stops at siblings/nephews/nieces; Art. 1009 gives equal shares to other collaterals).*
9. **Spot the Degree:** Uncle vs. Nephew (both 3rd civil degree) in intestacy. Who wins? *(Nephew — Rank 5 beats Rank 6; proximity yields to Order).*
10. **Quick Compute:** Decedent X leaves no close heirs, only a first cousin's legitimate child (*anak ng pinsang buo*). Does the State inherit? *(No — that child is 5th degree, exact limit under Art. 1010).*
11. **Spot the Trap:** Three grandchildren represent a predeceased child vs. three grandchildren inheriting after ALL children repudiate. *Per stirpes* or *per capita*? *(Predecease = per stirpes Art. 982; All repudiate = per capita Art. 969).*
12. **Bar Update Check:** Under *Aquino v. Aquino* (2021), can a nonmarital child represent a predeceased marital parent to inherit from a legitimate uncle? *(No — Aquino relaxed Art. 992 ONLY in the direct ascendant line; collateral curtain remains).*
13. **T/F:** Saying *"I give my house equally, 1/3 each, to A, B, and C"* prevents accretion. *(False — aliquot fractions allow accretion; only physical earmarking bars it, Art. 1017).*
14. **Quick Rule:** Free portion vacancy caused by predecease, no substitute named: Representation or Accretion? *(Accretion — voluntary shares are NEVER represented).*
15. **Spot the Trap:** A fetus with a **6.5-month intrauterine life** cries after the umbilical cord is cut and dies 18 hours later. Did it acquire capacity to inherit? *(No — <7 months requires surviving 24 full hours after complete delivery, Art. 41).*
16. **Spot the Trap:** Catholic priest only prays the Rosary with dying testator (no confession) and gets ₱1M in the will. Barred under Art. 1027(1)? *(Not under confession clause, BUT class notes caution praying the Rosary may fall under "extending spiritual aid").*
17. **Quick Rule:** Testator leaves ₱10M for masses *"at Quiapo every Friday and Baclaran every Wednesday."* Does the State get ₱5M under Art. 1029? *(No — 50-50 Church-State split applies only when application is NOT specified).*
18. **Quick Rule:** Disinherited heir reconciles informally with testator (no writing) vs. non-disinherited unworthy heir reconciles informally. Which one is cured? *(Only the disinherited heir — Art. 922 allows informal bilateral reconciliation; pure unworthiness under Art. 1033 requires written or testamentary pardon).*
19. **T/F:** Presumptive death after **7 years** of ordinary absence opens succession under Art. 1043 / Art. 390. *(False — Art. 390 requires 10 years, or 5 years if >75; 4 years for danger under Art. 391).*
20. **T/F:** Public official establishment needs government approval to repudiate, but NOT to accept an unconditional legacy. *(False — Art. 1046 requires government approval for BOTH acceptance and repudiation).*
21. **T/F:** Sending a formal notarized extrajudicial demand letter by registered mail revokes a legacy of credit under Art. 936. *(False — Art. 936 requires bringing a judicial action).*
22. **Spot the Trap:** Testator leaves `"a 500-sqm commercial lot in Makati"` without owning any commercial realty at death. Valid like `"a 2024 SUV"`? *(False — generic immovables are void under Art. 941 unless immovable property of that kind exists in the estate at death).*
23. **Trap:** Testator sells the devised house to Buyer *a retro*, then exercises the right to repurchase before dying. Devise revived or revoked? *(Not revoked — Art. 957[2] expressly exempts reacquisition under pacto de retro).*
24. **Quick Compute:** A childless testator leaves `"all my estate to my relatives"` survived by his wife, a 2nd-degree sister, and a 3rd-degree nephew. Does his wife share, and can the nephew represent? *(No to both — Art. 959 excludes spouses/affines and applies strict proximity [2nd-degree sister takes 100%] without representation).*
25. **Doctrine Switch:** Only testamentary legacies exist (no compulsory heirs hurt, no donations *inter vivos*), and the estate cannot pay all legacies: apply Art. 911 *pro rata* or Art. 950 `R-P-S-E-S-A`? *(Art. 950 R-P-S-E-S-A — Art. 911 applies only when legitimes are impaired or collated donations inter vivos are involved).*
