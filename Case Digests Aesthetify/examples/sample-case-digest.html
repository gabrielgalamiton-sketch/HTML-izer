Torts & Damages: Quasi-Delict & Vicarious Liability

I. Statutory Foundations: Articles 2176 & 2180

Under Article 2176 of the Civil Code, whoever by act or omission causes damage to another, there being fault or negligence, is obliged to pay for the damage done. Such fault or negligence, if there is no pre-existing contractual relation between the parties, is called a quasi-delict.

II. Landmark Jurisprudence on Special Parental & Institutional Authority

1. St. Mary's Academy v. Carpitanos

St. Mary's Academy v. Carpitanos and Guingona, G.R. No. 143363, February 27, 2002

During a religious campaign organized by St. Mary's Academy, high school students were being transported in a vehicle driven by an enrolled minor student, James Daniel II. The jeep overturned, resulting in the death of fellow student Sherwin Carpitanos. The parents of Sherwin filed an action for damages against St. Mary's Academy and the parents of the minor driver under Article 218 of the Family Code.

The Court of Appeals held St. Mary's Academy liable for damages on the ground that it had custody and supervision over the students during an official school activity. St. Mary's Academy appealed, arguing that it was neither the owner of the vehicle nor was James Daniel II authorized to drive, and that a mechanical defect in the vehicle's steering was an intervening cause.

"Under Article 218 of the Family Code, the school, its administrators and teachers, or the individual, entity or institution engaged in child care shall have special parental authority and responsibility over the minor child while under their supervision, instruction or custody. However, for liability to attach, there must be a showing that the negligence of the school was the proximate cause of the injury."

The Supreme Court reversed the appellate court's finding of liability against the school. The Court clarified that special parental authority under Article 218 requires proximate causation between the school's lack of supervision and the resulting accident. The detached steering wheel was an unanticipated mechanical failure that constituted the immediate cause of the accident, negating the finding that lack of faculty supervision was proximate.

Quick Snapshot • G.R. 143363
Facts

A minor student drove a jeep during a school-sponsored religious activity; the steering wheel detached, the vehicle flipped, and another minor student died.

Main Issue

Is the school liable under Art. 218 of the Family Code for the student's death during an official school event?

SC Ruling

NO. For special parental liability under Art. 218 to attach, the school's negligence must be the proximate cause. The mechanical steering detachment was the proximate cause, not lack of teacher supervision.

2. Amadora v. Court of Appeals

Amadora v. Court of Appeals, G.R. No. L-47745, April 15, 1988

Alfredo Amadora was fatally shot by his classmate, Pablito Daffon, inside the auditorium of Colegio de San Jose-Recoletos. The incident occurred after classes had ended for the semester, but while the students were in school to complete graduation requirements, specifically the physics experiment report.

The trial court found the school and its rector, dean, and teachers liable under Article 2180 of the Civil Code. The Court of Appeals reversed on the ground that the academic school year had concluded, Daffon was not in physical class session, and Article 2180 applies exclusively to schools of arts and trades.

"Article 2180 of the Civil Code applies to all schools, academic as well as non-academic. The student is under the custody of the school so long as he is inside school premises for a legitimate educational reason, whether within or outside semester classes."

Justice Cruz, speaking for the Court en banc, held that custody persists as long as the student remains in school for a legitimate school activity, even after regular class hours. Furthermore, Article 2180 applies broadly to all schools. However, the teachers and school administrators were ultimately exonerated because they successfully demonstrated that they exercised the diligence of a good father of a family in maintaining campus security.

Quick Snapshot • G.R. L-47745
Facts

A graduating student was fatally shot inside the school auditorium by a classmate submitting physics requirements during post-semester clearance.

Main Issue

Does Art. 2180 apply to academic schools after regular class hours when students are finishing requirements?

SC Ruling

YES. Art. 2180 applies to all schools (academic or vocational) whenever students are on campus for legitimate purposes. However, the school was exonerated upon showing exercise of due diligence of a good father of a family.
