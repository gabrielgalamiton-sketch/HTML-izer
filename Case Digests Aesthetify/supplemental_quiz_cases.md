### [OUTPUT 1: COMPREHENSIVE ACADEMIC DIGEST]

**Trigger Words:**
Demurrer to Evidence, Double Jeopardy, Office of the Solicitor General (OSG), Private Complainant's Standing, Cyberlibel (R.A. No. 10175)

**Case Identification:**
* **Case Title:** *Francisco O. Yasa v. Mary Jean Chavez and Beberly Hoyumpa Cuasito, and People of the Philippines*
* **Citation:** G.R. No. 273948
* **Date of Promulgation:** June 23, 2026
* **Ponente:** Justice Maria Filomena D. Singh (Third Division)

**Facts of the Case:**
Respondents Mary Jean Chavez (**Mary Jean**) and Beberly Hoyumpa Cuasito (**Beberly**) were charged in separate Informations with Libel in relation to Sections 4(c)(a) [sic] and 6 of **Republic Act No. 10175** (Cybercrime Prevention Act of 2012) before the Regional Trial Court (**RTC**) of Parañaque City. Specifically, Mary Jean was charged with two counts (Criminal Case Nos. 2021-1115 and 2021-1116) and Beberly with one count (Criminal Case No. 2021-1117) for allegedly posting malicious and defamatory Facebook comments on June 28, 2020. The comments were posted on the public Facebook posts of Ma Grethel L. Rufa and Marivic J. Bautista regarding a Supreme Court First Division Notice in G.R. No. 214308 (*Francisco O. Yasa v. Hon. Fernanda Lamper Peralta, et al.*), and imputed workplace harassment and abuse against petitioner Francisco O. Yasa (**Francisco**). During arraignment under **Rule 116**, both Mary Jean and Beberly pleaded "Not Guilty." At trial, the prosecution presented Emelita dela Cruz, a co-worker who testified that respondents were union officers and that she informed Francisco of the posts, though she admitted on cross-examination that no name was identified in the comments and that earlier libel and unjust vexation cases filed by Francisco against union officers had been dismissed. Francisco also testified that he filed the cases upon learning of the comments from Emelita to protect his reputation. After the prosecution rested and formally offered its evidence, respondents filed a **Demurrer to Evidence** with leave of court, assailing the RTC's jurisdiction (akin to **Rule 117** grounds—failure of the Informations to allege whether Francisco was a public officer or private individual, where the defamations were first published, or where he resided) and arguing that the prosecution failed to prove the elements of libel. The RTC ruled that it had jurisdiction under cybercrime venue rules, but granted the **Demurrer to Evidence** and acquitted respondents because the prosecution's electronic evidence was incomplete and unauthenticated. Dissatisfied, Francisco filed a **Rule 65** Petition for *Certiorari* before the Court of Appeals (**CA**) without the conformity of the **Office of the Solicitor General (OSG)**, and later elevated the CA's dismissal to the Supreme Court via a **Rule 45** Petition.

**Parties & Contentions:**
* **Petitioner (Francisco O. Yasa):** Contends that the CA erred in dismissing his **Rule 65** Petition to set aside the RTC's grant of the Demurrer to Evidence despite sufficient proof to sustain respondents' indictment and prove their guilt beyond reasonable doubt.
* **Respondents (Mary Jean Chavez & Beberly Hoyumpa Cuasito):** Contend in their Demurrer to Evidence that the RTC lacked jurisdiction due to defective allegations in the Informations, that the facts alleged did not constitute an offense, and that the prosecution failed to prove all elements of libel or present defamatory statements.

**Procedural History:**
* **RTC (Branch 258, Parañaque City):** In a Resolution dated August 10, 2023, the RTC upheld its jurisdiction but **GRANTED** the Demurrer to Evidence and **ACQUITTED** Mary Jean and Beberly for insufficiency of evidence, finding the electronic evidence incomplete and unauthenticated.
* **CA (Twelfth Division, CA-G.R. SP No. 180612):** On October 13, 2023, the CA **DISMISSED OUTRIGHT** Francisco's Rule 65 Petition for failure to file a Motion for Reconsideration and for lack of authority to appeal an acquittal in a criminal case; it denied his Motion for Reconsideration on May 14, 2024.
* **Supreme Court:** Francisco filed a Petition for Review on *Certiorari* under **Rule 45** of the Rules of Court.

**Issues:**
1. **Primary Procedural Issue:** Whether or not a private complainant has the legal standing and authority to assail an acquittal in a criminal case—without the conformity of the **Office of the Solicitor General (OSG)**—and whether or not reviewing an acquittal based on a **Demurrer to Evidence** violates the accused's constitutional right against **double jeopardy**.
2. **Secondary/Incidental Issue:** Whether or not the RTC committed **grave abuse of discretion** amounting to lack or excess of jurisdiction in granting the Demurrer to Evidence based on incomplete and unauthenticated electronic evidence.

**Supreme Court Ruling and Ratio Decidendi:**
* **Dispositive Portion:** The Petition for Review on *Certiorari* is **DENIED**. The CA Resolutions dated October 13, 2023 and May 14, 2024 in CA-G.R. SP No. 180612 are **AFFIRMED**.
* **Legal Analysis & Ratio:**
  * *Brief Answer:* **NO.** A private complainant has no authority to appeal or assail the criminal aspect of an acquittal without the OSG's conformity, and an acquittal via a Demurrer to Evidence immediately triggers the bar against double jeopardy absent grave abuse of discretion.
  1. **Statutory / Rule Breakdown:** Under **Section 35(1), Chapter 12, Title III, Book IV of the Administrative Code of 1987**, the authority to represent the People of the Philippines in criminal appeals before the CA and the Supreme Court is vested solely in the **OSG**. Meanwhile, **Section 23, Rule 119 of the Revised Rules of Criminal Procedure** authorizes the trial court to dismiss a criminal action on the ground of insufficiency of evidence upon a demurrer to evidence filed with or without leave of court, which operates as a judgment on the merits.
  2. **Substantive / Procedural Requirements:** In criminal prosecutions, the offended party is the State, and the private complainant's interest is limited strictly to the **civil liability** (*People v. Santiago*). Here, Francisco did not raise any argument regarding civil liability; instead, he challenged the sufficiency of evidence to overturn respondents' acquittal without the OSG's conformity. Furthermore, unlike ordinary dismissals with the accused's express consent under **Rule 117, Section 7**, a dismissal via demurrer to evidence is an adjudication on the merits equivalent to an **acquittal**.
  3. **Judicial Doctrines Applied:** Applying *People v. Alapan* and *Chiok v. People*, the Court reiterated that only the OSG may prosecute criminal appeals. Applying *People v. Sandiganbayan* and *Sanvicente v. People*, the Court held that an acquittal on demurrer to evidence is protected by the **constitutional right against double jeopardy** (**Art. III, Sec. 21, 1987 Constitution**). While double jeopardy does not attach if the trial court committed **grave abuse of discretion** (such as denying the prosecution its day in court or conducting a sham trial), the RTC here allowed the prosecution to present and formally offer its evidence and judiciously evaluated it.
  4. **Legal Consequence / Relief:** Even if the RTC erred in its assessment of the electronic evidence, such an error of judgment cannot be rectified by *certiorari* because **double jeopardy** had already set in. The acquittal of Mary Jean and Beberly stands final.

### [OUTPUT 2: HIGH-YIELD RECITATION DIGEST]

* **Citation Header:** *Francisco O. Yasa v. Mary Jean Chavez, et al.* | G.R. No. 273948 | June 23, 2026 | J. Singh
* **Quick Facts:** Respondents Mary Jean Chavez and Beberly Cuasito were charged with cyberlibel under **R.A. No. 10175** and pleaded "Not Guilty" upon arraignment under **Rule 116**. After the prosecution presented its witnesses and rested, respondents filed a **Demurrer to Evidence** with leave of court, which the RTC granted, acquitting them because the prosecution's electronic evidence was incomplete and unauthenticated. Petitioner Francisco Yasa, the private complainant, filed a **Rule 65** Petition before the CA (and later a **Rule 45** Petition before the SC) without the **OSG's** conformity to overturn the acquittal. The CA dismissed the petition outright for lack of authority to appeal a criminal acquittal and failure to file a prior motion for reconsideration.
* **Core Recitation Issue:** *Whether or not a private complainant, without the conformity of the OSG, may assail an accused's acquittal granted via a Demurrer to Evidence without violating the constitutional proscription against double jeopardy.*
* **Short Ruling & Ratio:**
  * **Answer:** **NO.** Petition **DENIED**; the CA Resolutions dismissing the petition and affirming the acquittal are **AFFIRMED**.
  * **Recitation Pitch:** Under **Section 35(1), Chapter 12, Title III, Book IV of the Administrative Code of 1987**, only the **OSG** has the authority to represent the People in criminal appeals, limiting the private complainant's standing solely to the civil aspect of the case. Because Francisco sought to overturn the acquittal on the merits rather than pursue civil liability, he lacked legal standing to file the petition without the OSG's conformity. Moreover, under **Section 23, Rule 119**, the grant of a **Demurrer to Evidence** is an adjudication on the merits equivalent to an acquittal, which immediately triggers the **constitutional protection against double jeopardy** (**Art. III, Sec. 21**) unless the trial court committed grave abuse of discretion, which was absent here.
* **Recitation Doctrine / Bar Takeaway:** *For an acquittal via a Demurrer to Evidence to be set aside, the State—represented exclusively by the OSG—must establish grave abuse of discretion amounting to lack or excess of jurisdiction (e.g., denial of due process or a sham trial); otherwise, double jeopardy bars any further review, even for errors of judgment.*

# CASE 2: *People of the Philippines v. XXX271087*

### [CONFIGURATION BLOCK]
* **Subject / Course:** Criminal Procedure (in relation to Criminal Law & Evidence)

* **Governing Rule, Statute, or Provision:** Rule 116, Revised Rules of Criminal Procedure; **R.A. No. 9262** (Anti-Violence Against Women and Their Children Act of 2004), Secs. 3(c) and 26; **Revised Penal Code (RPC)**, Arts. 11 and 246; **CEDAW** General Recommendation No. 35

### [OUTPUT 1: COMPREHENSIVE ACADEMIC DIGEST]

**Trigger Words:**
Battered Woman Syndrome (BWS), Section 26 of R.A. No. 9262, Parricide (Art. 246 RPC), Cycle of Violence, Learned Helplessness

**Case Identification:**
* **Case Title:** *People of the Philippines v. XXX271087*
* **Citation:** G.R. No. 271087
* **Date of Promulgation:** July 14, 2026
* **Ponente:** Senior Associate Justice Marvic M.V.F. Leonen (Second Division)

**Facts of the Case:**
In a February 23, 2011 Information, accused-appellant **XXX271087** was charged with **Parricide** under **Article 246 of the Revised Penal Code (RPC)** for shooting her legitimate husband, **AAA271087** (a police officer), several times with a 9mm pistol on February 8, 2011, causing his immediate death. During arraignment under **Rule 116**, XXX271087 pleaded **not guilty** and subsequently interposed the defense of **battered woman syndrome (BWS)** under **Section 26 of R.A. No. 9262**. At trial, the prosecution presented AAA271087's sister-in-law (BBB271087), who testified that after hearing gunshots at the spouses' house, she saw XXX271087 holding a gun and saying *"mas gugustuhin ko nalang makulong, te,"* as well as the arresting officer (PO2 Sheryl Respicio) and the victim's mother (CCC271087). For the defense, XXX271087 testified that during their 11-year marriage, AAA271087 became unfaithful and violently abusive—beating her about four times a week, choking her, and pointing a firearm at her. On September 16, 2010, after catching AAA271087 in a hotel with another woman, she was restrained and physically assaulted, sustaining head and hand injuries documented in a medical certificate. On February 8, 2011, AAA271087 violently threw their two-year-old son onto a chair; later that night, when XXX271087 packed her clothes to leave, he threatened her life, beat her, and slammed her head against a wall, causing her vision to blur and her memory to go blank until she woke up at the police station. To corroborate her psychological state, the defense presented a court-ordered psychiatric report by government psychiatrist **Dr. Emeryl S. Layugan** and the testimony of **Dr. Ethel Maureen Pagaddu**, diagnosing XXX271087 with **Major Depressive Disorder** resulting from prolonged physical, emotional, and psychological abuse. The RTC convicted XXX271087 of parricide, and the CA affirmed, ruling that she failed to prove at least two full cycles of violence and lacked corroborating police or medical reports.

**Parties & Contentions:**
* **Accused-Appellant (XXX271087):** Argues that she suffered from **battered woman syndrome** after undergoing at least two cycles of violence, that the court-ordered psychiatric evaluation is a public document proving her psychological state, and that she acted in self-preservation and defense of her children.
* **Plaintiff-Appellee (People of the Philippines, through the OSG):** Argues that all elements of **parricide** were established beyond reasonable doubt, and that there was no collateral evidence proving two complete cycles of violence to sustain the BWS defense.

**Procedural History:**
* **RTC (Branch ___, Crim. Case No. 19-7211):** On January 21, 2020, the RTC **CONVICTED** XXX271087 of Parricide, sentencing her to *reclusion perpetua* and ordering her to pay civil indemnity (₱75,000), moral damages (₱75,000), exemplary damages (₱30,000), and temperate damages (₱25,000) with 6% legal interest.
* **CA (Fourth Division, CA-G.R. CR-HC No. 14921):** On August 9, 2023, the CA **DENIED** the appeal and **AFFIRMED WITH MODIFICATIONS** (increasing exemplary damages to ₱75,000 and temperate damages to ₱50,000).
* **Supreme Court:** XXX271087 elevated the case via a **Notice of Appeal**, with both the OSG and the Public Attorney's Office (PAO) adopting their CA briefs.

**Issues:**
1. **Primary Substantive & Evidentiary Issue:** Whether or not accused-appellant XXX271087 sufficiently proved that she was suffering from **battered woman syndrome** under **Section 26 of R.A. No. 9262**, thereby exempting her from criminal and civil liability for **parricide**.
2. **Secondary Evidentiary Issue:** Whether or not the absence of exhaustive corroborating testimonies or prior police reports negates a claim of battered woman syndrome when supported by documented injuries and expert psychiatric findings.

**Supreme Court Ruling and Ratio Decidendi:**
* **Dispositive Portion:** The appeal is **GRANTED**. The August 9, 2023 Decision of the Court of Appeals in CA-G.R. CR-HC No. 14921 is **REVERSED AND SET ASIDE**. Accused-appellant XXX271087 is **ACQUITTED** and ordered immediately **RELEASED** from detention, unless confined for some other lawful cause.
* **Legal Analysis & Ratio:**
  * *Brief Answer:* **YES.** XXX271087 proved at least two full cycles of violence and presented expert psychiatric evidence establishing BWS, entitling her to full exemption from criminal and civil liability under **Section 26 of R.A. No. 9262**.
  1. **Statutory / Rule Breakdown:** While traditional self-defense under **Article 11 of the RPC** requires unlawful aggression, reasonable necessity of the means employed, and lack of sufficient provocation, **Section 26 of R.A. No. 9262** expressly mandates that victim-survivors found suffering from **battered woman syndrome** *"do not incur any criminal and civil liability notwithstanding the absence of any of the elements for justifying circumstances of self-defense under the Revised Penal Code."*
  2. **Substantive / Procedural Requirements:** Applying the three-phase **cycle of violence** (tension-building, acute battering, and reconciliation/contrition) required to occur at least twice (*People v. Genosa*), the Court found two complete cycles: (a) the first cycle began with AAA271087's infidelity and coldness, escalated into the September 16, 2010 hotel assault (corroborated by a medical certificate), and was followed by continued cohabitation representing a temporary calm/reconciliation; and (b) the second cycle involved over two years of continued beatings behind closed doors, culminating in the February 8, 2011 violent confrontation where he threw their child and slammed her head against a wall.
  3. **Judicial Doctrines Applied:** The Court applied the **Cycle of Violence** and **Learned Helplessness** doctrines from *People v. Genosa*, as expanded by **Section 26 of R.A. No. 9262**, alongside the Philippines' treaty obligations under **CEDAW General Recommendation No. 35**, which prohibits courts from applying stereotypical notions or unrealistic evidentiary standards against victims of gender-based violence. As mandated by Section 26, the Court relied on the expert findings of Dr. Layugan and Dr. Pagaddu, who bridged the causal link between the husband's abuse and XXX271087's Major Depressive Disorder and desperation.
  4. **Legal Consequence / Relief:** The CA's demand for exhaustive police reports or corroboration for every prior beating ignored the realities of domestic abuse—especially where the abuser was a police officer. Finding that XXX271087 suffered from BWS at the time of the shooting, the Court **ACQUITTED** her of parricide and exempted her from both criminal and civil liability.

### [OUTPUT 2: HIGH-YIELD RECITATION DIGEST]

* **Citation Header:** *People of the Philippines v. XXX271087* | G.R. No. 271087 | July 14, 2026 | S.A.J. Leonen
* **Quick Facts:** Charged with **parricide** (**Art. 246, RPC**) for fatally shooting her police-officer husband, XXX271087 pleaded not guilty upon arraignment (**Rule 116**) and interposed **battered woman syndrome (BWS)**. She testified to years of infidelity and beatings—including a 2010 hotel assault documented by a medical certificate and a 2011 confrontation where he threw their child and slammed her head against a wall—supported by a court-ordered psychiatric diagnosis of **Major Depressive Disorder**. Both the RTC and CA convicted her, ruling that she failed to prove two cycles of violence and lacked corroborating police reports or witness accounts.
* **Core Recitation Issue:** *Whether or not an accused charged with parricide who proves at least two cycles of domestic violence and presents expert psychiatric testimony diagnosing her with abuse-induced Major Depressive Disorder is exempt from criminal and civil liability under Section 26 of R.A. No. 9262 despite the absence of prior police reports.*
* **Short Ruling & Ratio:**
  * **Answer:** **YES.** Appeal **GRANTED**; CA Decision **REVERSED AND SET ASIDE**; accused-appellant **ACQUITTED**.
  * **Recitation Pitch:** Under **Section 26 of R.A. No. 9262**, victim-survivors suffering from **battered woman syndrome** incur no criminal or civil liability even in the absence of any of the elements of traditional self-defense under **Article 11 of the RPC**. Here, the totality of the evidence—the documented 2010 hotel assault, continued cohabitation, ongoing abuse, the violent February 2011 confrontation, and the court-appointed psychiatrists' findings of Major Depressive Disorder and learned helplessness—sufficiently established at least two full cycles of violence. Consistent with **CEDAW General Recommendation No. 35**, courts cannot demand an impossible standard of exhaustive corroboration or prior police reports, especially when the abuser is a police officer.
* **Recitation Doctrine / Bar Takeaway:** *For Battered Woman Syndrome under Section 26 of R.A. No. 9262 to exempt an accused from criminal and civil liability, the totality of evidence—assisted by expert psychiatric or psychological testimony—must establish at least two full cycles of violence resulting in learned helplessness, without requiring exhaustive police or third-party corroboration of every prior assault.*
