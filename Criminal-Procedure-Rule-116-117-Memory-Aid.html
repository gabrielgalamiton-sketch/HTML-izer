1. Arraignment & Plea Mechanics (Rule 116)

Memory Anchor — Why Arraignment Matters: Operationalizes Art. III, Sec. 14(2) (right to be informed). No valid arraignment & plea = issues not joined → trial is void ab initio → no double jeopardy attaches. Recall the 3 courtroom duties: C–L–R (Counsel, Language, Refusal to plead).
A. Right to Counsel & Counsel de Officio Rule 116, Secs. 6, 7 & 8
General Rule An accused cannot be forced to plead without counsel. If unrepresented, the judge has a mandatory affirmative duty to: (1) inform accused of the right to counsel; (2) ask if he desires counsel of choice (granting reasonable time if he can afford one); or (3) appoint a counsel de officio (routinely PAO) and give sufficient time to confer, inspect records, and examine the Information before pleading (Sec. 8). Instantaneous plea = reversible error.
Exception The accused may be allowed to defend himself in person upon a showing of capacity to do so.
B. Language, Translation & Qualifying Circumstances Rule 116, Sec. 1(a)
General Rule The Information must be read in open court in a language or dialect known to and understood by the accused. Translation must explain legal terms of art—such as treachery (defenseless attack without risk to offender) and evident premeditation (deliberate, cold-blooded planning) in Murder (Art. 248, RPC), and culpable lack of care in quasi-offenses (Art. 365, RPC). Mere nodding to an English reading = no valid arraignment.
Efficiency Practice Courts may direct the prosecutor, clerk, or interpreter to read the Information directly in the chosen vernacular (avoiding double English-then-vernacular reading), followed by an explicit on-record inquiry confirming the accused understood before receiving the plea.
C. Refusal to Plead & Plea Bargaining to a Lesser Offense Rule 116, Sec. 1(c) & Sec. 1(f)
General Rule (Refusal) When the accused stands mute, refuses to plead, or makes a conditional plea, a plea of "Not Guilty" shall automatically be entered for him (Sec. 1[c]). Silence never admits guilt; the State retains the burden of proof beyond reasonable doubt.
General Rule (Plea Bargain) Pleading guilty to a lesser included offense requires the joint, concurrent consent of BOTH the public prosecutor AND the private offended party (Sec. 1[f]). Without both, the plea is irregular and does not bar a second prosecution (Rule 117, Sec. 7[c]).
Exception (Plea Bargain) The private offended party’s presence or consent may be dispensed with if the offended party failed to appear at the arraignment despite due notice (court + fiscal may proceed validly, and double jeopardy attaches).
D. Grounds to Suspend or Postpone Arraignment (Mnemonic: U–P–P–P–B) Rule 116, Sec. 11 & Sec. 9
General Rule Arraignment proceeds on schedule; the court is not bound to grant resets for mere counsel convenience. "Failure to furnish an advance physical copy of the Information" is NOT a valid ground (records are public and the Information is read in open court).
Exceptions (5 Exclusive Grounds — U-P-P-P-B)
  1. Unsound mental condition rendering accused unable to understand/plead intelligently (court orders psychiatric exam/confinement);
  2. Prejudicial question in a pending civil action determinative of criminal liability;
  3. Pending Petition for Review at the DOJ or Office of the President (suspension not to exceed 60 days from filing);
  4. Pending Motion for Regular Preliminary Investigation in inquest cases (Rule 112, Sec. 6) invoked within the 5-day reglementary period from learning of filing;
  5. Motion for Bill of Particulars (Rule 116, Sec. 9) to clarify vague/ambiguous averments prior to plea.
E. Motions to Quash in Summary Procedure vs. Regular Criminal Procedure Sec. 19, Sum. Proc. vs. Rules of Court
General Rule (Summary) Under Section 19 of the Revised Rules on Summary Procedure (penalty ≤ 6 months imprisonment or fine), a Motion to Quash is a PROHIBITED pleading (unlike regular criminal cases where all 9 grounds under Rule 117, Sec. 3 are allowed).
Exceptions (Only 2 Allowed in Summary Procedure)
  1. Lack of jurisdiction over the subject matter (offense); or
  2. Failure to comply with a condition precedent — specifically mandatory prior Barangay Conciliation (R.A. 7160).
Crucial Procedural Trap (Barangay Conciliation): In Summary Procedure, the court may dismiss motu proprio at any stage if no Certification to File Action is attached. In Regular Cases, the court cannot dismiss motu proprio; failure to object before plea constitutes an irrevocable waiver.

2. Motion to Quash: Timing, Waiver & Comparisons (Rule 117)

Formal Requisites (Rule 117, Sec. 2): Must be (1) In Writing (no oral MTQ), (2) Signed by accused or counsel, (3) Distinctly Specifying factual & legal grounds, and (4) Set for Hearing with at least 3 days prior notice (Rule 15, Sec. 4).
A. Pre-Plea Mandate & Waiver Rule (Mnemonic for Non-Waivable: F–J–E–D) Rule 117, Secs. 1 & 9
General Rule A Motion to Quash must be filed BEFORE the accused enters his plea (Sec. 1). Once a plea is entered, all grounds not invoked are deemed WAIVED (Sec. 9)—curing grounds (c), (d), (e), (f), and (h).
Exceptions (4 Non-Waivable Grounds — F-J-E-D) May be raised at any stage (even on appeal) or dismissed motu proprio:
  1. Facts charged do not constitute an offense — Sec. 3(a)
  2. Lack of Jurisdiction over the offense charged — Sec. 3(b)
  3. Extinction of criminal action or liability (e.g., prescription) — Sec. 3(g)
  4. Double Jeopardy (prior conviction, acquittal, or termination without consent) — Sec. 3(i)
B. Legal Effects of Granting or Denying a Motion to Quash Rule 117, Secs. 4–6
General Rule (Grant) Granting a Motion to Quash is NOT an acquittal; the State may amend or refile. Specifically under Sec. 3(a) (facts don't constitute offense) and Sec. 3(e) (form), the court shall not immediately dismiss—it must first allow the prosecution to amend the Information (Sec. 4).
Exceptions (Permanent Bar on Grant) Quashal bars another prosecution permanently ONLY when grounded on Extinction / Prescription (Sec. 3[g]) or Double Jeopardy (Sec. 3[i]) (or when prosecution fails/cannot cure a Sec. 3[a] defect via amendment).
General Rule (Denial) An order denying a Motion to Quash is interlocutory and cannot be immediately appealed; the accused must plead and proceed to trial.
Exception (Denial) Extraordinary Petition for Certiorari (Rule 65) lies if the court acted with patent lack of jurisdiction or grave abuse of discretion.
C. Extrinsic Matters EXCLUDED from a Rule 117 Motion to Quash Rule 112 vs. Rule 110 vs. Rule 117
General Rule (No PI / No Cert.) Absence of Preliminary Investigation or omission of the fiscal’s certification (Rule 112, Sec. 4) is NOT a ground to quash. Validity of the Information depends on Rule 110, Secs. 6 & 8, and upon filing, the court acquires full control (Crespo v. Mogul).
→ Proper Remedy: File a Motion to Suspend Proceedings and Remand for preliminary investigation prior to plea (or direct fiscal to supply oath certification).
General Rule (Desistance) An Affidavit of Desistance is NOT a ground to quash (crime is against the People; complainant is merely a State witness; private compromise cannot extinguish public criminal liability as a matter of right).
→ Practical Effect: Operates on prosecutor/court discretion via a prosecutor’s Motion to Dismiss or dismissal for insufficiency of evidence if witness refuses to testify.
Distinction (Judicial Probable Cause) Under Rule 112, Sec. 5, the judge independently evaluates supporting evidence within 10 days from filing and may dismiss motu proprio for lack of judicial probable cause (e.g., P.D. 1602 gambling complaint lacking operator identity/bet proof). This is a Rule 112 dismissal, NOT a Rule 117 quashal.

(a) Facts do not constitute an offense NO
(F-J-E-D)		 Test: Hypothetical Admission + Four Corners (no extraneous proof).
GR: Order amendment first (Sec. 4). EXC: Quash if fiscal refuses/fails/cannot cure.
(b) Lack of jurisdiction over offense NO
(F-J-E-D)		 Test: Allegations in Info (territory + statutory penalty: MTC ≤ 6 yrs / BP 22 / Summary; RTC > 6 yrs).

(c) Lack of jurisdiction over person YES Acquired by: Lawful arrest (warrant / Rule 113, Sec. 5) or voluntary surrender/appearance.

(d) Officer had no authority to file YES
(Sec. 9 matrix)		 Assistant prosecutor needs prior written approval of Provincial/City Prosecutor (or Ombudsman/Special Prosecutor); bound by territorial office limits. Lack of authority = absolute nullity (not cured by post-plea approval).
(e) Non-conformity to prescribed form YES Test: Substantial compliance with Rule 110, Secs. 6–12.
GR: Remedy is amendment (Sec. 4), rarely fatal dismissal.
(f) Duplicity (More than 1 offense) YES GR: Info must charge only one offense (Rule 110, Sec. 13). If waived by plea, court may convict for ALL proved offenses & impose separate penalties.
EXC: (1) Complex crimes (Art. 48, RPC); (2) Quasi-offenses (Art. 365, RPC).
(g) Extinction / Prescription NO
(F-J-E-D)		 Art. 89/90 RPC; Act 3326 (special laws); death, service, amnesty, pardon, prescription. Substantive bar; permanently bars refiling.
(h) Legal excuse or justification YES GR: Affirmative defenses (e.g., self-defense) require trial proof aliunde.
EXC: Quashable ONLY if justifying (Art. 11), exempting (Art. 12[4] accident), or absolutory cause (Art. 247) appears expressly on the face of the Information.
(i) Double Jeopardy NO
(F-J-E-D)		 Art. III, Sec. 21, Const.; prior conviction, acquittal, or dismissal without express consent for same offense; permanently bars refiling.
High-Yield Exam Traps in Grounds (c), (f), and (h) Rule 117, Sec. 3(c), (f) & (h)
Trap 1: Illegal Arrest Entering a plea cures lack of jurisdiction over the person (Sec. 3[c]), BUT DOES NOT validate an unlawful incidental search. Under Art. III, Sec. 3(2), confiscated items remain inadmissible, leading to acquittal for insufficiency of evidence.
Trap 2: Duplicity Waiver Silence before plea on an Information charging Theft + Estafa (Sec. 3[f]) authorizes the trial judge to impose two separate convictions and penalties if both are proven at trial.
Trap 3: Battered Woman Syndrome (BWS) While recognized under R.A. 9262 and People v. Genosa, BWS normally requires psychiatric expert testimony and proof of a cycle of violence—so it is rarely established on the face of an Information under Sec. 3(h) and must be proven at trial.

A. Finality of Acquittal & Appellate Standing (Yasa v. Chavez) G.R. No. 273948 (June 23, 2021)
General Rule A judgment of acquittal is immediately final, executory, and unappealable (no 15-day period; no motion for reconsideration). A private complainant lacks legal standing to appeal or file Rule 65 certiorari to reverse a criminal acquittal or a Demurrer dismissal.
Exceptions
  1. Criminal Aspect (Rule 65 Certiorari): May be filed exclusively by the Office of the Solicitor General (OSG) ONLY upon proof of grave abuse of discretion amounting to lack/excess of jurisdiction or denial of due process (e.g., sham/mock trial)—never mere errors of law or evidence appreciation.
  2. Civil Aspect: The private offended party HAS standing to appeal solely the civil liability (restitution/damages); double jeopardy does not apply to civil obligations.
B. Termination "Without Express Consent" of the Accused 4th Requisite of Jeopardy
General Rule If the case is dismissed upon motion or with the express consent of the accused, double jeopardy does NOT attach and the State may refile.
Exceptions (2 Dismissals on Defense Motion = Acquittal & Jeopardy)
  1. Grant of Demurrer to Evidence (Rule 119, Sec. 23) — adjudication on merits of insufficiency of proof;
  2. Dismissal for Violation of Constitutional Right to Speedy Trial — repeated, unjustified prosecution delays across settings.
C. Single Act Violating Distinct Statutes vs. Quasi-Offenses (Ivler Doctrine) Ivler v. Modesto-San Pedro (G.R. No. 172716)
General Rule (Distinct Statutes) When a single intentional act violates two distinct penal statutes, double jeopardy does NOT attach if each statute requires proof of an element the other does not (Same Evidence / Different Elements Test) — e.g., Estafa (Art. 315) + B.P. 22; Estafa + Illegal Recruitment (R.A. 8042).
Exception / Special Rule (Art. 365 Quasi-Offenses — Ivler) Ivler v. Modesto-San Pedro (Nov. 17, 2010): Reckless imprudence under Art. 365, RPC is a single, indivisible crime penalizing negligence itself, NOT the resulting harms. Art. 48 (Complex Crimes) does NOT apply to Art. 365. Prior plea/conviction for a lesser result (e.g., slight physical injuries) completely bars prosecution for graver consequences (e.g., homicide) from the same mishap.
D. Statutory Exceptions (S–F–P) & Non-Judicial Proceedings Rule 117, Sec. 7
General Rule (Inclusion) Prior conviction bars another prosecution for any offense that necessarily includes the offense charged in the former Information.
Exceptions (3 Cases Under Sec. 7 — S-F-P)
  1. Supervening Event (Sec. 7[a]): Graver offense developed from supervening facts (e.g., victim dies after assault conviction—State must prove direct medical causation, no intervening cause);
  2. Facts Discovered After Plea (Sec. 7[b]): Facts existed earlier but were unknown and undiscoverable with due diligence until after plea;
  3. Plea to Lesser Offense Without Consent (Sec. 7[c]): Plea entered without joint consent of prosecutor & offended party (Exception to exception: offended party absent despite due notice under Rule 116, Sec. 1[f]).
Non-Applicable Forums: Double jeopardy never attaches in Preliminary Investigation dismissals (executive probable cause; refilable) or Administrative Proceedings (substantial evidence vs. beyond reasonable doubt).

5. Provisional Dismissal, Finality & Pre-Trial (Rules 117–120)

A. Provisional Dismissal & The Time-Bar Clock Rule 117, Sec. 8
General Rule A valid provisional dismissal requires (1) express, affirmative consent of accused (silence ≠ consent) AND (2) actual notice to the offended party. It temporarily clears the docket, allows cancellation of the bail bond, and may be revived within the statutory period exclusively by a motion signed by the Public Prosecutor (private complainant has NO standing to revive, though approved civil compromises may be enforced by Writ of Execution).
Time-Bar Exception (Automatic Permanent Bar) Once the statutory window lapses without a valid prosecutor's motion to revive, the dismissal becomes PERMANENT (analogous to double jeopardy):
  • One (1) Year: Offenses punishable by imprisonment ≤ 6 years or fine of any amount (MTC/First-Level Courts);
  • Two (2) Years: Offenses punishable by imprisonment > 6 years (RTC/Second-Level Courts).
Defense Alert: Never consent to provisional dismissal after repeated prosecution witness no-shows—object and demand absolute dismissal for violation of Constitutional Right to Speedy Trial (instant acquittal + double jeopardy)!
B. Mandatory Pre-Trial, Corpus Delicti & Finality Periods Rule 118, Secs. 1–2 & Rule 120, Sec. 6
General Rule (Pre-Trial) Pre-trial is mandatory after a Not Guilty plea (plea bargaining, stipulations, exhibit marking, admissibility waivers, witness limits). In Murder (Art. 248, RPC), corpus delicti is proven by Certificate of Death + Medico-Legal Autopsy Report + medical examiner testimony (not the corpse), SOCO photos, receipts (Arts. 2199 & 2206 CC), and Judicial Affidavits.
Mandatory Safeguard (Rule 118, Sec. 2) Pre-trial agreements or admissions CANNOT be used in evidence against the accused UNLESS reduced to writing and signed by BOTH the accused AND his counsel.
Finality Periods Conviction (Rule 120, Sec. 6): Final after 15 days from promulgation.
Final Dismissal Order (e.g., Prescription): Final after 15 days from receipt.
Acquittal: NO waiting period—immediately final, executory & unappealable.

6. 60-Second Rapid Quiz Drill & Exam Admin Reminders

Test Yourself (High-Yield Bar & Quiz Traps):

  1. Defense moves to reset arraignment because counsel was not furnished a physical copy of the Information. Grant or deny?
  2. Information omits the investigating fiscal’s certification and no preliminary investigation was held. Should the court grant a Motion to Quash?
  3. Accused was illegally arrested without a warrant, entered a plea of Not Guilty, and now objects to the gun seized during that arrest. Is the gun admissible?
  4. Accused pleaded guilty to Reckless Imprudence Resulting in Slight Physical Injuries and received public censure. Can the State prosecute him under a separate Information for Reckless Imprudence Resulting in Homicide from the same crash?
  5. Accused files a Demurrer to Evidence without leave of court, and the judge denies it. May accused present his alibi witness?
  6. After an acquittal, the private complainant files a Petition for Certiorari (Rule 65) in the Court of Appeals seeking to convict the accused. Will it prosper?
  7. Prosecutor fails to present any witness across 5 settings and moves for provisional dismissal. What should defense counsel do?
Rapid Answers:
  1. Deny. Not a valid ground under Rule 116, Sec. 11; court records are public and Information is read in open court.
  2. No. Not a ground under Rule 117, Sec. 3 (Crespo v. Mogul; valid under Rule 110, Secs. 6 & 8). Proper remedy = Motion to Suspend & Remand for PI (or direct fiscal to supply certification).
  3. Inadmissible. Plea waived jurisdiction over person (Sec. 3[c]), but did NOT waive the Exclusionary Rule (Art. III, Sec. 3[2]; fruit of poisonous tree).
  4. No — barred by Double Jeopardy. Ivler v. Modesto-San Pedro: Art. 365 is a single indivisible quasi-offense; Art. 48 does not apply.
  5. No. Filing Demurrer without leave waives the right to present defense evidence; case is submitted for judgment on prosecution evidence alone.
  6. No. Yasa v. Chavez: Only the OSG may challenge criminal acquittals via Rule 65 (for grave abuse/sham trial); private complainant may appeal only the civil aspect.
  7. Object vigorously to provisional dismissal and move for absolute dismissal for violation of Constitutional Right to Speedy Trial (operates as acquittal + double jeopardy).
