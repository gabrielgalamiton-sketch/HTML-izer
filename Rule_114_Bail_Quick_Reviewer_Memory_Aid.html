1. Constitutional Anchor, Custody & Detainee Control

Constitutional Right to Bail & Suspension of Habeas CorpusArt. III §13 • Rule 114 §§4, 7
GENERAL RULE All persons in custody are entitled to bail before conviction as a matter of right, rooted in the presumption of innocence to ensure trial appearance. The right to bail shall not be impaired even when the privilege of the writ of habeas corpus is suspended, and excessive bail shall not be required.
EXCEPTION(S) Persons charged with a capital offense, or an offense punishable by reclusion perpetua or life imprisonment, when evidence of guilt is strong (Sec. 7).
Custody of the Law Requirement vs. Motion to Quash WarrantPaderanga v. CA • Miranda v. Tuliao
GENERAL RULE An applicant for bail must be in actual physical custody of the law—via arrest or personal voluntary surrender in court (which also counts as a mitigating circumstance if made before arrest). Counsel cannot post bail while the accused is hiding or abroad.
EXCEPTION(S) / DISTINCTION
  • Constructive Custody Suffices (Paderanga v. CA): A hospitalized accused who submits to the court's authority and signifies intent to post bail is under constructive custody; court staff may process the undertaking, fingerprints, and forms at the hospital.
  • Motion to Quash Arrest Warrant (Miranda v. Tuliao): Custody of the law is NOT required to challenge a warrant's validity; jurisdiction over the person suffices.
Who Furnishes Bail, Duration, Transfers & Multiple ChargesRule 114 §§1, 2, 3
GENERAL RULE 1. Who Furnishes (§1): The accused furnishes bail personally or through a bondsman/surety—including defense counsel (refund goes strictly to the payor named in the receipt, and the lawyer assumes forfeiture risk as jailer).
2. Duration (§2): Effective upon approval until RTC judgment promulgation (unless cancelled).
3. Detention Lock (§3): No detained person shall be released or transferred (e.g., provincial jail to BJMP) without court authority.
EXCEPTION(S)
  • Release/Transfer Allowed (§3): Only (1) upon order of the court (including discretionary escorted leave to attend a parent's wake) or (2) when admitted to bail.
  • Multiple Charges Rule: Posting bail on a bailable charge will NOT release the accused if detained for another non-bailable offense (release orders read: "unless detained for any other legal cause or causes").

2. Four Forms (Ca-Su-Pro-Re) & Special Forums (M-E-D)

Mnemonic for Forms of Bail (Sec. 1): Ca–Su–Pro–Re  |  Practice Checklist (OCA Cir. 48-2026): 2×2 name-tag photos (front, left, right), barangay certification of residence, residence sketch, signed undertaking, fingerprints, personal info form, and incidental fees (~₱500+).

Form Mechanics & Financial Rule Key Risk / Statutory Basis
Cash Deposit
Sec. 14		 Most common. Full bail deposited via JEPS (JudEx) or manual cash; refundable to payor on final disposition. Refund goes strictly to the receipt payor; court must accept manual cash if JEPS glitches.
Corporate Surety
Sec. 10		 Issued by a surety company accredited by the Supreme Court. Pay only a percentage premium. Premium is non-refundable and must be renewed yearly until case disposal.
Property Bond
Secs. 11–13		 Real property posted as security; requires extensive forms and Register of Deeds annotation on title/tax dec. Hard to sell while encumbered; sold at public auction if forfeited.
Recognizance
Sec. 15		 Release to own custody or a responsible citizen (e.g., barangay official, lawyer) without cash/bond to decongest jails. Applies under R.A. 6036 / R.A. 10389 (indigents; ≤6 mos. / ₱2,000 fine), Sec. 16, Sec. 24 (probation), CICL (R.A. 9344), and Summary Procedure non-appearance.
Payment of Cash Bail via JEPS (JudEx)Sec. 14 • OCA JEPS Circulars
GENERAL RULE Cash bail bonds are paid electronically through the Supreme Court's Judiciary Electronic Payment Solution (JEPS) (treated as an optional online transaction under OCA JEPS guidelines).
EXCEPTION(S) If JEPS suffers a glitch or downtime, the court must accept direct manual cash payment and issue an official receipt—technical failures cannot delay the constitutional right to bail.
Special Proceedings: Military, Extradition & Deportation (M–E–D)A.M. No. 22-03-29-SC • Comendador
GENERAL RULE The constitutional right to bail under Art. III §13 does NOT apply as a matter of right in:
  • Military Court-Martial (Comendador v. De Villa): Bail is not allowed due to military discipline/national security; safeguard is speedy trial.
  • Extradition (A.M. No. 22-03-29-SC, Rule 3 §1): Sui generis treaty proceeding; bail is not a right.
  • Deportation: Aliens in deportation proceedings have no right to bail.
EXCEPTION(S)
  • Military in Civilian Courts: When soldiers are prosecuted before civilian courts (e.g., RTC for coup d'état), Rule 114 applies.
  • Extradition Discretionary Standard: Extraditee may obtain bail by proving by clear and convincing evidence they are (1) not a flight risk and (2) will abide by court orders.
  • Deportation Discretion: The Commissioner of Immigration may grant bail discretionarily.
XPN TO XPN (EXTRADITION) Bail is strictly prohibited if the extraditee is sought for service of a sentence or has been convicted awaiting sentence (A.M. No. 22-03-29-SC). Political crimes and non-treaty states are generally non-extraditable.

3. Bail Not Required (Fi-Su-De) & Bail Prohibited

When No Warrant Issues & No Bail Is Required (Fi–Su–De)Rule 112 §5(c) • A.M. No. 08-8-7-SC • Sec. 16
GENERAL RULE No warrant of arrest issues—and thus no bail is required—when the charge is:
  • Fine Only: Offense penalized by a fine only (Rule 112 §5[c] — court issues summons);
  • Summary Procedure: Covered by A.M. No. 08-8-7-SC, Rule III (Expedited Procedures in First-Level Courts); or
  • Destierro: Penalty is banishment only (e.g., concubine only in concubinage under Art. 334 RPC; the husband faces prision correccional).
EXCEPTION(S) (In–Fa) Bail (or recognizance) becomes required if the accused: (1) is caught in flagrante delicto and held for inquest, OR (2) fails to appear despite notice when required by the court.
Mandatory Release After Preventive ImprisonmentRule 114 §16
GENERAL RULE An accused unable to post bail remains in preventive custody during trial and appeal.
EXCEPTION(S) (MANDATORY IMMEDIATE RELEASE)
  • Custody has lasted equal to or longer than the possible maximum imprisonment for the offense charged → immediate release (trial/appeal continues).
  • Maximum imposable penalty is destierro → release after 30 days of preventive imprisonment.
Bail After Final Judgment & Service of SentenceRule 114 §24
GENERAL RULE No bail shall be allowed after a judgment of conviction has become final.
EXCEPTION(S) If the accused applies for probation before finality, they may be allowed temporary liberty under existing bail (or released on recognizance if none was filed or they are incapable of filing one).
XPN TO XPN (ABSOLUTE BAR) In NO case shall bail be allowed after the accused has commenced to serve sentence (Sec. 24).

First-Level & Pre-Conviction RTC Bail vs. Reduction & Capital PetitionsSecs. 4, 7, 8
GENERAL RULE Bail is a matter of right (no motion/hearing required) in all first-level court cases (before or after conviction) and in RTC cases before conviction for offenses not punishable by death, reclusion perpetua, or life imprisonment.
EXCEPTION(S)
  • Motion for Reduction of Bail: Always discretionary (even in MTC); copy-furnish the public prosecutor for comment/no objection.
  • Capital / RP / Life Offenses (Secs. 7–8): Requires a Petition for Bail and a mandatory summary hearing where the prosecution bears the burden to show evidence of guilt is strong; the order must contain a summary and evaluation of the evidence.
Post-Conviction Bail Pending Appeal & Where to FileSec. 5 • Leviste v. CA
GENERAL RULE 1. Discretion (Sec. 5): Upon RTC conviction of an offense not punishable by death/RP/life, bail pending appeal is discretionary (and court may increase the bond).
2. Filing Court (Sec. 5 par. 2): May be filed with and resolved by the trial court despite a notice of appeal, provided records have not yet been transmitted to the appellate court.
EXCEPTION(S) & TWO-STAGE TEST (LEVISTE)
  • Non-Bailable to Bailable Downgrade (Sec. 5 par. 2): If conviction changed the offense from non-bailable to bailable (e.g., charged Murder → convicted Homicide), bail can ONLY be filed with and resolved by the appellate court, even if records are still in the RTC.
  • Two-Stage Test for Penalty > 6 Years (Leviste v. CA):
    Stage 1 (Bail-Negating Check — R–E–P–F–U): If prosecution proves accused is a (R)ecidivist/quasi/habitual/reiteracion, (E)scaped/violated bail, acted on (P)robation/parole/pardon, poses (F)light risk, or (U)ndue risk of another crime → MUST deny or cancel.
    Stage 2: Even if NONE are present, bail is never automatic; court exercises discretion with grave caution because presumption of innocence is gone.
Fixing Bail Amount (Sec. 9) & Remedy Against Denial (Rule 65)Sec. 9 • Enrile • DOJ Circulars
GENERAL RULE 1. Amount (Sec. 9): Judges fix reasonable bail using 10 factors—Offense (nature, penalty, evidence weight), Accused (financial ability, character, age & health per Enrile v. Sandiganbayan), and Flight (appearance probability, prior bail forfeitures, fugitive status, other bail cases).
2. Final Rulings: Final judgments/orders are challenged by ordinary appeal.
EXCEPTION(S) / QUALIFICATION
  • DOJ Bail Guides Are Recommendatory: DOJ DC No. 013-2018 (₱6k/yr standard RPC; ₱2k/yr certain theft tiers; ₱10k/yr prioritized special laws like R.A. 9165/7610/10591) and DOJ DC No. 011-2023 (indigent 50% reduction / ₱10k cap) do not bind judges.
  • Remedy from Bail Denial = Rule 65, NOT Appeal: A bail order is interlocutory; challenge grave abuse of discretion via Certiorari under Rule 65 (RTC → CA; Sandiganbayan → SC).

5. Where to File (Sec. 17), Forfeiture (Sec. 21) & Pre-Plea (Sec. 26)

Scenario (Rule 114 §17) Proper Court(s) Hierarchy / Limit
1. Case Pending; Judge Available (§17[a]) Court where the case is pending Default venue.
2. Case Pending; Judge Absent (§17[a]) Any RTC, MeTC, MTCC, MTC, or MCTC judge in the same province, city, or municipality Never cross provincial lines (e.g., La Union → Pangasinan = gross ignorance).
3. Arrested Elsewhere (§17[a]) Any RTC of place of arrest → if unavailable, any MeTC/MTCC/MTC/MCTC therein Strict hierarchy (RTC first, then lower courts).
4. In Custody, Not Yet Charged (§17[c]) Any court in the province, city, or municipality where held No hierarchy required (e.g., inquest arrests).
5. Discretionary Bail / Reduction / Recognizance (§17[b]) ONLY the court where the case is pending Outside courts cannot reduce bail; must collect full fixed amount.
Where to File Bail & The Reduction LockRule 114 §17(a)–(c)
GENERAL RULE Bail must be filed in the court where the case is pending (§17[a]).
EXCEPTION(S) May be filed elsewhere if (1) the judge is absent/unavailable (any judge within the same province/city/municipality), (2) the accused is arrested outside the court's territory (RTC first, then lower court), or (3) the person is not yet charged (any court where held, §17[c]).
XPN TO XPN (§17[b] LOCK) When bail is discretionary (including reduction of bail) or the accused seeks recognizance, it may ONLY be filed in the court where the case is pending.
Trial Presence & Mandatory Two-Step Forfeiture (F–30–J–15–E)Sec. 21 • Mendoza v. Alarma
GENERAL RULE 1. Presence: After arraignment, the accused's trial attendance is a waivable personal right.
2. Forfeiture: Upon unexcused non-appearance when required, the court summarily declares the bail forfeited (conditional/interlocutory) and issues an arrest warrant.
EXCEPTION(S) & DUE PROCESS PREREQUISITES
  • Mandatory Attendance: Presence is required at arraignment, pre-trial, promulgation, and when ordered for eyewitness identification. (No trial in absentia if not yet arraigned.)
  • No Immediate Execution (Mendoza v. Alarma): Court cannot execute directly from an Order of Forfeiture. It must: (1) give bondsman 30 days to produce the living body + show cause; (2) render a separate Judgment on the Bond; and (3) wait 15 days for finality before issuing a Writ of Execution (cash to Treasury / property auction).
Effect of Bail on Objections to Arrest & Preliminary InvestigationSec. 26 • Borlongan v. Peña
GENERAL RULE An application for or admission to bail shall NOT bar the accused from challenging (1) the validity of their arrest or warrant, or (2) the lack or irregularity of a preliminary investigation.
EXCEPTION(S) (WHEN WAIVED) Those objections are waived if the accused fails to raise them before entering a plea (arraignment) and voluntarily pleads or actively participates in trial (Borlongan v. Peña). Practice Rule: Never allow arraignment before bail or pre-plea motions.

  2. Maria is charged with Murder, granted pre-trial bail, then convicted of Homicide (8–14 years) and appeals. Can the same RTC grant her bail pending appeal before records are transmitted?
  3. Pedro jumps bail at arraignment; after 45 days without producing him, the court immediately auctions his uncle's land based on the Order of Forfeiture. Valid?
  4. After Pedro is arrested, he posts cash bail and immediately moves to quash the Information for lack of preliminary investigation before arraignment. Barred?

Answers:
1. Dagupan MTCC may accept bail only if Dagupan RTC judges are unavailable (§17[a]), but it CANNOT reduce bail—only the La Union RTC where the case is pending can reduce it (§17[b]).
2. No. Because conviction changed the offense from non-bailable (Murder) to bailable (Homicide), only the Court of Appeals may grant bail (§5 par. 2), applying Leviste discretion.
3. No (Void). A separate Judgment on the Bond + 15-day finality must precede execution (§21; Mendoza v. Alarma).
4. No (Not barred). Bail does not waive pre-plea objections raised before entering a plea (§26; Borlongan v. Peña).
