1. Rule 115 Sequence, Waiver & Presumption of Innocence

Mnemonic for Rule 115, Section 1(a)–(i): P–I–P • T–S • C–C • S–A (Trap Fix: Self-incrimination is 1(e); Confrontation is 1(f)!)

Waiver by Silence & Presumption of InnocenceRule 115 §1(a) • Art. III §14(2)
GENERAL RULE Rule 115 rights are personal and must be asserted promptly on the record; remaining silent during repeated postponements weakens a speedy-trial objection. Under §1(a), the prosecution bears the burden of proof (offense elements + identity) beyond reasonable doubt; a weak defense cannot cure weak prosecution evidence.
EXCEPTION(S) / QUALIFICATION
  • Silence ≠ Automatic Waiver: Failure to object earlier does not create a rigid permanent waiver; courts still apply the 4-factor balancing test (length, reason, assertion, prejudice).
  • Alibi Shifts Burden of Production Only: Denial and alibi are weak unless corroborated to show physical impossibility of presence; credible alibi shifts only the practical duty of rebuttal to the State.
Equipoise Doctrine & Effect of RTC Conviction Pending AppealCuico v. People • Leviste v. CA
GENERAL RULE 1. Equipoise Rule: When inculpatory and exculpatory evidence are evenly balanced (or support two reasonable explanations), the balance tilts to acquittal (quality & reliability govern, not witness count).
2. Merits Pending Appeal: Presumption of innocence continues until finality; appellate court independently reviews proof beyond reasonable doubt (Cuico v. People).
EXCEPTION(S) / QUALIFICATION Bail Pending Appeal: RTC conviction ends bail as a matter of right; post-conviction bail becomes discretionary subject to Rule 114, §5 bail-negating circumstances (Leviste v. CA).

2. Positive Identification, Corpus Delicti & Notice of the Charge

Two-Fold Burden: Corpus Delicti vs. Perpetrator IdentityRule 118 §2 • Trial in Absentia
GENERAL RULE State must prove both: (1) Corpus delicti (crime actually occurred—not necessarily a corpse) and (2) perpetrator identity. At pre-trial, admitting the accused is "the person named and arraigned" admits procedural identity only, not guilt; factual stipulations require writing and signatures of accused + counsel (Rule 118 §2).
EXCEPTION(S) / QUALIFICATION Absence of In-Court Pointing: If accused jumps bail after valid arraignment and trial proceeds in absentia, lack of physical pointing does not automatically acquit; identity may be proved via photographs, prior identification, and testimony.
Out-of-Court ID (O–A–A–C–E–S) & Drug Chain of CustodyCariño v. People • R.A. 9165 §21 / 10640
GENERAL RULE 1. Out-of-Court ID (Show-up, Mug Shots, Line-up): Evaluated via Totality of Circumstances Test (Cariño v. People): (1) Opportunity to view, (2) Attention, (3) Accuracy of prior description, (4) Certainty (not conclusive alone), (5) Elapsed time, (6) Suggestiveness.
2. Dangerous Drugs Chain (Sec. 21): Must establish all 4 custodial links (marking, inventory/photo, turnover, court exhibit). Amended law requires 2 insulating witnesses: elected public official + NPS or media rep (with accused/rep). Exhibit must make physical sense (no "evaporated shabu").
EXCEPTION(S) / SAVING CLAUSE Noncompliance with Sec. 21 is excused only if the State proves both: (1) justifiable grounds for noncompliance (bare "witnesses unavailable" fails) AND (2) integrity and evidentiary value of the seized items were properly preserved.
Notice of the Charge & Public TrialRule 115 §1(b), (h) • Rule 110 §§8–11
GENERAL RULE Information must allege facts in ordinary, concise language (legal labels alone are insufficient) and be read/explained at arraignment in a language or dialect known to the accused (plus disability interpreter if needed). Trials are public by default.
EXCEPTION(S) / QUALIFICATION Family Courts (Minor Victims): Confidentiality and identity protection apply to shield minors; however, not every Family Court hearing is automatically closed without a court-specific legal basis.

3. Competent & Independent Counsel + Inquest Timelines

Right to Counsel: Custody vs. TrialArt. III §12 • Rule 115 §1(c)
GENERAL RULE Under Art. III §12, a suspect under custodial investigation has the right to remain silent and to competent and independent counsel, preferably of own choice (active protection, not a phone-scrolling bystander). Uncounseled custodial confessions are inadmissible. At trial, counsel has an active duty to defend (refusing to defend warrants suspension).
EXCEPTION(S) / WHEN ART. III §12 COUNSEL IS NOT REQUIRED
  • Standard Police Lineup: Visual identification ordinarily needs no counsel—unless police elicit incriminating answers (disguised interrogation).
  • Spontaneous Barangay Admissions: Unsolicited admissions to a barangay chair not acting as law-enforcement interrogator fall outside custodial interrogation (still tested for voluntariness, capacity, and agency).
Warrantless Arrest, Article 125 RPC & Preliminary InvestigationArt. 125 RPC • Rule 112 • In re Senin
GENERAL RULE Persons arrested without warrant must be delivered to judicial authorities (via inquest filing) within 12 hours (light), 18 hours (correctional), or 36 hours (afflictive/capital)—weekend/holiday duty applies.
EXCEPTION(S) / WAIVER & POST-INQUEST REMEDIES
  • Pre-Filing Waiver: Arrestee may request regular PI by signing an Art. 125 waiver in counsel's presence; PI must finish within 15 days from inception (cannot detain indefinitely — In re Senin), and bail may still be sought.
  • Post-Filing PI Request: If Information was filed after inquest without PI, accused may request PI within 5 days of learning of the filing.

Postponements & Speedy Trial DismissalArt. III §§14(2), 16 • Rule 115 §1(h)
GENERAL RULE Motions for postponement are prohibited under Continuous Trial Guidelines (judge's illness, extra dockets, absent staff, or late transcripts do not excuse overdue decisions; court must request extension). Speedy trial formula: Delay + Reason + Assertion + Prejudice (must be vexatious, capricious, or oppressive).
EXCEPTION(S) / EFFECT OF DISMISSAL
  • Permitted Postponements: Allowed only for (1) Acts of God, (2) Force Majeure, or (3) Physical inability of witness to appear and testify (subject to postponement fees).
  • Dismissal as Acquittal: Dismissal for speedy-trial violation bars reprosecution (double jeopardy) only if delay is State-chargeable, unjustified, and first jeopardy had attached.
Privilege Against Self-IncriminationArt. III §17 • Rule 115 §1(d)–(e) • Beltran / Olvis
GENERAL RULE Nemo tenetur seipsum accusare: Protects natural persons against compelled testimonial or communicative self-incrimination (e.g., answering "Did you do it?", confession re-enactment, compelled handwriting specimen under Beltran v. Samson, or uncounseled arrest signatures on evidence envelopes per People v. Olvis). Accused cannot be forced to take the stand; ordinary witness must appear and claim privilege per specific question.
EXCEPTION(S) / NON-TESTIMONIAL & WAIVER
  • Physical / Mechanical Acts: Authorized blood/DNA samples or trying on clothes/shoes are not testimonial (other privacy/search rules apply).
  • Corporate Records: Privilege does not allow corporations to withhold business records.
  • Voluntary Testimony (§1[d]): Accused who testifies is subject to cross-examination on matters covered by direct.
  • Statutory Immunity: Transactional (bars prosecution for transaction) vs. Use & derivative-use (bars using testimony/fruits; independent proof allowed).
Trial Presence, Confrontation, Compulsory Process & AppealArt. III §14(2) • Rule 115 §1(f), (g), (i)
GENERAL RULE 1. Presence: Accused has the right to be present at trial.
2. Confrontation (§1[f]): Adverse witnesses must be cross-examined; mere death of an affiant does not admit their affidavit.
3. Compulsory Process (§1[g]) & Appeal (§1[i]): Defense may seek subpoena ad testificandum / duces tecum (no fishing expeditions). Appeal is purely statutory.
EXCEPTION(S)
  • Trial in absentia: Allowed if (1) valid arraignment, (2) due notice, and (3) unjustified absence. Waives personal attendance only (right to counsel & State burden remain).
  • Former Testimony: Admissible if witness is dead/unavailable from another proceeding with same parties & subject matter where adverse party had opportunity to cross-examine.

5. Arraignment, Remedies, Plea Bargaining & Guilty Pleas (Rule 116)

Mandatory Arraignment, Personal Plea & Counsel DutyRule 116 §§1(a)–(b), 6–8
GENERAL RULE Arraignment cannot be skipped: court furnishes copy, reads/explains in known language/dialect, and asks for plea. Accused must personally plead (counsel cannot enter plea; courtroom or authorized videoconference). If accused disappears before arraignment, trial in absentia cannot start (case archived; warrant/bail forfeiture safeguards). Court must inform of right to counsel and appoint counsel de oficio with reasonable time to confer.
EXCEPTION(S) / QUALIFICATION
  • Multi-Count Waiver (e.g., B.P. 22): Reading of Informations may be waived under Continuous Trial Guidelines if accused understands consequences, expressly consents with counsel, and waiver is documented after judicial exam (counsel alone cannot waive).
  • Private Counsel Appearing: Repeated judicial inquiries on obtaining counsel are unnecessary if counsel de parte already formally appears.
  • Flight After Arraignment: If accused absconds after valid arraignment + notice, trial in absentia proceeds.

Suspension of Arraignment (3 Exclusive Grounds)Rule 116 §11
GENERAL RULE Arraignment proceeds strictly within the Continuous Trial schedule (10 days detained / 30 days non-detained).
EXCEPTIONS — 3 EXCLUSIVE GROUNDS FOR SUSPENSION
  1. Unsound mental condition preventing understanding/intelligent plea (court orders mental exam/confinement).
  2. Prejudicial question (previously instituted civil action determinative of criminal case — Rule 111 §§6–7).
  3. Pending Petition for Review with DOJ or Office of the President (Trap Fix: not Regional State Prosecutor!) → capped at 60 days from filing with reviewing office.
Court-Entered "Not Guilty" & Double Jeopardy AttachmentRule 116 §1(b)–(d) • Rule 119 §11(e)
GENERAL RULE An accused's plea determines the case route. First jeopardy requires valid Information + competent court + valid arraignment & plea + termination without consent.
EXCEPTION(S) / MANDATORY NOT-GUILTY TRIGGERS
  • 3 Triggers Where Court Enters "Not Guilty": (1) Refuses to plead / silent (§1[c]); (2) Conditional plea ("only if probation"); (3) Exculpatory plea ("guilty, but self-defense" — §1[d] withdraws guilty plea & may trigger reverse/modified order of trial under Rule 119 §11[e]).
  • Recording Defect ≠ Void (§1[b]): Failure to enter arraignment/plea of record does not affect validity of proceedings.
Plea Bargaining to a Lesser OffenseRule 116 §§1(f), 2 • Sayre v. Xenos
GENERAL RULE At arraignment (or after arraignment before trial upon withdrawing prior not-guilty plea), accused may plead guilty to a lesser offense necessarily included in the charge (e.g., Murder → Homicide; Robbery → Theft; NOT Homicide → Physical Injuries or Rape → Unjust Vexation) with consent of trial prosecutor + offended party AND court discretion (Sayre v. Xenos). No amended Information needed; distinct from Art. 13(7) RPC mitigating plea.
EXCEPTION(S) Absent Offended Party (§1[f]): If private offended party fails to appear despite due notice, court may allow plea to necessarily included lesser offense with trial prosecutor's conformity alone (court approval still discretionary).
Guilty Plea: Non-Capital vs. Capital Offenses & Improvident PleasRule 116 §§3–5 • People v. Pagal
GENERAL RULE (NON-CAPITAL — SEC. 4) On a plea of guilty to a non-capital offense, reception of evidence to determine the penalty is discretionary upon the court (court must still confirm validity and civil liability).
EXCEPTION(S) / MANDATORY SAFEGUARDS (CAPITAL — SEC. 3 & IMPROVIDENT — SEC. 5)
  • Capital Offense (Sec. 3 — 3 Mandatory Duties): Even with R.A. 9346 abolishing death penalty (substituting reclusion perpetua/life), the court MUST: (1) conduct a searching inquiry (personal, substantive, role-specific); (2) require prosecution evidence proving guilt & precise culpability (People v. Pagal — State failure = acquittal); and (3) ask if accused wants to present evidence.
  • Improvident Plea (Sec. 5): Guilty plea entered without informed, voluntary understanding may be withdrawn and replaced by not guilty before judgment becomes final.

30-Second Rapid Recall Drills:

  1. Pedro is convicted of Homicide by the RTC and appeals. Does he still enjoy the presumption of innocence? Can he claim bail as a matter of right?
  2. Charged with Murder, Juan pleads "Guilty, because I acted in self-defense." Should the judge immediately conduct a Searching Inquiry under Sec. 3?
  3. Charged with Murder, Maria enters an unequivocal guilty plea, and the prosecutor presents zero evidence assuming the plea suffices. What is the verdict?
  4. During a drug buy-bust inventory, only an elected barangay official was present; the prosecutor says "media and NPS were unavailable" with no proof of effort. Does the saving clause apply?

Answers:
1. Yes on merits review (Cuico — continues until finality); No on bail as of right (Leviste — becomes discretionary under Rule 114 §5).
2. No. Self-defense is an exculpatory claim; court must withdraw the plea, enter Not Guilty (Sec. 1[d]), and may apply modified/reverse order of trial (Rule 119 §11[e]).
3. Acquittal (People v. Pagal — Sec. 3 requires mandatory prosecution proof of guilt & degree of culpability despite a guilty plea).
4. No. Saving clause requires both justifiable grounds (bare "unavailable" fails) and preserved integrity/evidentiary value.
