1. The four articles to recall first

Article Memory cue Rule
2176 AFDC Act or omission + fault or negligence + damage + proximate cause. A juridical person acts through its officers or agents and may also answer as an employer.
2180 Select and supervise Culpa in eligendo and culpa in vigilando. Once the requisites are shown, negligence is presumed. Prove the diligence of a good father of a family to prevent damage. Liability is primary and direct, and solidary where Art. 2194 applies. Under strict respondeat superior, careful selection and supervision do not excuse the employer.
2194 + 1217 Whole outside, shares inside The victim may collect the whole from any one, some, or all solidary tortfeasors. Full payment extinguishes the obligation to the victim. The payer may recover each co-debtor’s corresponding share, with interest on the payment made.
2181 Recover what was paid One who pays for damage caused by a dependent or employee may recover from that person what was paid or delivered in satisfaction of the claim.
C
Concerted Cooperation
Joint enterprise or common concerted scheme
S
Single Causal Set
Concurrent contributions producing unified harm
I
Independent Acts
Separate proximate causes of one indivisible injury

Three paths to joint tortfeasorship: C–S–I. Concerted cooperation, contributions to a single causal set, or independent acts that concur as proximate causes of one indivisible injury. Conspiracy is unnecessary. [Supreme Court E-Library]

2. Parents and guardians · Age, Company, Authority

🎂
Age Thresholds
15, 18, 21 Rules (RA 9344 & RA 6809)
🏡
Living in Company
Actual physical cohabitation & custody
⚖️
Parental Authority
Joint legal power of governance & discipline
  • Parents — Arts. 211 and 221, Family Code. Both parents jointly exercise parental authority. For a child living in their company and under their parental authority, liability may be primary, direct, and solidary for negligent or intentional acts. The defense is diligence to prevent the damage. Paying tuition and providing support do not establish preventive supervision. [Supreme Court E-Library]
  • Guardians — Art. 2180(3). The minor or incapacitated ward must be under their authority and living in their company. In default of parents or a judicial guardian, remember G–S–C under Art. 216: surviving grandparent → oldest qualified sibling over 21 → qualified actual custodian over 21. [Supreme Court E-Library]
  • 15–18–21. Under R.A. 9344, §6, 15 or under is exempt from criminal liability. Over 15 but below 18 is exempt if acting without discernment. Criminal exemption leaves civil liability enforceable under existing law, including RPC Arts. 100–101 as applicable. 18 is majority. R.A. 6809, §3 preserves Art. 2180(2)–(3) responsibility for children and wards below 21. [lawphil.net]
Case trigger Recall
Pending adoption Tamargo v. CA, G.R. No. 85044, June 3, 1992. Ask who had actual custody when the tort occurred. Retroactive adoption did not shift liability to adopters who lacked custody then. [lawphil.net]
Gun locked, key accessible Libi v. IAC, G.R. No. 70890, September 18, 1992. Parents failed to prove preventive diligence. A third-party gun owner may separately answer under Arts. 2176 and 2194 if negligent storage was a concurrent proximate cause. [lawphil.net]

3. Schools · Who caused the injury?

Actor Governing rule
Unemancipated minor Family Code Arts. 218–219. School, administrators and teachers, or the person or entity engaged in child care, have special parental authority during supervision, instruction or custody in all authorized activities, on or off premises. For damage caused by the minor, they are principally and solidarily liable. Parents, judicial guardians or substitute parents are subsidiarily liable. Defense for each is proper diligence required under the particular circumstances. [Supreme Court E-Library]
Pupil, student or apprentice in custody, including an adult Art. 2180(7). Academic school → teacher-in-charge. Arts and trades → head of the establishment. Custody is protective and supervisory and can include recess or a legitimate campus purpose after classes end. The school entity may answer on a separate legal basis. [lawphil.net]
Teacher or school employee Art. 2180(4)–(5). Ask if the act was within the scope of assigned tasks. For the teacher’s own negligence, test Art. 2176. An injury suffered by a student does not by itself establish Art. 219 liability. [Supreme Court E-Library]
Outsider Culpa contractual, Arts. 1170 and 1173. Enrollment includes an implied duty to take reasonable measures for campus safety. Establish breach and causation. The outsider’s act falls outside Art. 2180(7). [lawphil.net]

Four case triggers worth memorizing

  • Physics report — Amadora v. CA, G.R. No. L-47745, April 15, 1988. Custody can continue after classes end. The teacher-in-charge of the offending student was not established. [lawphil.net]
  • Private picnic — St. Francis High School v. CA, G.R. No. 82465, February 25, 1991. The principal’s awareness did not amount to authorization. The Supreme Court also absolved the petitioning teachers, who proved diligence. [lawphil.net]
  • Steering defect — St. Mary’s Academy v. Carpitanos, G.R. No. 143363, February 6, 2002. Authorization alone does not establish proximate cause. The Court excluded the academy and remanded to determine the remaining defendants’ liability. [Supreme Court E-Library]
  • Outsider stabbing — PSBA v. CA, G.R. No. 84698, February 4, 1992. The Court allowed the contractual claim to proceed to trial. It did not adjudge the school liable for damages. A consent form likewise does not by itself establish diligence. Art. 1171 expressly addresses future fraud. [lawphil.net]

4. Employers and vehicles · Task, Duty, Passenger

Basis What to remember
Quasi-delict · Art. 2180(4)–(5) Employment + employee’s quasi-delict + assigned task. Par. 4 covers owners and managers of an enterprise and acts in the service of the branch or on the occasion of functions. A mere managerial employee does not become the employer. Par. 5 includes household helpers, even without a business. Defense requires proof of both selection and supervision. Think verified qualifications and testing before hiring, then actual training, monitoring and enforcement after hiring. [Supreme Court E-Library]
Ex delicto · RPC Art. 103 Employment + industry + final conviction for a felony in discharge of duties + insolvency. Liability is subsidiary. After unsuccessful execution against the employee, move for subsidiary execution in the same criminal case, with an opportunity for the employer to be heard. Diligence in hiring or supervision is unavailable as a defense. [lawphil.net]
Contract of carriage · Art. 1759 Carrier answers for passenger injury or death through employee negligence or willful acts, even beyond authority or against orders. Careful hiring and supervision do not excuse it. Art. 1763 covers strangers and fellow passengers if carrier employees could have prevented or stopped the act through the required diligence. [Supreme Court E-Library]
  • Company car → ask whose business the trip served. Castilex Industrial Corp. v. Vasquez, Jr., G.R. No. 132266, December 21, 1999. Ordinary commutes, meals and personal errands generally fall outside assigned tasks absent a special business benefit. Valenzuela v. CA, G.R. No. 115024, February 7, 1996, illustrates company-car use serving the employer’s business and inadequate proof of supervision. [Supreme Court E-Library]
  • Art. 2184 → In? Could prevent? An owner in the vehicle is solidarily liable with the negligent driver if due diligence could have prevented the mishap. Think sustained speeding versus a sudden swerve with no reasonable chance to intervene. Owner absent → Art. 2180. Two findings of guilt in the preceding two months for reckless driving or traffic violations create a disputable presumption of driver negligence. [Supreme Court E-Library]
  • Registered owner → check the LTO record. An open deed of sale or unregistered lease does not defeat an injured third party’s claim. Where the registered-owner rule and Art. 2180 both apply, Caravan treats registration as raising a disputable presumption and shifts the evidentiary burden to the registered owner. Keep the victim’s claim separate from any recourse against the buyer, lessee or driver. Kabit is void as against public policy. The CPC holder cannot hide behind the arrangement, and parties in pari delicto cannot enforce it between themselves. [lawphil.net]

5. Special liabilities · One-line recall

Topic Memory rule
Innkeepers · RPC Art. 102 Regulation, or Notice + Directions. Subsidiary liability for a crime in the establishment when the keeper or employee violated an ordinance or police regulation. For theft or robbery of a lodging guest’s goods, require prior notice of deposit + compliance with directions. Violent or intimidating robbery is excluded unless committed by the keeper’s employees. [lawphil.net]
Partnership · Arts. 1822–1824 Ordinary course or authority → solidary. Partnership and all partners answer for a partner’s wrongful act within ordinary business or with co-partners’ authority, and for covered misapplication of third-party money or property. Mutual agency governs. [Supreme Court E-Library]
Spouses · Which property pays? Exclusive property first. ACP, Art. 94(9) → if insufficient, community advances payment, deducted at liquidation. CPG, Arts. 121–122 → same shortfall rule, after Art. 121 charges are covered. Complete separation → debtor-spouse’s separate property. [Supreme Court E-Library]
State · Consent and commission Const., Art. XVI, §3 → consent to suit is distinct from liability. Art. 2180(6) → State answers for a special agent with a definite commission foreign to regular duties. For the official whose regular task it was, test personal liability under Art. 2176. [Supreme Court E-Library]
LGU · Control or supervision Art. 2189 → provinces, cities and municipalities answer for death or injury caused by defective public works under their control or supervision. National ownership does not decide the issue. Recall Guilatco’s open manhole and San Juan’s inadequate gasera. Also check the LGU’s proprietary capacity and LGC §24. [lawphil.net]
Public officers · Personal capacity Actionable ultra vires conduct, bad faith, malice or gross negligence can support personal liability. Arts. 19–21 and 32 have distinct requisites. Varela v. Revalez, G.R. No. 171705, July 29, 2010. Cadiz v. Presiding Judge, G.R. No. 178941, April 27, 2011, protects honest errors in the exercise of quasi-judicial functions. [lawphil.net]

30-second recall. A 20-year-old lives with his parents and injures a neighbor. Which law preserves possible parental liability? An adult student injures a classmate during recess. Who had supervisory custody? A delivery driver is convicted and insolvent. Which article governs subsidiary execution?

Answers: R.A. 6809, §3 with Art. 2180(2) → teacher-in-charge in an academic school under Art. 2180(7), subject to diligence → RPC Art. 103, if its other requisites are met.
